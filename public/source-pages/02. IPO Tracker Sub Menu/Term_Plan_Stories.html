03
❌ Claim Rejected
🏥
Dinesh Patel, 41
Factory Supervisor · Surat · ₹9L/year
📍 Surat, Gujarat
📋

"Dinesh had bought a ₹50L term plan. He died of liver failure in 2023. His family filed the claim. Two months later — REJECTED. The insurer said he had hidden his diabetes diagnosis during application."

✕
In 2019, Dinesh was told not to disclose diabetes by the agent — "it'll increase your premium, just leave it blank."
✕
Insurer's investigation team found hospital records from 2017 showing diabetes diagnosis. Claim repudiated under Section 45 for material misrepresentation.
✕
Family received zero. 4 years of premiums forfeited. His wife went to consumer court — case ongoing.
🚫
Non-disclosure = Zero payout
The agent saved ₹2,000/year in premium by hiding diabetes. The family lost ₹50L. Full disclosure would have cost ₹3,000 more annually — and guaranteed the claim.

Construction Contractor · Hyderabad · ₹11L/year

Irfan HAD done the right thing. One missed payment erased it all. Auto-debit setup — a 5-minute task — would have saved this family.

06
❌ Group Cover Only
💻
Sneha Krishnamurthy, 31
Product Manager · Bengaluru Startup · ₹24L/year
📍 Bengaluru, Karnataka
📉

"Sneha's startup laid off 60% staff in Feb 2024. She was among them. 3 months later, she was in a severe road accident while on a rental scooter. She didn't survive. Her parents were dependents."

✕
Sneha had relied on employer group term cover of ₹48L. That cover ended the day she was laid off. She had no individual policy.
✕
At the time of the accident — 3 months after layoff — she had ZERO life insurance coverage. She was between jobs, "planning to buy after joining new company."
✕
Her aging parents (father had diabetes, mother with knee surgery) had no other income. Relatives had to pool money for their support.
🏢
Group cover ends with your job
Employer insurance is a benefit, not a plan. It is non-portable and ends the moment you resign or get laid off. Every individual needs personal term insurance regardless of employer cover.

🧠
Kapil Mehta, 37
Finance Analyst · Mumbai · ₹22L/year
📍 Mumbai, Maharashtra
🧠

"Kapil had a massive stroke in March 2024. He survived — but with partial speech loss and right arm weakness. He was 37. He's alive, but can no longer work in his finance role. His family would have collapsed — except for one decision at 30."

✓
At 30, Kapil had added a ₹30L Critical Illness rider to his ₹1Cr term plan — "on Ditto's recommendation." CI rider triggered on stroke diagnosis.
✓
₹30L paid within 21 days of claim. Used for: rehab therapy (₹8L), home loan EMI buffer (18 months), income replacement while relearning.
✓
Waiver of Premium rider kicked in — no more premiums payable. ₹1Cr base cover still intact for his family. Life cover continues for free.

Alive but disabled — CI rider matters
Death is not the only risk. Disability and critical illness can be worse — you're still alive but can't earn. CI rider + WOP rider = complete protection. Kapil's story proves it.
