Broker Types

Discount vs Full-Service Brokers

Understanding the difference is the first step to choosing the right broker for your investing style.

Discount ⚡
Discount Brokers

Technology-first platforms with ultra-low flat-fee brokerage. No frills, no advisors — pure execution at ₹20 or less per order. Best for self-directed, research-driven investors.

✅ Pros
Flat ₹0–₹20 per order
Zero delivery brokerage
Advanced trading platforms
Fast digital onboarding
❌ Cons
No personalised advisory
Limited research reports
Self-service only
Limited branch network
Full-Service 🏢
Full-Service Brokers

Traditional bank-affiliated or legacy brokerages offering research, advisory, wealth management, and relationship management. Higher fees for holistic financial support.

✅ Pros
Research & advisory included
Relationship manager access
Wider branch network
PMS & HNI services
❌ Cons
Higher brokerage %
Higher AMC charges
Account opening fees
Less transparent pricing

Investor Safety

SEBI Regulations & Your Protection

Every broker reviewed is SEBI-registered. India has robust investor protection mechanisms in place.

🛡️
SEBI Oversight
All brokers are regulated by the Securities and Exchange Board of India. SEBI sets strict norms on client fund segregation and reporting.
💰
Investor Protection Fund
NSE and BSE maintain IPF (Investor Protection Fund) that covers up to ₹25 lakh per investor in case of broker default.
🔒
Pledge-Based Margin
SEBI mandates pledge-based margin system since 2020. Brokers cannot use client securities as collateral without explicit consent.
📊
Daily Ledger Reports
SEBI mandates brokers to send daily fund and holdings reports. Review these regularly to spot unauthorized transactions.
🏦
Segregated Client Accounts
Broker's own funds must be completely separate from client funds in designated client bank accounts per SEBI rules.
⚠️
How to File a Complaint
Complaints can be filed on SCORES (SEBI Complaint Redress System) at scores.gov.in. Brokers must resolve within 30 days.
Investor Education

Master the Market Language

12 essential terms every investor must understand before opening a broking account in India — decoded simply.

💰 Cost
💸
Brokerage
Fee charged per trade. Discount brokers cap at ₹20 flat or zero; full-service brokers charge 0.02%–0.55% of trade value.
📁 Account
🗂️
Demat Account
Dematerialized account that holds your shares electronically via CDSL or NSDL. Mandatory to trade in Indian stock markets.
📁 Account
🏦
AMC — Annual Maintenance Charge
Yearly fee for maintaining your Demat account. Ranges from ₹0 (Groww, FYERS, mStock lifetime) to ₹750/yr (HDFC / Axis from 2nd yr).
💰 Cost
🔄
DP Charge
Depository Participant charge on every delivery sell transaction. Ranges ₹13.50–₹35 + GST. Applies to all brokers — unavoidable.
📊 Segment
📊
F&O — Futures & Options
Derivative contracts that derive value from an underlying stock or index. High leverage, high risk. SEBI mandates income & trading experience proof.
🏛️ Tax
🏛️
STT — Securities Transaction Tax
Government tax on equity trades: 0.1% on delivery, 0.025% on intraday sell side, 0.02% on F&O options exercise. Cannot be avoided.
🛡️ Regulatory
🛡️
SEBI
Securities and Exchange Board of India — the apex capital market regulator. All brokers, exchanges, MFs, and FIIs are regulated by SEBI.
📁 Account
🔗
3-in-1 Account
Integrated savings bank + Demat + trading account in one. Offered by bank-backed brokers: ICICI Direct, HDFC Securities, Kotak, Axis, SBI.
⚡ Trading
⚡
Intraday Trading
Buying and selling shares within the same trading day (9:15AM–3:30PM IST). Open positions auto square-off at 3:20PM by the broker.
💼 Service
💼
PMS — Portfolio Management Service
Discretionary management of your equity portfolio by professional fund managers. SEBI mandates minimum ₹50 lakh investment. Offered by Motilal Oswal, Nuvama, etc.
🛡️ Regulatory
📋
IPO via ASBA
Application Supported by Blocked Amount — funds are blocked in your bank account (not debited) until IPO allotment. Mandatory method in India since 2016.
🛡️ Regulatory
🔒
Margin Pledge
Since Sep 2020 SEBI mandates you must pledge shares via CDSL/NSDL OTP to use holdings as trading margin. Broker cannot pledge without your consent.

13What happens if a stock broker goes bust?▾
⚠️ Broker Default — What Happens
Holdings already in demat: Safe — you can transfer your account to another broker with holdings intact (T+2 settlement = shares already credited)
Pending settlement (within T+2): Shares still in pipeline may be lost in rare cases
Pledged shares / margin: Shares used as leverage collateral may be at risk
Idle wallet balance: Uninvested cash left in broker's wallet can be lost — always withdraw excess funds
IPF protection: NSE/BSE Investor Protection Fund covers up to ₹25 lakh per investor in case of default
Best practice: Never keep idle funds in a broker wallet. Withdraw to your bank account regularly and keep only trading capital deployed.
14Can stock brokers be trusted? Is my money safe?▾
🛡️ Safety Framework
SEBI oversight: All listed brokers are SEBI-registered and must follow strict fund segregation, margin pledge, and reporting norms

Caveat: Idle wallet cash is not protected the same way shares are. Keep minimal cash with the broker
Trusted brokers: verify SEBI registration at sebi.gov.in and cross-check NSE/BSE member lists before depositing funds.
