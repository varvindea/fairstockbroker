Dollar Index (DXY) — The Force That Controls FII Flows Into India
The US Dollar Index (DXY) measures the US dollar's strength against a basket of 6 major currencies (Euro, Yen, Pound, Canadian Dollar, Swedish Krona, Swiss Franc). When DXY rises, the dollar is strengthening globally — causing global fund managers to repatriate money to dollar-denominated assets. For India, a rising DXY almost always means FII outflows, Rupee pressure, and Nifty weakness. The DXY is the single most important global currency signal for Indian markets.

04 — Practical Application

How to Use Forex Knowledge in Your Portfolio

Currency signals are free, real-time, and publicly available. Here's how to systematically use INR movements, DXY trends, and reserves data to position your portfolio:

📡
Daily Monitoring
Track Three Numbers Every Morning
Check these three before market open: (1) INR/USD rate — is Rupee above or below ₹84? (2) DXY — is it above or below 103? (3) FII daily buy/sell (on NSE website). These three numbers tell you if the day is likely to be risk-on (weak DXY + FII buying) or risk-off (strong DXY + FII selling).

IT Strategy
Buy IT on Sustained Rupee Weakness
When the Rupee is sustainably weak (₹84+) for 2+ months, increase IT allocation. Every ₹1 of Rupee fall adds EPS automatically — no business improvement needed. This is a free margin expansion. Conversely, reduce IT exposure when Rupee strengthens sharply (below ₹82) as the currency headwind hits margins.
✈️
Cyclical Strategy
Buy Aviation + Paints When INR Strengthens
When the Rupee appreciates (fed cuts, FII inflows, oil falls), aviation and paint companies get a double benefit — lower import costs AND improved consumer sentiment. IndiGo and Asian Paints both dramatically outperform in strong-Rupee environments. This is a reliable sector rotation trade.
🇺🇸
DXY Watch
DXY Above 105 = Reduce India Risk
DXY above 105 historically signals FII outflows from EMs including India. When DXY crosses 105 on an upward trend, reduce high-beta positions (banking, real estate, midcap) and increase defensives (IT, pharma, FMCG). When DXY falls below 100, the reverse — increase cyclicals as FII money returns.
🏦
Reserves Check
Weekly Reserves Release — Friday Ritual
RBI releases weekly forex reserves data every Friday. Rising reserves = structural positive for India (more currency defence capacity). Falling reserves at high rate (>$5Bn/week) = watch for RBI rate action or emergency measures. India's reserves at $620Bn make it structurally one of the most resilient EMs — a consistent long-term positive.

FII Flow Trick · The INR-FII-Nifty Triangle
Three variables that move together — understand the loop
"FII buys → rupee strengthens → Nifty rises · FII sells → rupee weakens → Nifty falls"

📊DII cushion: Domestic institutions (LIC, MFs via SIP) now absorb FII selling. SIP inflows ₹20,000+ crore/month (2024) = natural market stabiliser.

"INR weak + DXY high + Reserves falling = maximum stress · INR stable + DXY low + Reserves high = ideal"
🟢IDEAL (INR stable, DXY low, Reserves high): FII inflows → Banks rally → Rate-sensitives outperform → Mid-caps surge. Add cyclical exposure.
🟡MIXED (INR weakening, DXY rising, Reserves stable): Rotate to IT + Pharma (natural hedge). Reduce banks and autos. Monitor FII daily flows.
🔴STRESS (INR falling fast, DXY 110+, Reserves declining): Maximum IT/Pharma weight. Reduce cyclicals. Add gold as portfolio hedge. Wait for DXY peak before re-entering rate-sensitives.
💡 The 3-variable morning scan: (1) INR/USD — check on MoneyControl. (2) DXY — check on tradingeconomics.com. (3) Weekly forex reserves change — RBI website every Friday. These 3 numbers give you the complete currency picture in under 2 minutes before India markets open.

📖 Glossary

Key Terms Glossary

Every key currency/forex term — INR/USD, DXY, forex reserves, import cover, REER, NDF, hedging, carry trade — defined with India-specific market impact and quantified examples.

🔍
INR/USD Exchange Rate
INR & Forex
India's exchange rate — quoted as INR per 1 USD. India operates a "managed float" — RBI intervenes to prevent excessive volatility but allows market-determined long-term trends. INR at ₹83–85 (2023–2024) represents a structurally weaker rupee vs the ₹65–70 range of 2017–2020. Structural drivers of INR weakness: India's persistent trade deficit (imports > exports), oil import dependence, FII flow volatility, and US-India interest rate differential.
💡 INR depreciation pace: INR has depreciated ~3–4% annually on average over the last 20 years vs USD (from ₹45 in 2003 to ₹84 in 2024). This long-term structural trend means Indian equity investors who buy exporters get a natural FX tailwind — sector returns + currency appreciation (for USD-based investors) = double return.
REER (Real Effective Exchange Rate)
INR & Forex
The trade-weighted, inflation-adjusted value of the rupee against a basket of India's trading partner currencies. Published by RBI monthly. REER above 100 = rupee overvalued relative to fundamentals (makes Indian exports expensive → competitiveness concern). REER below 100 = undervalued (exports become competitive, FDI more attractive). REER is more accurate than the bilateral INR/USD rate for assessing India's export competitiveness.
💡 REER practical use: India's REER was ~106–108 in 2023–24 (slightly overvalued) — suggesting Indian exports are marginally expensive vs key competitors. When REER rises above 110, export sectors (IT services, textiles, gems) face competitiveness headwinds — even if the INR hasn't moved much against USD, it may have appreciated vs EUR, GBP, or CNY, hurting those export markets.
India Forex Reserves
INR & Forex
India's total foreign currency assets, gold, SDRs, and IMF reserve tranche. Managed by RBI. Components: Foreign Currency Assets (largest, ~$600B+), Gold (~$55B), SDRs (~$18B). Released weekly by RBI every Friday at ~5:30 PM. Import cover: Reserves ÷ Monthly imports = months of imports that can be funded without any new inflow. India import cover: 10–11 months (2024) vs 7 months (2013). Higher reserves = stronger currency defence = lower contagion risk = more FII confidence in India.
💡 $700B milestone (2024): India's forex reserves peaked above $700 billion in September 2024 — highest ever. This buffer means RBI can intervene to sell $10–15B per week for months without depleting reserves. For investors: high reserves = lower rupee crisis risk = narrower risk premium on Indian assets = lower equity risk premium = higher P/E justified.
DXY (Dollar Index)
DXY & USD
The US Dollar Index tracking USD value against a basket of 6 currencies (Euro 57.6%, Yen 13.6%, Pound 11.9%, CAD 9.1%, SEK 4.2%, CHF 3.6%). DXY is the primary external driver of India FII flows and Nifty direction. DXY rising = USD strengthening = FII prefer US assets = EM (including India) sell-off. DXY falling = USD weakening = EM attractive = FII buy India. Critical levels: Below 100 = strong EM tailwind. 100–105 = neutral. Above 105 = headwind. DXY 115 in 2022 = worst FII outflow year (₹1.6 lakh crore) in India's history.
💡 DXY-Nifty correlation: When DXY fell from 115 (Oct 2022) to 100 (Dec 2023), Nifty rose from 17,500 to 21,500 (23% return in 14 months) — driven primarily by FII return. This correlation is one of the most reliable India-US macro relationships. Check DXY on your morning routine to predict FII flow direction for the day.
NDF (Non-Deliverable Forward)
DXY & USD
An offshore INR forward market where global institutions trade Indian rupee without physical delivery — primarily in Singapore and London. NDF positions can sometimes diverge from onshore INR rates, creating pressure. When global institutions take large NDF short-INR positions (betting rupee will weaken), it creates downward pressure on the rupee even before the onshore market opens. RBI monitors NDF markets. Periods of sustained NDF weakness vs onshore INR → RBI may intervene in both markets.
💡 NDF-onshore spread: When NDF market (Singapore) shows INR at ₹84 while onshore RBI rate is ₹83.70, the 30-paise NDF premium signals strong international selling pressure. This spread often predicts next-day's onshore rupee weakness. Professional traders monitor this spread (published on Reuters/Bloomberg) for currency direction signals.
FII/FPI Daily Flow Data
FII Flows
Foreign Institutional Investors (FIIs) / Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPIs) daily net buying or selling in Indian equity and debt markets. NSE publishes provisional FII equity data by ~6 PM every trading day. NSDL publishes comprehensive data including debt flows. Key rule: FII net buying → INR demand → rupee strengthens → Nifty positive feedback. 5+ consecutive days of strong FII buying → short-term bullish signal. 10+ consecutive days of FII selling → currency stress and market pressure.
💡 FII data access (free): NSE website → Market Data → FII/DII Data → Daily data with equity and debt figures. NSDL → Market → FPI Investment → Country-wise breakdown available. Set up daily check at 6:30 PM — 30-second review tells you whether to expect INR strength/weakness the next morning and the broader market direction.
DII as FII Counter-Balance
FII Flows
Domestic Institutional Investors (DIIs) — LIC, domestic mutual funds (Nippon, SBI, ICICI Prudential, HDFC MF, etc.) — increasingly absorb FII selling, cushioning market falls. SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) inflows: ₹7,000 crore/month (2020) → ₹20,000+ crore/month (2024) = structural increase in domestic "buy the dip" capacity. When FIIs sold ₹1.6 lakh crore in 2022, DIIs bought ₹1.9 lakh crore — Nifty fell only 17% vs a potential 30–40% fall without DII support.
💡 DII SIP math: ₹20,000 crore/month SIP inflow = ₹2.4 lakh crore annual steady buying from retail investors via SIPs. This acts as a monthly "floor" for Indian markets — any FII selling below ₹20,000 crore/month is absorbed by SIP inflows alone. This structural shift has fundamentally changed India's market dynamics — reducing crisis-level falls and accelerating recoveries.
Natural Forex Hedge (Exporters)
Sector Impact
Companies that earn significant revenue in foreign currency (USD, EUR, GBP) have a "natural hedge" against INR weakness — when INR depreciates, their reported revenue and profits in INR terms automatically increase without any business improvement. IT companies (TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCL) — 60–80% USD revenue. Pharma companies — 40–60% USD/EUR revenue. These companies partially hedge their receivables (50–80% hedge ratio) via forward contracts and options, limiting short-term windfall but protecting against INR appreciation.
💡 IT hedge ratio: TCS typically hedges 70–80% of short-term (0–12 month) receivables. This means ~20–30% of receivables are unhedged — getting full rupee benefit. On ₹1 INR fall vs USD: unhedged portion creates windfall, hedged portion is pre-locked at old rate. Management discusses hedge book in every quarterly earnings call — watch for changes in hedge ratio as a signal for currency outlook expectations.
Current Account Deficit (CAD)
General
India's current account balance = Trade Balance (goods exports − imports) + Services Balance + Primary Income + Secondary Income. India consistently runs a current account deficit (imports more than exports, including goods). Structural factors: Oil dependence (largest import), gold imports, merchandise trade deficit. India's saving grace: large services surplus (IT exports, software services) + NRI remittances (~$100B+ annually). CAD as % of GDP: Comfort zone 1–2.5%. Above 3.5% = rupee pressure and rating concern. Taper Tantrum 2013: CAD 4.8% → rupee crisis. 2024: CAD ~1.5% → manageable.
💡 CAD vs Capital Account: India's current account deficit is funded by capital account surplus (FDI + FPI inflows + ECB). When FPI outflows spike (risk-off), the capital account surplus shrinks, leaving the CAD unfunded → rupee falls. This is why India's currency is more volatile during global risk-off episodes. Monitoring both CAD and capital flows together gives the complete INR picture.
