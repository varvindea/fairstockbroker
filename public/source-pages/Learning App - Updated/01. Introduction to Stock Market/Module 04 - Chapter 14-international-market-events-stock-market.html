01 — The Foundation

Why Indian Markets Follow Global Indices So Closely

India is not an island economy. FIIs (Foreign Institutional Investors) hold 25–30% of India's equity market. When global sentiment turns negative, they sell India — regardless of India's own economic health. When global markets rally, they buy India too.

S&P 500 — The Single Most Important Global Signal for Indian Markets
The S&P 500 tracks the 500 largest publicly traded companies in the United States — representing approximately 80% of total US stock market capitalisation. It includes Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, Nvidia, Alphabet, Meta, and hundreds of industry leaders across every sector. Because the US is the world's largest economy and the S&P 500 is the global benchmark for institutional portfolios, any significant move in the S&P 500 triggers FII rebalancing across all global markets — including India. It is the most important overnight data point for any Indian investor.

⬇️ Key Bearish Event
US Fed Rate Hike — The Biggest Nifty Mover of 2022
When the US Federal Reserve hikes interest rates aggressively (as in 2022 — 4 consecutive 75bps hikes), global capital flows back to US Treasury bonds which now offer higher risk-free returns. FIIs exit EMs including India to invest in safer US bonds. Result: India sees ₹2–4 lakh crore FII outflows in a year, Rupee weakens, Nifty underperforms. The 2022 cycle caused the largest FII outflow from India in history.
⚠️ 2022 Fed hike cycle: FIIs sold ₹2.55 lakh crore from India — the largest-ever annual outflow. Nifty fell 20% from peak. Rupee hit ₹83.
⬆️ Key Bullish Event
US Fed Rate Cut Signal — Triggers EM Buying Wave
When the Fed signals rate cuts or pauses hikes, bond returns fall → global money searches for higher returns → EMs including India attract massive FII inflows. Fed's November 2023 pivot signal triggered the largest FII buying in India in a single month — ₹57,000 crore in October 2023. Nifty crossed 20,000 for the first time. Every major Indian bull run has been preceded by a global rate easing cycle.
✅ Fed pivot signal Oct–Nov 2023: FIIs bought ₹57,000Cr in India in October alone. Nifty crossed 20,000 for first time. Banking and IT led the rally.

Dow Jones Industrial Average · DJIA · 30 US Blue-Chip Stocks · Economic Health Barometer
Dow Jones — The World's Oldest Index, Built on Industrial America
The Dow Jones Industrial Average (DJIA) is the world's most famous stock market index — tracking just 30 of the most established US industrial and commercial companies including Boeing, Caterpillar, 3M, JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Walmart, McDonald's, and Disney. Unlike the S&P 500 (broad economy) or NASDAQ (tech-heavy), the Dow Jones represents traditional industrial America — manufacturing, finance, consumer goods, and services. For Indian investors, the Dow Jones signals the health of US domestic consumption and industrial production — which affects Indian exports, raw material demand, and metals sectors.

Nifty 50 — India's Own Benchmark That Reflects the Nation's Economic Health
The Nifty 50 is India's primary stock market benchmark — tracking the 50 largest and most liquid companies listed on the National Stock Exchange (NSE). It represents approximately 66% of India's total listed market capitalisation. The Nifty is the index that FIIs, domestic mutual funds, and index funds all benchmark against. Understanding its composition — heavily weighted towards banking (36%), IT (15%), and energy (12%) — explains why certain global events affect it more than others. Every Indian investor's portfolio is implicitly compared to Nifty's performance.

India-Specific Risks — What Disconnects Nifty from Its Story
Nifty underperforms when: FII outflows (DXY rises → dollar-denominated returns fall for FIIs), inflation rising (RBI can't cut rates → banking sector depressed), political uncertainty (election outcome fears), or banking stress (NPA cycle, NBFC crisis). 2018 had Nifty flat while NASDAQ +38% — banking NPA crisis kept Indian markets in a range while global indices rallied.
⚠️ 2018: NBFC crisis + banking NPA cycle kept Nifty flat while S&P +11%, NASDAQ +38%. India-specific headwinds disconnected Nifty from global bull run.

04 — Practical Strategy

How to Use Global Market Data Every Day

Global market data is free, public, and available every morning before Indian markets open. Here's a practical system to use it as a consistent market edge:

🌅
Morning Ritual
Check 5 Numbers Before 9:15 AM Every Day
Before the market opens: (1) S&P 500 overnight change, (2) NASDAQ change, (3) Dow Jones change, (4) INR/USD rate, (5) FII yesterday buy/sell. These 5 numbers — all free on any financial app — tell you the likely opening direction and which sectors will lead. Takes 3 minutes. Gives you enormous context over unprepared investors.
💻
NASDAQ Watch
NASDAQ Determines Your IT Stocks' Mood
If you hold TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCL, or any IT company — check NASDAQ every morning. NASDAQ down 2%+ = IT stocks will face selling pressure; consider if your thesis still holds. NASDAQ up 2%+ = IT stocks likely to have a good session. The 0.82 correlation means NASDAQ is your daily IT stock weather report.
🌐
Fed Watch
Mark Every FOMC Meeting on Your Calendar
The US Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meets 8 times a year. Each meeting result moves Indian markets 1–3% the next morning. Mark all 8 FOMC dates annually. Before each meeting: if rate hike expected, reduce banking, add IT/pharma. If rate cut expected, add banking, midcap, real estate. This one calendar event has been the most reliable large-move predictor for Nifty every year since 2021.
📊
FII Data
Track FII Daily Buy/Sell — NSE Publishes it Free
NSE publishes FII (Foreign Institutional Investor) daily buy/sell data by 6 PM every trading day — completely free on nseindia.com. When FIIs are net buyers for 5+ consecutive days, it's a bullish signal. When they sell 5+ days consecutively (especially above ₹5,000Cr/day), it's a bear signal. This data is the most direct indicator of Nifty direction and is ignored by most retail investors.
🇮🇳
India vs Global
When Indian Markets Decouple — Act Accordingly
Some days, Indian markets decouple from global indices — usually on Budget Day, RBI policy day, election results, or major domestic earnings announcements. These are days where knowing the global context is less important than the domestic event. Recognise these moments: if S&P is flat but Nifty is moving sharply (up or down), the story is India-specific — analyse it separately from global context.
