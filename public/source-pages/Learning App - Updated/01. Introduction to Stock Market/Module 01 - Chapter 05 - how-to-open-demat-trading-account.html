01 — Understanding the Accounts

Demat Account vs Trading Account — What's the Difference?

Most beginners think they need to open "a Demat account." In reality, you need two linked accounts — a Demat account and a Trading account. They work together but serve completely different purposes. Understanding this difference prevents costly confusion on Day 1.

🏛
Account 01
Demat Account
Dematerialised Account — your electronic share locker

A Demat (Dematerialised) account holds your shares in electronic form — just like a bank account holds your money. Every share you buy gets credited here. Every share you sell gets debited from here. It is maintained by a Depository Participant (your broker) and ultimately recorded with CDSL or NSDL (the two national depositories).

  • What it storesEquity shares, ETF units, bonds, mutual fund units (in statement of account form), sovereign gold bonds, and rights/bonus shares
  • Who maintains itYour broker acts as a Depository Participant (DP) — an agent of CDSL or NSDL. The actual record is with the depository — not your broker
  • Your safety guaranteeEven if your broker shuts down or goes bankrupt, your shares are safe — they are recorded in your name in CDSL/NSDL's records independently
  • Linked toYour Trading Account (for buying/selling) and your Bank Account (for dividend credits and fund settlement)
Think of it as: A bank locker for your shares. Just as a bank locker holds your physical valuables safely, your Demat account holds your shares digitally — secure, accessible, and protected even if the locker provider (broker) closes.
📱
Account 02
Trading Account
The platform you use to buy and sell on NSE and BSE

A Trading account is the interface through which you place buy and sell orders on stock exchanges. It is the "operating account" — when you tap "Buy 10 shares of Reliance" on Zerodha or Groww, you are using your Trading account. Shares then move between your Demat account and the exchange.

  • What it enablesPlacing buy/sell orders on NSE and BSE, accessing real-time stock prices, using order types (market, limit, SL), and accessing F&O, currency, and commodity segments
  • Who provides itYour broker (Zerodha, Groww, Upstox, HDFC Securities, etc.) — SEBI-registered Trading Members of NSE and BSE
  • What flows through itFunds from your linked bank account are transferred to the trading account before buying. Selling proceeds are returned to your bank within T+1 settlement
  • Linked toYour Demat Account (shares move here after buying) and your Bank Account (fund inflows and outflows for trades)
Think of it as: The counter at a stock exchange where you give buy/sell instructions. You walk up to the counter (Trading Account), give your order, and the shares either come to your locker (Demat) or leave it and become cash in your bank.
The 3-Account Linkage
Bank Account ↔ Trading Account ↔ Demat Account — all three must be linked for seamless investing
When you open a Demat + Trading account, your broker links all three automatically. When you buy: money moves from Bank → Trading Account → Exchange. Shares move from Exchange → Demat. When you sell: shares move from Demat → Exchange. Cash moves from Exchange → Trading Account → Bank (T+1). All of this happens automatically and electronically — you never handle physical share certificates or manually transfer funds between accounts after the initial setup.
✅ Most modern brokers like Zerodha and Groww open both Demat and Trading accounts together in a single application process — you don't need to apply separately for each.

Step 01 · Research
Choose the Right Broker
⏱ 15–30 Minutes

Your broker choice determines your trading experience, costs, and tools. There are two types: Discount Brokers (low-cost, tech-first, ideal for most) and Full-Service Brokers (personalised advice, higher fees, ideal for those wanting guidance). For most self-directed investors, a discount broker is the right choice.

💰
Discount Brokers — Best for Most BeginnersZerodha (largest by users — 1.3 Cr+), Groww (most beginner-friendly interface), Upstox (backed by Tiger Global), Angel One (good for research tools). These charge ₹0 brokerage on delivery equity trades and typically ₹20 flat per F&O trade. No advisory services — you make your own decisions. Technology-first, mobile-optimised.
🏢
Full-Service Brokers — When You Want GuidanceHDFC Securities, ICICI Direct, Kotak Securities, Motilal Oswal. These offer research reports, relationship managers, and advisory services — but charge higher brokerage (0.3–0.5% per trade). Suitable if you value personalised guidance over cost savings. Margin policy — how much leverage do they offer (lower is safer for beginners). Customer support responsiveness. Depository — CDSL or NSDL (both are equally safe).
💡 Recommendation for beginners: Start with Zerodha (Kite) or Groww. Both have zero delivery brokerage, excellent mobile apps, strong SEBI compliance, and free educational resources. You can always add a second broker account later for comparison.

Step 03 · Identity Verification
PAN Card Verification
⏱ 1 Minute

Enter your PAN number. The broker's system instantly validates it with the Income Tax Department's database to verify your name and date of birth. Ensure the name entered matches exactly what is on your PAN — even minor spelling differences can cause KYC rejection. If your PAN has a name mismatch with Aadhaar, resolve it first with UIDAI.

📌 Your PAN number is in the format ABCDE1234F (5 letters, 4 digits, 1 letter). The 4th character indicates account type — P = Individual PAN. Check this matches what you're entering.

Step 04 · Aadhaar eKYC
Complete Aadhaar-Based KYC (The Fastest Step)
⏱ 2 Minutes

Aadhaar eKYC is what makes 10-minute account opening possible. This step verifies your identity and address in one shot using your Aadhaar data — replacing the old paper-based KYC that used to take days. Enter your 12-digit Aadhaar number, consent to UIDAI data sharing, and an OTP is sent to your Aadhaar-linked mobile number.

🔑
How Aadhaar eKYC WorksYou provide your Aadhaar number and consent to the broker accessing your Aadhaar data via UIDAI's API. UIDAI sends a 6-digit OTP to your registered mobile number. You enter the OTP. Your name, address, date of birth, and photo from UIDAI are pulled automatically — you don't need to manually enter or upload any details. The entire verification takes under 2 minutes.
📵
If Your Mobile is Not Linked to AadhaarYou will need to do offline KYC instead — upload Aadhaar XML (downloaded from UIDAI website with a share code) or visit a broker's partner office/CAMS/Karvy branch for physical KYC. This takes 2–5 working days. It is strongly recommended to link your mobile with Aadhaar via a UIDAI service centre or Aadhaar mobile app before starting account opening.
🔒
Privacy — What Data is SharedOnly your name, date of birth, gender, and address are shared from Aadhaar — your biometric data (fingerprint, iris) is never shared. You must explicitly consent. SEBI mandates that brokers cannot store your full Aadhaar number — only the last 4 digits are retained in their systems.
✅ Once Aadhaar eKYC is complete, your KYC is considered fully done for all capital market instruments — no re-KYC is needed for other brokers or mutual funds using the same Aadhaar.

Step 05 · Liveness Check
Live Selfie — In-Person Verification (IPV)
⏱ 1 Minute

SEBI mandates that every new account must be opened by the actual applicant in person — called In-Person Verification (IPV). For online accounts, this is done via a live selfie or short video where you may be asked to blink, smile, or move your head to confirm you are present in real time — not a photo being held up.

💡
Tips for a Successful SelfieUse good natural lighting — avoid direct sunlight from behind. Face the camera directly. Remove glasses if possible. Ensure your full face is within the frame. Do not use filters. Your face in the selfie is matched against your PAN/Aadhaar photograph by the broker's AI system. Mismatches trigger manual review, slowing account activation.
📝
Some Brokers Ask for a Short VideoZerodha, for example, asks you to record a short video where you state your name and city — as additional verification. This is done within the app using your smartphone camera. The video is reviewed by a team member within a few hours. It adds a human verification layer beyond the automated selfie match.
📌 The selfie is processed by AI in real time. If it fails 3 times, the application may be paused and require manual review — usually resolved within 24 hours by the broker's KYC team.

Step 06 · Bank Linkage
Link Your Bank Account
⏱ 2 Minutes

Your bank account must be linked to your trading account for fund transfers — adding money to buy stocks and receiving money when you sell. This is done through a penny-drop verification: the broker sends ₹1 to your bank account to confirm it is a real, active account in your name.

🔗
Penny Drop VerificationThe broker transfers ₹1 to your bank account. The account holder name returned by the bank is matched with your PAN/Aadhaar name. If they match, the account is considered verified and linked. This usually takes 5–15 minutes. If the name doesn't match (e.g. NRI accounts or joint accounts), additional verification is needed.
📱
UPI Linkage for Instant Fund TransfersAfter bank verification, link your UPI ID (Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm). This allows you to add funds to your trading account instantly using UPI — essential when you see a buying opportunity and need funds in the account quickly. NEFT/RTGS fund transfers can take 30 minutes–2 hours; UPI is instant.
⚠️
Which Bank Account to UseUse the bank account where your salary or primary income is received. This simplifies tax filing (your CA will thank you). Avoid using a joint account or parent's account — SEBI rules require the bank account to be in the same name as the trading/Demat account. Joint accounts with you as primary holder are acceptable.
✅ Most top banks (SBI, HDFC, ICICI, Axis, Kotak) support instant penny-drop verification. Rural cooperative banks may require manual verification — expect 1–2 extra days.

Step 07 · Legal Formalities
Fill Nominee Details + e-Sign Agreement
⏱ 3 Minutes

Two final steps before your account is activated: adding a nominee (who will inherit your investments if something happens to you) and digitally signing the account opening agreement using Aadhaar-based e-Sign. These are legally binding documents — read the key terms before signing.

👨‍👩‍👧
Nominee Registration — Do Not Skip ThisAdding a nominee ensures your investments transfer smoothly to your chosen beneficiary if you pass away. Enter the nominee's name, date of birth, Aadhaar number, relationship, and percentage share (100% if one nominee). Without a nominee, your family may face legal complications to claim your portfolio. You can update nominees anytime after account opening.
✍️
e-Sign with Aadhaar OTPThe broker will generate a PDF of your account opening agreement — the legal contract between you and the broker, Demat agreement with the depository, risk disclosure documents, and SEBI-mandated investor rights declaration. You sign it digitally using another Aadhaar OTP. This Aadhaar e-Sign has the same legal validity as a wet signature on paper.
📖
What to Read Before SigningThe brokerage charges and fee schedule. Margin pledge terms (if applicable). Data privacy terms. Voluntary demat freeze rules. Power of attorney (POA) terms — many brokers ask for POA to debit shares when you sell; understand its scope. You can refuse the POA and use CDSL TPIN-based verification instead for each sell transaction.
📌 SEBI has mandated that brokers provide a "Most Important Terms" (MIT) document — a simplified 1-page summary of key terms. Read this before signing the full agreement.

Account Activated — Make Your First Investment
⏱ Next Business Day

Your account is typically activated within 24 hours of completing all steps — often on the same day if done before 2 PM on a working day. You will receive your Client ID (trading account ID), Demat account number (BO ID), and login credentials by email and SMS. Log in, add funds via UPI, and you are ready to place your first order.

Credentials You Will ReceiveClient ID (your trading account number — e.g. AB1234), UCC (Unique Client Code — same as Client ID), Demat account number/BO ID (16-digit — e.g. 1208160012345678 for CDSL), login password (temporary — change immediately on first login), and MPIN for mobile app.
💰
Add Funds Before Your First TradeGo to "Add Funds" in the app. Select UPI or NEFT/RTGS. Enter the amount. Complete UPI payment. Funds appear in your trading account instantly via UPI. Start with an amount you're fully comfortable losing — ideally ₹5,000–₹10,000 for your first few learning trades. Never invest money you can't afford to lose while you're still learning.
📊
Your First Smart InvestmentFor your very first purchase, consider a Nifty 50 Index ETF (NIFTY BEES or any broker-branded Nifty ETF). Buy 1–5 units. This gives you instant exposure to India's 50 largest companies, zero stock-picking stress, and an understanding of how buying, settlement, and portfolio tracking works — all at minimal risk.

06 — KYC Explained

Two Ways to Complete KYC

KYC (Know Your Customer) is the legally mandated identity verification process for all financial accounts in India. There are two ways to complete it — Aadhaar-based instant eKYC (recommended) or offline physical KYC. Here's everything you need to know about both.

⚡
Recommended · Instant
Aadhaar eKYC — 2 Minutes
UIDAI's Aadhaar eKYC API allows instant identity and address verification using your Aadhaar data. A single OTP to your registered mobile completes the entire KYC process. All your details are populated automatically from UIDAI — no manual entry or document uploads needed for identity/address proof.

6-digit OTP sent to your Aadhaar-linked mobile number
4
Enter OTP — name, DOB, address, photo auto-populated
5
KYC complete — account activation in 24 hours
📄
Alternative · 2–5 Days
Offline / Physical KYC
If your mobile number is not linked to Aadhaar or you prefer not to use eKYC, you can use offline KYC. This involves submitting physical copies of documents to the broker or a KYC Registration Agency (KRA) — CAMS, CVL, Karvy. Takes 2–5 working days. Required for NRIs and those with unlinkd Aadhaar.

07 — Avoid These

Common Mistakes When Opening an Account

These are the most common errors new investors make during account opening — and how to avoid each one before they become problems.

⚠️
Mistake 01 · Very Common
Opening with an Unregistered or Fake Broker
Scam brokers with professional-looking websites promise guaranteed returns, steal your documents, and disappear with your money. Always verify SEBI registration at sebi.gov.in before uploading any document. A legitimate broker's SEBI registration number is prominently displayed on their website. Never open an account based on unsolicited WhatsApp or Telegram recommendations.
📛
Mistake 02 · KYC Issue
Name Mismatch Between PAN and Aadhaar
Even a single character difference between your name on PAN and Aadhaar causes KYC rejection. "Rahul Kumar" vs "Rahul K" — rejected. Check both documents side by side before starting. If there's a mismatch, update your Aadhaar name via UIDAI's online portal or visit an Aadhaar centre. This is the #1 reason for account opening delays in India.
📵
Mistake 03 · Mobile Issue
Aadhaar Mobile Number Not Active or Unlinked
eKYC requires your Aadhaar-linked mobile to be active and in your possession. If you changed numbers without updating Aadhaar, eKYC will fail. Visit an Aadhaar Seva Kendra or your telecom operator to update. Alternatively, get a new SIM on your old number. This is especially common with users who have changed cities or operators since their original Aadhaar registration.
🏦
Mistake 04 · Bank Issue
Linking Someone Else's Bank Account
SEBI strictly mandates that the bank account linked to a trading account must be in the same name as the account holder. Linking your parent's, spouse's, or sibling's bank account causes penny-drop verification failure and KYC rejection. Open a savings account in your own name first — it takes 30 minutes online with Aadhaar. Many brokers (Zerodha, Groww) even help you open a partner bank account during the process.
🎯
Mistake 05 · Strategy Issue
Activating F&O Before Learning Equity
Many new investors activate F&O segments immediately "just in case" without understanding that SEBI's 2023 study shows 89% of retail F&O traders lose money. Activating F&O requires income proof — and the temptation to use it without proper education is financially dangerous. Open a basic equity + mutual fund account first. Add F&O only after 2+ years of profitable equity investing experience.
💸
Mistake 06 · Financial Issue
Investing Money You Need Within 3 Years
Stock market investments can fall 30–50% in bear markets. Never invest your emergency fund, home down-payment savings, or any money you may need within the next 2–3 years into equities. Markets can take months to years to recover. The rule: invest only money that you don't need for at least 3 years and can remain untouched even if its value halves temporarily.

📖 Glossary

Key Terms Glossary

Every key term from this chapter — Demat, Trading, KYC, brokers, order types — defined in plain language with real examples.

🔍
Demat Account
Accounts
Dematerialised Account — an electronic repository that holds your shares, bonds, ETFs, and mutual fund units in digital form. Maintained by CDSL or NSDL (SEBI-regulated depositories). Your shares are safe in your Demat even if your broker shuts down.
💡 Think of it as a bank locker for shares. Just as your gold is safe in a locker even if the bank branch closes, your Demat holdings are safe regardless of broker.
Trading Account
Accounts
The interface through which you place buy and sell orders on NSE and BSE. Routes your orders to the exchange's matching engine. Always linked to your Demat (for share delivery) and bank (for fund transfers). Cannot function independently.
💡 Trading account = the door. Demat = the locker. You use the door every time you buy/sell; the locker holds what you've accumulated over time.
CDSL
Accounts
Central Depository Services Limited — one of India's two SEBI-regulated depositories that holds shares in electronic form. Used by Zerodha, Groww, Upstox, and most discount brokers. Holds 13+ crore Demat accounts. Backed by BSE.
💡 Your 16-digit Beneficiary Owner ID (BO ID) starting with "12" is a CDSL account. Verify your holdings anytime directly at cdsl.com — independent of your broker app.
NSDL
Accounts
National Securities Depository Limited — India's other SEBI-regulated depository, backed by NSE. Holds 4.1+ crore Demat accounts. Used by ICICI Direct, HDFC Securities, Kotak Securities, and most full-service brokers.
💡 Your 16-digit DP ID starting with "IN" is an NSDL account. Both CDSL and NSDL are equally safe — your choice of broker determines which depository you use.
KYC
KYC & Docs
Know Your Customer — SEBI-mandated identity and address verification process required before opening any financial account. For Demat accounts, KYC involves verifying PAN, address proof, bank account, and signature. Prevents money laundering and fraud.
💡 KYC is a one-time process per PAN. Once KYC is done with a SEBI-registered intermediary, it's shared across the financial ecosystem — you won't need to repeat it for every account.
eKYC (Aadhaar OTP)
KYC & Docs
Electronic KYC using Aadhaar — the fastest way to complete identity verification. An OTP is sent to your Aadhaar-linked mobile. One OTP click completes all identity and address verification digitally. Account activated within 24 hours. Initial trading limit: ₹2 lakh/year until upgraded.
💡 eKYC = 2 minutes. Video KYC = 5 minutes but gives full trading access. Upgrade eKYC to Video KYC anytime from the broker app to remove the ₹2 lakh limit.
PAN Card
KYC & Docs
Permanent Account Number — a unique 10-character alphanumeric identifier issued by the Income Tax Department. Mandatory for all investments above ₹50,000 and for opening a Demat account. Links all your financial transactions for tax purposes.
💡 Apply for PAN at incometax.gov.in if you don't have one. Processing: 7–10 days. E-PAN (PDF) delivered in 48 hours for digital-only option. Without PAN, no Demat account is possible.
Aadhaar
KYC & Docs
12-digit Unique Identification Number issued by UIDAI to every Indian resident. Used for address proof and identity verification in Demat KYC. Must have an active mobile number linked for OTP-based eKYC. Update your mobile at the nearest Aadhaar centre if not linked.
💡 Check if your mobile is linked: visit uidai.gov.in → "Verify Aadhaar" → enter Aadhaar number → check linked mobile. If not linked, visit nearest Aadhaar enrolment centre.
Discount Broker
Brokers
A SEBI-registered broker offering low-cost, self-directed trading without personalised advisory. Charges flat ₹0 brokerage on delivery (CNC) trades; flat ₹20 per order on intraday/F&O. Examples: Zerodha, Groww, Upstox, Dhan, Angel One (hybrid).
💡 Best for: self-directed investors who research independently. Save 0.3–0.5% per trade vs full-service. On ₹10 lakh annual equity SIP, that's ₹3,000–₹5,000 saved annually.
Full-Service Broker
Brokers
A SEBI-registered broker offering research, stock recommendations, portfolio advisory, relationship managers, and physical branches — in addition to trading access. Higher cost: 0.3–0.5% per trade. Examples: HDFC Securities, ICICI Direct, Kotak Securities, Sharekhan.
💡 Best for: investors who want hand-holding, research access, or are not comfortable with self-directed investing. The higher cost is justified if you actively use the advisory services.
SEBI Registration
Brokers
All stockbrokers in India must be registered with SEBI before they can operate. SEBI assigns a unique registration number (e.g., INZ000031633 for Zerodha). Always verify a broker's SEBI registration at sebi.gov.in before opening an account.
💡 Never open an account with an unregistered "broker" promising high returns. Go to sebi.gov.in → "Registered Entities" → search by broker name. If not found, do not proceed.
CNC (Cash & Carry)
Trading
Order type for delivery-based equity investment — you buy shares that are credited to your Demat account on T+1. No leverage, no time limit, no auto-square-off. The correct order type for all investors. Shares stay in Demat until you manually sell them.
💡 Always use CNC for your first trades and all long-term investments. CNC = investor mode. When you see "Add to Cart" in broker apps (Zerodha Kite), it defaults to CNC for delivery.
MIS (Margin Intraday Square-off)
Trading
Order type for intraday trading — must be closed before market close (auto square-off at 3:20 PM). Leveraged: control larger positions with smaller capital. High risk. If you forget to close, broker automatically squares off at potentially unfavourable price.
💡 Never use MIS as a beginner. Intraday leverage amplifies losses as much as gains. SEBI 2023: 89% of retail intraday traders lose money. Master CNC investing first.
Limit vs Market Order
Trading
Market Order: executes immediately at best available price — fast but price not guaranteed. Limit Order: executes only at your specified price or better — price controlled but may not execute if price doesn't reach your level. Always prefer Limit Orders to avoid slippage.
💡 Prefer Limit Orders always. Place limit order ₹0.50–₹1 above current ask price for buy orders to ensure execution while avoiding wide spread slippage, especially on less liquid stocks.
DP ID & BO ID
General
DP (Depository Participant) ID identifies your broker with the depository. BO (Beneficiary Owner) ID is your unique Demat account number. Together they form your 16-digit Demat account number. Required for IPO applications, pledging shares, and CDSL/NSDL direct access.
💡 Your Demat account number is printed in your account opening confirmation email. Keep it handy for IPO applications — ASBA requires it during subscription.
Nomination
General
A legal designation specifying who inherits your Demat account holdings in case of death. SEBI has made nomination mandatory for Demat accounts. Can be added online in the broker app. Without nomination, transfer of shares to legal heirs is a lengthy legal process.
💡 Add a nominee immediately after opening your Demat account. Takes 2 minutes. Protects your family — shares worth lakhs can be transferred quickly vs years of legal complications without nomination.
AMC (Annual Maintenance Charge)
General
Annual fee charged by brokers for maintaining your Demat account. Most discount brokers waive AMC for the first year. Typically ₹300–₹750/year after the free period. Charged by the Depository Participant (broker), not CDSL/NSDL directly.
💡 Zerodha: ₹300/year AMC. Groww: ₹0 AMC (free). Compare AMC before choosing — on a ₹1 lakh portfolio, ₹300 AMC = 0.3% drag. Worth comparing across platforms.
