01 — The Foundation

Why Commodity Prices Move Stock Markets

Commodities are the raw materials of the economy. Every company that uses energy, metals, or chemicals as inputs sees its profitability directly change when commodity prices move. The same price move can be a boon for one sector and a crisis for another.

Crude Oil · Brent & WTI · OPEC Controlled · India Imports 85%
Crude Oil — India's Single Biggest Commodity Vulnerability
India is the world's third-largest crude oil importer, buying 85% of its requirements from abroad at ~₹12–18 lakh crore annually. Every $10 change in Brent crude price adds or removes approximately ₹80,000–90,000 crore from India's annual import bill. This makes crude oil the single most influential commodity for the Indian economy and stock market, touching everything from airline profits to paint company margins to the Rupee itself.

Gold — The Crisis Barometer and Safe Haven Asset
India is the world's second-largest gold consumer, buying ~800–900 tonnes annually — primarily for jewellery. Gold serves two distinct roles: (1) A safe haven asset that rises during geopolitical crises, market crashes, and inflation fears — as investors flee to safety. (2) A jewellery commodity that affects companies like Titan, Kalyan Jewellers, and PC Jeweller. Rising gold prices signal fear; falling prices signal risk-on sentiment.

Silver · MCX & COMEX · Half Safe Haven · Half Industrial Metal
Silver — The Hybrid Commodity with Dual Personality
Silver is unique — it behaves like both a safe-haven asset (like gold) AND an industrial metal (like copper). Approximately 60% of silver demand comes from industrial applications (solar panels, electronics, batteries, medical devices), while 40% is investment/jewellery demand. This dual nature makes silver more volatile than gold and more economically sensitive. Rising silver can signal either fear (crisis) or growth (industrial demand).

Natural Gas — The Hidden Commodity That Controls Fertiliser and Power
Natural gas is the primary feedstock for nitrogenous fertilisers (urea) — making it the most critical input cost for India's entire agricultural sector. It also powers industrial boilers, CNG vehicles, and city gas networks. India produces some gas domestically (ONGC, GAIL) but imports as LNG at international prices. A spike in global gas prices can destroy the entire profitability of fertiliser companies in a single quarter.

Copper — The Economy's Health Monitor and EV Revolution's Most Critical Metal
Copper is nicknamed "Dr Copper" because its price reliably predicts global economic health — rising copper signals economic growth (manufacturing, construction expanding), falling copper signals contraction. Copper is in every electrical wire, transformer, motor, and EV battery. The EV revolution has created a new structural demand driver — an EV uses 4× more copper than a conventional car. India's Hindalco (via Novelis) and Vedanta are the key copper plays.

When gold rallies sharply (MCX +5%+ in a week), reduce equity risk. Gold is the oldest and most reliable fear signal in markets. 04 — Practical Application

How to Use Commodity Signals in Your Portfolio

Commodity prices are publicly available, move every second, and predict stock sector moves 1–4 weeks before they fully appear in quarterly results. Here's how to use them:

🛢️
Oil Strategy
Track Brent Crude as Your Daily India Macro Signal
Check Brent crude every morning (it trades internationally overnight). Above $90/barrel = reduce aviation, FMCG, paint exposure. Add IT and Pharma as rupee plays. Below $75/barrel = increase aviation, OMCs, FMCG. ONGC and Oil India are direct oil price plays — no analysis needed, pure commodity beta.
🪙
Gold Strategy
Gold as Crisis Insurance — Hold 5–10% Always
Maintain a permanent 5–10% gold allocation via Sovereign Gold Bonds (best: 8-year tenure, 2.5% annual interest + gold price appreciation, tax-free on maturity) or Gold ETF. Gold consistently rallies during India's biggest market drops — COVID, 2008, Russia-Ukraine. It's portfolio insurance that also earns returns.
🪨
Silver Strategy
Silver as Green Economy Play — Not Just Safe Haven
Silver's EV and solar demand story makes it different from gold. If you believe India will massively expand solar (targeting 500GW by 2030) and EV adoption (30% by 2030), silver has structural tailwinds as a green energy input metal. Silver ETF (Nippon India, ICICI Pru) is now available on NSE/BSE for easy access.
🔥
Gas Strategy
Natural Gas — Track TTF for Fertiliser Plays
Track European TTF gas prices (available on commodities websites). When TTF falls below $10/mmBtu from elevated levels, fertiliser companies (Chambal, Coromandel, GSFC) are likely to see dramatic margin recovery in the next quarter. This 1-quarter lead time is one of the most reliable commodity-to-stock trading setups in Indian markets.
🔵
Copper Strategy
Copper = Economic Health Check + EV Signal
Watch LME copper monthly. Above $9,000/tonne with rising trend = global economy expanding → add cyclicals (metals, capital goods, industrials). Below $7,000/tonne with falling trend = slowdown warning → reduce cyclicals, add defensives. For cable companies (Polycab, KEI), falling copper is a buy signal as their margins improve dramatically.
⚙️
Steel Strategy
Steel — Separate Producers from Consumers in Portfolio
Steel producers (Tata Steel, JSW) and steel consumers (Maruti, Whirlpool) move in opposite directions. Never hold both heavily simultaneously — they cancel each other out. In infrastructure bull cycles (Budget capex push), overweight producers. When auto cycle is strong but steel is falling, overweight auto. The steel cycle is India's most predictable commodity-equity pattern.

Dr. Copper — the commodity with a PhD in economics
"Copper rising = global economy expanding · Copper falling = recession signal"

Steel Trick · India's Infrastructure Metal
Steel price = India capex health indicator
"Steel rises = India capex boom · Steel falls = China slowdown or global recession"

🇨🇳China dumping risk: China overproduces steel → dumps cheaply globally → Indian steel prices collapse → JSW, Tata Steel margins compressed. Government responds with anti-dumping duties (safeguard tariffs).
💡 Anti-dumping duty indicator: When government imposes safeguard duty on Chinese steel imports (as in 2015–2017 and again in discussions 2024), it signals Indian steel price floor protection → buy Indian steel stocks. Monitor Ministry of Steel announcements for duty imposition/removal — these are highly tradeable events.

Gas prices drive two critical India sectors
"Gas price rises = Fertiliser subsidy burden rises + Power cost rises"

📖 Glossary

Key Terms Glossary

Every key commodity term — Brent, WTI, ATF, HRC steel, OPEC+, Gold ETF, SGB, APM pricing, LNG, MCX — defined with India market impact and quantified sector examples.

🔍
Brent Crude (vs WTI)
Crude Oil
Brent Crude: The international benchmark for oil prices, based on North Sea crude. India prices all its oil imports against Brent (or Brent-linked Middle East grades like Arab Light, Murban). WTI (West Texas Intermediate): US domestic benchmark, typically $2–4 cheaper than Brent. For India analysis, always use Brent — it's the relevant pricing reference. India imports ~5 million barrels/day. At $85/barrel = ~$155 billion annual oil import bill (≈ largest single import item, ~27% of total imports).
💡 Brent quick math: India imports 5 mb/d. Brent at $80 = 5 × $80 × 365 = $146B/year. At $90 = $164B/year — a $18B increase for $10 oil rise. At ₹84/USD = ₹1.5 lakh crore extra annual burden. This is why every $10 Brent rise = macro stress for India — trade deficit widens, rupee depreciates, inflation rises, RBI gets hawkish.
OMC (Oil Marketing Companies)
Crude Oil
India's state-owned petroleum retailers — HPCL (Hindustan Petroleum), BPCL (Bharat Petroleum), and IOC (Indian Oil Corporation). They refine crude and sell petrol, diesel, LPG, and CNG to consumers. When government controls retail fuel prices (as India often does), OMCs bear "under-recovery" losses when crude is expensive and retail prices aren't raised. Under-recovery = share price falls. When crude falls and retail prices stay fixed = marketing margins expand = share price rallies. OMC stocks are direct plays on Brent crude inverse.
💡 OMC trading insight: When Brent is sustainably below $80, OMCs generate strong marketing margins on frozen retail prices → HPCL, BPCL, IOC stocks rally 20–40%. When Brent spikes above $95 with no retail price hike → under-recovery losses → stocks fall 15–25%. Track the Brent price vs last retail fuel price revision — the spread reveals OMC margin direction.
ATF (Aviation Turbine Fuel)
Crude Oil
Aviation Turbine Fuel — jet fuel for commercial aviation. Priced as a derivative of Brent crude (typically a 15–20% premium to crude due to refining). ATF constitutes 35–45% of an airline's operating costs — the single largest expense. Every $10/barrel Brent rise = ~3–5% increase in airline operating costs. ATF is revised monthly by OMCs in India. When ATF falls 10%, IndiGo (India's largest airline, ~60% market share) EBIT improves by ~₹2,000–2,500 crore annualised — directly translating to stock upside.
💡 Airline-oil relationship: IndiGo profitability is highly correlated to Brent. FY2024 (oil $80–85): IndiGo reported record profits (₹8,000+ crore PAT). Earlier periods of $100+ oil (FY2022): Airline sector losses. Buy airline stocks when Brent sustainably falls below $80 — ATF relief directly improves earnings visibility for the next 2–4 quarters.
Gold ETF & Sovereign Gold Bond (SGB)
Gold
Two investment vehicles for gold exposure without physical gold: Gold ETF: Mutual fund investing in physical gold, traded on NSE/BSE like a stock. Price tracks gold MCX price. Expense ratio: 0.5–1%. SGB (Sovereign Gold Bond): Government of India bond denominated in grams of gold. Pays 2.5% annual interest + gold price appreciation. 8-year maturity (tax-free on redemption if held to maturity). SGB has triple advantage: gold price + 2.5% interest + tax-free at maturity. The best long-term gold instrument. Note: RBI has paused new SGB issuance since late 2023.
💡 SGB math: Buy ₹1 lakh SGB when gold at ₹6,000/gram (166 grams notional). Annual interest: ₹2,500 (2.5%). After 8 years if gold at ₹10,000/gram: ₹16.6 lakh redemption + ₹20,000 interest cumulative = ₹16.8 lakh total vs ₹1 lakh invested. Tax-free. This compounding makes SGB superior to physical gold and most gold ETFs for a 5–8 year horizon.
India Gold Demand Cycle
Gold
India is the world's 2nd largest gold consumer (after China), importing 700–800 tonnes annually. Two distinct demand peaks: (1) Wedding/Festive season: October–February, especially around Dhanteras (highest single-day gold purchase event in India), Dussehra, and winter weddings. (2) Year-round jewellery demand driven by cultural belief in gold ownership. Gold is the 2nd largest import after crude oil, making high gold prices (combined with high oil) a double trade deficit pressure on the INR.
💡 Dhanteras trading indicator: Gold sales on Dhanteras day are a leading indicator for jewellery retail demand that quarter. Check Tanishq (Titan), Kalyan Jewellers, Malabar Gold sales announcements on Dhanteras. Strong same-store growth on Dhanteras → buy jewellery stocks for the following 1–2 quarters (winter wedding season demand follow-through).
HRC (Hot-Rolled Coil) Steel Price
Metals
The benchmark price for flat steel in India — used for auto body panels, construction, appliances, and pipes. Published weekly by JSW Steel and monitored by all steel companies. HRC price is the primary determinant of Indian steel companies' realisations (the price they sell at). When HRC rises ₹2,000–3,000/tonne, Tata Steel and JSW Steel earn significantly higher margins in the next quarter. Track HRC price trends on steelmint.com or SteelGuru — a 3-month sustained HRC rise strongly predicts an earnings beat.
💡 HRC-to-stock lead time: HRC rise in Q1 → Steel company EBITDA improves in Q2 earnings → Analyst upgrades → Stock rallies. The 6–8 week lag between HRC price move and stock price response creates a systematic trading opportunity. Example: HRC rises ₹3,000/tonne in May → buy JSW/Tata Steel in May → sell post-Q2 results in July/August when beat is confirmed and priced in.
Dr. Copper (Economic Barometer)
Metals
Copper's nickname — "Dr. Copper" — reflects its reputation as an economic health indicator. Copper is used in everything (electrical wiring, plumbing, construction, electronics, EVs) — so demand tracks real economic activity. When copper prices rise sustainably: global economic expansion is confirmed. When copper falls sharply: recession fears are real. EV structural demand: An EV uses 83 kg copper vs 23 kg in an ICE vehicle. As global EV penetration grows, copper faces structural demand increase of 30–40% by 2030–35. Hindalco (via Birla Copper) is India's primary copper play.
💡 Copper supercycle thesis: Global copper mine supply growth is limited (new mines take 10–15 years to develop). EV demand + data centre power + renewable energy installations will absorb all incremental supply. Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan both project copper at $15,000–20,000/tonne by 2030 (vs $9,000 in 2024). Hindalco is a long-term structural beneficiary of this copper supercycle thesis.
APM Gas Pricing (India)
Natural Gas
APM (Administered Price Mechanism): India's government-set price for domestically produced natural gas from ONGC and Oil India fields. Revised bi-annually (April 1 and October 1) based on a formula linking to international gas prices with a floor/ceiling. Separate from LNG (Liquefied Natural Gas) import prices which are market-linked. APM gas is used for: power generation, fertiliser (urea), CNG/PNG (City Gas Distribution). When APM price rises: Fertiliser subsidy burden increases, OMCs' upstream gets better pricing, CNG/PNG retailers may raise prices.
💡 APM price impact chain: APM gas rises $0.5/mmBtu → Urea production cost rises ₹500–800/tonne → Government subsidy outflow rises (urea retail price is fixed at ₹266.50/bag since 2018) → Finance Ministry concerned about fertiliser subsidy → Chambal Fertilisers, GSFC, GNFC face margin pressure even though they're pass-through — because government approval for subsidy is delayed.
City Gas Distribution (CGD)
Natural Gas
The regulated distribution of natural gas to retail consumers through piped networks in cities. CNG (Compressed Natural Gas) for vehicles; PNG (Piped Natural Gas) for homes and commercial establishments. CGD companies: IGL (Delhi NCR), MGL (Mumbai), Gujarat Gas (Gujarat cities), Adani Total Gas. CGD companies have regulated return on pipelines but market-linked margins on gas trading. When gas input prices rise sharply, CGD companies must either absorb or pass through to consumers — with PNGRB (Petroleum and Natural Gas Regulatory Board) oversight on tariffs.
💡 CGD volume growth indicator: Monthly CNG volumes (released by IGL/MGL quarterly) show urban natural gas adoption. India's CGD expansion plan: Connect 295 geographical areas by 2030 (vs 60+ as of 2024). Rising CNG vehicle conversions (especially autos, buses) = volume growth regardless of commodity price. IGL volume growth of 10–15% YoY makes it a structural growth story beyond just gas price cycles.
MCX (Multi Commodity Exchange)
General
India's largest commodity derivatives exchange — where gold, silver, crude oil, natural gas, copper, aluminium, zinc, and agricultural commodities are traded as futures contracts. MCX prices are the domestic Indian benchmark (in INR), combining international LME/NYMEX/COMEX prices with INR/USD conversion and India-specific demand-supply. Companies use MCX to hedge commodity price risk. Retail investors can trade MCX commodity futures through commodity brokers. MCX price is the most relevant reference for Indian companies' cost/realisation — not just LME in USD.
💡 MCX practical use: When checking how a commodity move affects an Indian company: always convert to MCX price (International price × ₹/USD rate). Brent at $90 × ₹84 = ₹7,560/barrel MCX crude. When MCX crude rises, domestic oil marketing economics change. MCX gold at ₹72,000/10g (2024 levels) sets the cost for jewellers and the benchmark for gold ETFs/SGBs.
