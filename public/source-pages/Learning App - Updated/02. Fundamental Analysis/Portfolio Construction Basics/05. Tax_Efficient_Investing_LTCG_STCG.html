This is the highest return-per-unit-of-effort activity available to any Indian retail investor.

02 — STCG vs LTCG

Short-Term vs Long-Term Capital Gains — The Tax You Pay on Every Profit

Every time you sell an investment at a profit, you pay capital gains tax. The rate depends entirely on how long you held the investment. Understanding this one rule — and acting on it — can save lakhs of rupees over an investing lifetime.

⚡ STCG — Short-Term Capital Gains
20%
If you sell within 12 months of buying · Effective from July 2024
Short-Term Capital Gain (STCG) applies when you sell any listed equity share or equity mutual fund within 12 months of purchase. At 20%, this is the expensive way to profit. Many retail investors who trade frequently — buying and selling stocks over weeks or months — are unknowingly paying 20% tax on every gain. This is why active trading is one of the most wealth-destructive behaviours for retail investors: the combination of trading costs, emotional errors, and STCG tax makes it nearly impossible to beat buy-and-hold long-term investors.
📅
Trigger: Sell within 12 months of purchase date
Day of purchase is Day 0. If you sell on Day 364 or earlier, every rupee of profit is taxed at 20%. No exceptions. Even one day short of the 12-month mark costs you the lower LTCG rate.
🧮
Calculation: (Sell price − Buy price) × 20%
Example: Buy TCS at ₹3,000, sell at ₹3,600 within 11 months. Gain = ₹600/share. If you held 100 shares, gain = ₹60,000. STCG tax = ₹60,000 × 20% = ₹12,000 paid to government.
⚠️
The trap: STCG loss still can offset STCG gain
If you have made an STCG loss on another stock in the same financial year, you can net it against your STCG gain. But you cannot offset STCG gains with LTCG losses — they must be matched by type.
🌱 LTCG — Long-Term Capital Gains
12.5%
If you sell after 12 months of buying · First ₹1.25L per year is tax-free
Long-Term Capital Gain (LTCG) applies when you hold listed equity or equity mutual funds for more than 12 months before selling. At 12.5% (versus STCG's 20%), you are paying 37.5% less tax on the same profit — simply by waiting. And the government gives you the first ₹1.25 lakh of LTCG per financial year completely tax-free. This ₹1.25 lakh exemption is per person — so a couple investing together has ₹2.5 lakh of annual LTCG completely exempt. Over a 20-year career, this exemption alone can legally eliminate tax on a significant portion of your equity gains.
✅
Trigger: Hold for 12 months + 1 day minimum
Day of purchase is Day 0. On Day 366 or beyond, you qualify for LTCG. If you bought on April 1, 2023, you qualify on April 2, 2024. Simple rule: wait at least one year + one day.
🎁
₹1.25 lakh annual exemption — completely tax-free
Each financial year (April–March), the first ₹1.25 lakh of LTCG is exempt from tax. On gains above ₹1.25L, you pay 12.5%. Strategy: plan sales so annual LTCG stays at or below ₹1.25L to pay zero tax on equity gains.
🔗
LTCG losses can offset LTCG gains (not STCG)
If you sell one stock at an LTCG loss, that loss reduces your LTCG gains in the same FY. Unused LTCG losses can be carried forward for 8 years and used against future LTCG gains — a powerful tool called loss harvesting.
03 — Three Tax-Saving Strategies

Legal Ways to Minimise Capital Gains Tax — Every Indian Equity Investor Should Know These

Tax efficiency in equity investing comes down to three legal strategies that any investor can implement without any financial advisor. Each strategy is simple to understand and powerful in its effect on long-term wealth.

⏳
Strategy 1 — Hold for 12+ Months (The Single Most Powerful Tax Rule)
Most Impactful
The single most effective tax strategy for any retail investor: simply wait 12 months and one day before selling. This one act halves your tax rate from 20% to 12.5% on every rupee of profit. On a ₹5 lakh gain, the difference is ₹37,500 in saved tax — in a single trade. Over a lifetime of investing, this discipline adds up to lakhs of rupees. It also has a secondary benefit: it naturally prevents short-term emotional trading, because you feel the "cost" of selling early in the form of higher tax. The 12-month hold rule is the most important tax strategy and costs you nothing except patience.
💡 Real impact: ₹10L profit. STCG: ₹2L tax. LTCG (12.5% after ₹1.25L exemption): ₹1.09L tax. Saved: ₹91,000 — simply by waiting one more month.
🌾
Strategy 2 — Tax Loss Harvesting (Convert Losses into Tax Savings)
Advanced
Tax loss harvesting is the practice of deliberately selling positions that are currently at a loss — not because you've lost faith in the business, but specifically to realise the loss for tax purposes. You can then immediately (or after 30 days, to avoid wash-sale implications) buy the same stock back. The realised loss reduces your taxable capital gains in the same financial year. Example: You have ₹3 lakh LTCG from Bajaj Finance. You also hold Asian Paints at a ₹1.5 lakh unrealised loss. Sell Asian Paints — your net LTCG becomes ₹1.5L, of which ₹1.25L is exempt, so you pay tax only on ₹25,000 (₹3,125 total). Without harvesting, you'd have paid tax on ₹1.75L above exemption (₹21,875).
📊 Harvesting impact: ₹3L LTCG − ₹1.5L harvested loss = ₹1.5L net. After ₹1.25L exemption = ₹25K taxable. Tax: ₹3,125. Without harvesting: ₹21,875. Saved ₹18,750 on one FY's taxes.
📋
Strategy 3 — Use the ₹1.25L Annual Exemption Every Year (Never Waste It)
Systematic
The ₹1.25 lakh annual LTCG exemption resets every April 1st. If you don't use it, you lose it — it does not accumulate. Smart investors use this exemption systematically: every March, they calculate how much LTCG they have realised so far in the FY. If it's below ₹1.25L, they sell enough of a long-term profit position to bring total LTCG up to ₹1.25L — realising profit completely tax-free — and then immediately buy back the same shares. This "grandfathering" strategy resets the cost basis at the current (higher) price, permanently reducing future tax liability on those shares. Done every year, this adds up to significant long-term tax savings.
🎯 The annual rebalance: March 28th — check total LTCG so far this FY. If ₹40K, sell enough of your most profitable position to realise ₹85K more (total = ₹1.25L). Pay zero tax. Buy back immediately at new higher price. Future gains are measured from the new higher basis.

Example 04 · Mutual Fund SIP Tax · FIFO Rule · Important for SIP Investors
Kavitha — The SIP Investor's Tax Calculation
Mutual funds have a specific tax rule that every SIP investor must understand: the FIFO (First In, First Out) rule for redemptions. When you redeem mutual fund units, the oldest units are considered sold first — which can either work in your favour (older units are more likely LTCG) or against you if you're not tracking correctly.
₹5K monthly SIPFIFO redemption ruleEach SIP instalment = separate purchasePlan partial redemptions carefully
How the FIFO Rule Works for SIP Investors
Understanding FIFO with a Real SIP Example
Kavitha started a ₹5,000/month SIP in April 2022 in a Nifty 50 index fund. By April 2024 (24 months of SIPs), she has 24 separate purchase lots. Each monthly SIP is a separate transaction with its own purchase date and cost. When she redeems in April 2024:

• The April 2022 SIP units (24 months old) → LTCG ✅
• The May 2022 SIP units (23 months old) → LTCG ✅
• The March 2024 SIP units (1 month old) → STCG ⚠️

FIFO says the oldest units are sold first. So partial redemptions mostly benefit from LTCG if you've been investing consistently for 12+ months.

SIP Tax Planning Rules
📋 Rule 1: Each monthly SIP is a separate purchase. Track each instalment's date separately. Units bought in Month 1 qualify for LTCG one year before Month 13 units.
📋 Rule 2: If you've been doing SIP for 12+ months and want to partially redeem, FIFO means your oldest (most likely LTCG) units are sold first. This is generally in your favour.
✅ Rule 3: Stop SIP 12 months before you plan to redeem to ensure all units qualify for LTCG. Don't start a fresh SIP and immediately redeem — those new units will be STCG.
💡 Key Lesson — SIP Investors Get Better Tax Treatment Than They Realise
Most SIP investors worry unnecessarily about their tax bill. If you have been running a SIP for more than 24 months and want to partially redeem, FIFO means your oldest units — the ones with the most gain — are sold first and almost certainly qualify as LTCG. Combined with the ₹1.25L exemption, a long-running SIP investor can often redeem significant amounts with minimal or zero tax.

⚠️ Critical rule: Capital losses MUST be reported in your ITR even in years where you have no gains. If you don't report the loss in the year it was incurred, you LOSE the carry-forward right permanently. This is the single most expensive ITR filing mistake made by Indian investors.
✅ Key Lesson — Always File Your ITR Even in Loss Years
Capital losses that aren't reported in the year they occur cannot be carried forward. Rohan's ₹3.5L loss — reported in FY22's ITR — saved him ₹43,750 over four years. If he had skipped filing (or missed the loss in his ITR), that tax saving would have vanished permanently. Always file ITR, always report capital losses, even in years where you have no tax liability.
