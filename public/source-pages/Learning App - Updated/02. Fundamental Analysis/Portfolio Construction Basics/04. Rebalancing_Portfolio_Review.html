01 — Key Learning Outcome

Why Rebalancing Forces You to Buy Low and Sell High — Automatically

🔄
Your Core Takeaway
Rebalancing is the single investing activity that most closely approximates the ideal of "buy low, sell high" — automatically, systematically, and without requiring you to predict markets. When you rebalance, you are mechanically trimming positions that have grown expensive and adding to positions that have become relatively cheap. This is disciplined contrarian investing with no emotional judgement required.
Imagine you start with a perfectly balanced portfolio: 50% Reliance, 25% HDFC Bank, 25% TCS — all equal by your conviction. A year later, Reliance has rallied 60% while TCS is flat and HDFC Bank is down 10%. Your portfolio is now 63% Reliance, 18% HDFC Bank, 19% TCS. You are now accidentally heavily concentrated in the stock that has already had its big run. Rebalancing says: sell some Reliance (take profits on the winner), buy more HDFC Bank and TCS (add to underperformers). This feels deeply uncomfortable — you're selling a winner and buying underperformers. But research consistently shows that this mechanical discipline outperforms both "buy and hold" and "chase winners" strategies over long periods. Rebalancing forces the "buy low, sell high" behaviour that every investor claims they want but almost none actually practises. By the end of this chapter, you will have a complete rebalancing framework: when to rebalance, how much to rebalance, and how to do it in a tax-efficient way that maximises your net returns.

02 — Why Rebalance?

What Portfolio Drift Does to Your Risk — Without You Noticing

Portfolio drift is silent and dangerous. You build a carefully balanced portfolio — then do nothing — and one year later you have accidentally created a completely different, far more concentrated portfolio that you never intended. Here is exactly how drift destroys your original risk plan.

📈
How Drift Happens — The Silent Portfolio Reshaper
The Problem
You start with 10 stocks at 10% each — perfectly balanced. Over 3 years, your 3 best stocks double while 3 others are flat and 4 are down 20%. Without rebalancing, those 3 winners now represent 22–24% of your portfolio each. You have accidentally created a portfolio that is 65%+ concentrated in just 3 stocks. This is not the portfolio you built — this is drift. And the irony: the stocks that have already doubled are now the most expensive relative to their value, while the underperformers may represent the best future opportunity.
🚨 The drift trap: An investor who bought Bajaj Finance, Asian Paints, and TCS in equal weight in 2018 and never rebalanced found 72% of their portfolio in these 3 stocks by 2023 — a concentration they never planned and may not have wanted at those valuations.
🔄
Why Rebalancing Outperforms Buy-and-Hold
The Evidence
Research across global markets consistently shows that annual rebalancing adds 1–2% per year to portfolio returns versus buy-and-hold. This is called the "rebalancing bonus." It comes from three sources: (1) Mean reversion — stocks that have risen sharply tend to give back some gains; buying underperformers systematically captures mean reversion. (2) Forced discipline — rebalancing prevents the emotional mistake of chasing recently-hot stocks. (3) Risk control — by consistently trimming overweight positions, you prevent catastrophic single-stock risk from building up invisibly.
✅ The evidence: MSCI research shows that equal-weight rebalanced portfolios outperformed cap-weighted (drift-allowed) portfolios by 1.8% per year over 20 years across 40 countries. The rebalancing bonus is real and consistent.
💰
Tax-Smart Rebalancing — Don't Let LTCG Eat Your Gains
Tax Efficiency
The biggest objection to rebalancing: "But I'll have to pay capital gains tax!" This is true — but manageable. Long-term capital gains (LTCG) on Indian equity are taxed at 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh. Smart rebalancing strategies: (1) Use new SIP money to buy underweight positions rather than selling overweight ones — avoids taxable events. (2) Rebalance only positions held for 1+ year to qualify for LTCG treatment. (3) Net LTCG gains against any LTCL losses in the same financial year. (4) The ₹1.25L annual LTCG exemption allows modest rebalancing each year completely tax-free.
💡 The smart move: Instead of selling overweight Bajaj Finance to rebalance, direct next 6 months of SIP contributions entirely to underweight positions. Same result, zero tax event. Only sell if drift is extreme (position >20% overweight vs target).
📅
The Annual Review Checklist — Not Just Allocation, But Thesis
Annual Discipline
Rebalancing is not just about fixing allocation weights — it should trigger a full annual review of every holding's thesis. This is the moment to ask: is the original reason I bought this stock still valid? Has management delivered on their promises? Has the competitive landscape changed? Has valuation drifted into expensive territory? Many investors skip this review and discover 3 years later that they are still holding a stock whose thesis broke 18 months ago — simply because they never scheduled the annual check.
📋 Annual review date: Set a fixed date — April (post full-year results), or November (Q2 results season). Block one full day. Review every position: thesis intact? Management delivering? Valuation zone? Only after this review, decide allocation changes.
03 — Three Rebalancing Methods

Calendar · Trigger · Hybrid — Which Rebalancing Method Is Right for You?

There is no single "correct" rebalancing method. Each approach has advantages and trade-offs. Understanding all three helps you pick the one that matches your investment style, time availability, and tax situation.

📅
Calendar-Based Rebalancing
Rebalance on fixed dates regardless of drift
You set a specific date — annually, semi-annually, or quarterly — and rebalance every time it arrives, regardless of how much or little drift has occurred. The discipline is in the schedule, not the magnitude of drift. Annual rebalancing is the most popular and most tax-efficient version (qualifies more positions for LTCG treatment). Quarterly is more precise but creates more taxable events. Best for investors who prefer simplicity and predictability.
📅 Rule: Review every April 1st (post full-year results). Any position deviating by more than 3% from target → rebalance to target. All others → hold.
Simple to execute — just set a reminder
Maximises LTCG qualification (annual)
Prevents emotional rebalancing during market crashes
Easy to explain and maintain consistently
🎯
Threshold / Trigger Rebalancing
Rebalance only when drift exceeds a pre-set threshold
Instead of a fixed date, you rebalance when any position drifts beyond a pre-defined threshold — typically ±5% from target weight. A stock targeted at 10% that grows to 15%+ triggers an immediate review and partial sale. This approach rebalances more often when markets are volatile (more drift) and less often when markets are calm (less drift) — which is actually the opposite of what most investors do emotionally.
🎯 Rule: Any position exceeding target +5% or falling below target −5% triggers a rebalance to the midpoint of the target range. Monitor monthly (5 minutes using a spreadsheet).
Catches extreme drift before it becomes a problem
Automatically more active in volatile markets
Prevents catastrophic single-stock concentration
More precise — only rebalances when needed
⚖️
Hybrid Rebalancing
Calendar review + threshold triggers — the best of both
The hybrid approach combines both: conduct a scheduled annual review (April/October) AND rebalance any time a position drifts beyond ±7–8% from target during the year. This gives the discipline of calendar-based rebalancing while catching extreme outliers before the annual review date. This is the most commonly recommended approach for long-term Indian equity investors — it balances tax efficiency with risk control.
⚖️ Rule: Annual April review (always). Plus immediate trigger if any position exceeds target by ±8% at any monthly check. Prioritise directing new SIP money to underweight positions before selling overweight ones.
Best balance of simplicity and precision
Annual review catches thesis changes too
Emergency trigger prevents extreme concentration
Recommended for most long-term investors

Why This Works Beautifully
✅ Zero capital gains tax: No selling. No taxable events. Bajaj Finance continues compounding. HDFC and Sun Pharma receive fresh capital at current (cheaper) prices.
✅ No emotional difficulty: Selling a winner feels wrong. Redirecting new money to laggards feels manageable. Same economic effect — psychologically much easier to execute consistently.
✅ Works slowly but surely: SIP-based rebalancing is slower than selling — takes 6–12 months to meaningfully correct large drift. But for positions only moderately overweight (not extreme), it's the most elegant solution available.
✅ Key Lesson — Use New Money to Rebalance Before Selling Old Holdings
Always exhaust the "redirect new SIP money" option before triggering any sales. If the drift is modest (within 10%), SIP redirection over 6–12 months achieves the same outcome with zero tax cost. Only sell existing positions if the drift is extreme (15%+ overweight) and cannot be corrected within 12 months of SIP redirection.

Example 04 · The Wrong Rebalancing · Cautionary Tale
Ravi — The Thesis-Blind Rebalancer
Ravi understood rebalancing mechanically but made the most dangerous mistake: he applied the "buy underweight positions" rule to a holding whose thesis had fundamentally broken — effectively averaging down into a deteriorating business. Rebalancing without checking the thesis first is not discipline; it is automated value destruction.

The Correct Process (What Should Have Happened)
The Right Rebalancing Sequence (Always)
Step 1
A position is underweight → pause before adding.
Step 2
Read the latest concall transcript + check what has changed since you bought.
Step 3
Ask: Is the original thesis still intact? If yes → add as rebalancing rule says. If no → exit the position entirely, don't add.
Step 4
Only after thesis confirmed → execute the rebalancing trade.
🚨 Key Lesson — Rebalancing Rules Are NOT a Substitute for Thesis Review
Mechanical rebalancing without thesis review is dangerous. "Buy the underweight position" is correct only when the original thesis is intact. When a stock has fallen because the business has fundamentally changed — not because of temporary market noise — the correct action is to exit, not to add. Always check thesis before adding to any underweight position. The sequence is: (1) thesis check, (2) rebalancing rule. Never the reverse.
Example 05 · Complete Annual Review Framework · The Full Checklist
The Complete Annual Portfolio Review
A comprehensive step-by-step annual review process that combines thesis review, allocation rebalancing, and tax planning into a single structured annual exercise. This is the complete playbook for how serious long-term investors review their portfolios once per year.
Full-day annual reviewThesis + allocation + taxApril (post full-year results)Comprehensive playbook
Morning Session — Thesis Review (3–4 Hours)
9:00 AM — Pull up all portfolio holdings
List every stock with: purchase date, purchase price, current price, target weight, current weight.
10:00 AM — Thesis check for each holding
For each stock: read the most recent annual report chairman's message + Q4 concall transcript. Rate thesis: Strong / Intact / Weakening / Broken.
12:00 PM — Exit decisions
Any "Broken" thesis → exit immediately. Any "Weakening" → reduce to half position and set 2-quarter watchlist. "Strong/Intact" → proceed to afternoon session.
Afternoon Session — Rebalancing & Tax Plan (2–3 Hours)
2:00 PM — Allocation drift analysis
Calculate each position's current weight vs target. Flag any deviating by more than 5%. Prioritise using SIP redirection before selling.
3:00 PM — Tax-efficient execution plan
List any required sales. Check holding period for LTCG qualification. Net gains against losses. Target staying below ₹1.25L LTCG for the year.
4:30 PM — New investment ideas
Any capital freed from exits → identify 1–2 replacement candidates. Only from your research watchlist. No impulse decisions on annual review day.
Output — What You Leave the Review With
📋 Updated thesis document for every current holding — 1 paragraph per stock stating current thesis status.
📊 12-month SIP redirection plan — which positions get new SIP money over the next year to correct drift.
💰 Tax plan for FY — list of any positions to sell, in what order, netting gains vs losses to stay within ₹1.25L LTCG exemption.
👁️ Watchlist — 2–3 positions on 2-quarter monitoring due to weakening thesis. Specific metrics to track each quarter.
🗓️ Next review date set — either November (semi-annual) or next April. Calendar blocked.
✅ Key Lesson — One Day Per Year Is All It Takes
This comprehensive annual review takes 6–7 hours, once per year. In exchange, you get a fully aligned portfolio, tax-optimised rebalancing, thesis validation for every holding, and the psychological clarity of knowing exactly where you stand. The investors who do this consistently — regardless of market conditions — outperform those who react to daily news by a significant margin over 10+ years. Block the day in your calendar right now.
