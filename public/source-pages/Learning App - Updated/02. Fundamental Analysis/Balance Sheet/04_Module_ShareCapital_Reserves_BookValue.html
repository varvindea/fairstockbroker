Calculate Book Value & Understand Equity Build-Up

By the end of this module you will open any company on Screener.in, read the equity section, calculate Book Value Per Share in under 2 minutes, and judge whether the company is building real shareholder wealth — or destroying it quietly.

📈
Your Core Takeaway — The One Formula That Matters
Book Value Per Share = (Share Capital + Reserves) ÷ Total Shares Outstanding
Picture a tea stall started by 4 friends. Each put in ₹1,000 → Share Capital = ₹4,000. Over 3 years the stall earned ₹6,000 profit and kept it all inside → Reserves = ₹6,000. Total Equity = ₹4,000 + ₹6,000 = ₹10,000. With 4 shares, Book Value Per Share = ₹10,000 ÷ 4 = ₹2,500. If someone offers ₹5,000 for your share, they pay 2× Book Value — a premium for a profitable business. Exact same logic applies to CDSL, TCS, Nestle, or any listed company.
📋 Share Capital = Face Value × Shares 📈 Reserves = Accumulated Profits 🏆 BV/Share = Equity ÷ Shares
02 — Three Numbers You Must Know

Share Capital, Reserves & Book Value — Explained Simply

These three numbers live in the Equity section of every balance sheet. Together they reveal whether a business is genuinely compounding shareholder wealth — or just standing still. Each concept has a simple real-world analogy.

📋
Concept 1 of 3
Share Capital
Share Capital is the face value of all shares ever issued — a fixed, mostly unchanging number. It is NOT the market price. A company with ₹2 face value shares and 100 crore shares has ₹200 Cr Share Capital regardless of whether the share trades at ₹500 or ₹5,000. It only changes when new shares are issued (dilution) or cancelled (buyback).
Share Capital = Face Value × Total Shares Issued
CDSL: ₹10 face value × 10.4 Cr shares = ₹104 Cr. Stayed completely flat for 9 consecutive years — excellent capital discipline.
💡 Like the ₹10 printed on a ₹10 note — regardless of what ₹10 buys today vs 20 years ago, the printed number is always ₹10. That is face value.
💰
Concept 2 of 3
Reserves & Surplus
Reserves are profits the company kept inside instead of distributing as dividends — accumulated over every profitable year. This is the most important equity number to watch. Reserves growing year-on-year = consistent profitability + smart reinvestment. It is the "profit memory" of the business — every good year adds to it permanently.
Reserves = Previous Reserves + Net Profit − Dividends Paid
CDSL: ₹265 Cr in 2015 grew to ₹1,751 Cr by 2026 — 6.6× in 11 years, purely from retaining profits. Zero new shares issued.
💡 Like your savings account — every month you earn salary (profits), spend some on bills (dividends), keep the rest. Your savings balance grows every profitable month. That balance is Reserves.
📊
Concept 3 of 3
Book Value Per Share
Book Value Per Share (BVPS) tells you what each share is worth based on the company's actual net assets after all liabilities. It is the "floor value" — the per-share net worth if the company wound up today. A consistently rising BVPS year after year means real, durable wealth is being built for every shareholder.
BVPS = (Share Capital + Reserves) ÷ Shares Outstanding
CDSL Mar 2024: (₹104 Cr + ₹1,359 Cr) ÷ 10.4 Cr shares = ₹140.7 per share. That is 4× growth in 9 years!
💡 Like your personal net worth = all assets (house, car, savings) minus all loans. BVPS is the per-share net worth of the company's balance sheet. Growing every year = wealth being built.

What does each row actually tell you? 👇

📋 Equity Capital = ₹104 Cr stable for 9 years → ₹209 Cr after bonus
What happened: CDSL had 10.4 crore shares at ₹10 face value = ₹104 Cr Share Capital for 9 years. In FY2025 it became ₹209 Cr because CDSL declared a 1:1 bonus — giving every shareholder one extra share funded entirely from its own Reserves. No external money came in. This is a reward, not dilution. Key rule: Share Capital rising due to a bonus = good (company funded it from profits). Rising due to QIP/rights = scrutinise whether it creates value per share.
📈 Reserves = ₹265 Cr → ₹1,751 Cr (6.6× in 11 years)
What happened: CDSL earned profits every year and kept most inside the business instead of paying it all as dividends. Reserves compounded from ₹265 Cr (2015) to ₹1,751 Cr (2026) — 6.6× without issuing a single share to outsiders. This compounding of Reserves is the signature of a quality business. When Reserves grow 15–20%+ per year for a decade on Screener.in, you are almost certainly looking at a high-quality business with pricing power and disciplined management.
📊 BV/Share = ₹35.5 → ₹140.7 (peak) → ₹93.8 (post-bonus)
What happened: BV grew from ₹35.5 to ₹140.7 — 4× in 9 years, ~17% CAGR. After the FY25 bonus issue, shares doubled and BV halved to ₹84. This is NOT bad — total equity is identical, just split among more shares. By FY26 BV recovered to ₹93.8 and continues growing. Always adjust for bonus/splits when comparing BV across years — watch the multi-year trend, never an isolated drop caused by a corporate action.
💡 P/BV Ratio — Why Book Value Matters to Investors
Price-to-Book Value (P/BV) = Market Price ÷ BV Per Share. When CDSL's BV was ₹105 (FY22), market price was ~₹1,400. P/BV = 1,400 ÷ 105 = 13.3×. Investors paid ₹13 for every ₹1 of book value — a huge premium for the monopoly moat. P/BV below 1× often signals distress or deep undervaluation. Above 5× requires very high ROE to justify. CDSL's ROE of 30%+ fully justifies its premium. Always pair P/BV with ROE — never analyse one without the other.

Pattern in many mid/small caps · Very common trap for retail investors

The trap: A company's Equity Capital rises each year via QIPs, rights issues, or preferential allotments. Even if total equity grows, Book Value PER SHARE may stay flat or fall — because the pie is cut into more slices. Reserves may grow ₹200 Cr but if ₹300 Cr of new shares were simultaneously issued, BV per share drops. Always divide by CURRENT shares — not last year's count.
Equity Capital row rising every year without a declared bonus/split
Company cannot self-fund growth — needs external capital constantly
BV/Share flat or declining despite growing total equity
Management announces each fundraise as "growth capital" — check BV/share result
How to spot on Screener.in: Balance Sheet → watch Equity Capital row. Rising 2+ times without declared bonus/split = external dilution. Then check Ratios tab → Book Value Per Share chart. If BV/share is flat or falling while total equity rises — your stake is being diluted quietly.
Good vs Bad: Equity Capital rises due to BONUS = own profits funded it = Good. Rises due to QIP/rights = external dilution = scrutinise value created per share.
