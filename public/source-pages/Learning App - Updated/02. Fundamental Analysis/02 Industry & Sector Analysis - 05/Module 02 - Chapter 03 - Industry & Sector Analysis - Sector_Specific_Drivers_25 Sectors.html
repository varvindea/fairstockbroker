Sector 01 — Technology
IT Services
TCS · Infosys · Wipro · HCL Tech · Tech Mahindra — India exports over $250B in software services annually. These are people-intensive, margin-driven businesses where talent is the only asset that matters.
Revenue/Employee Attrition Rate Deal Wins TCV EBIT Margin
Key Performance Indicators
💰
Revenue per Employee
Annual revenue divided by headcount. Measures how productively each person generates value. TCS targets ₹30L+ per employee. Rising trend = efficiency gain.
📉
Attrition Rate
% of employees leaving annually. IT's biggest cost driver. High attrition (above 20%) forces expensive re-hiring and disrupts client delivery — directly compresses margins.
🤝
TCV of Deal Wins
Total Contract Value of new deals signed. A large TCV pipeline (3–5× quarterly revenue) means revenue growth is locked in for the next 2–3 years even if current market is slow.
📊
EBIT Margin
Operating profit as % of revenue. TCS maintains 25%+, Infosys targets 20–22%. Watch for margin compression from salary hikes, currency moves, or over-hiring ahead of demand.
🌍
USD Revenue Growth
IT earns mostly in USD but reports in INR. Always track USD constant-currency growth — INR depreciation can flatter headline numbers without real business improvement.
🔄
Utilisation Rate
% of billable employees actually billing clients. 80–85% = healthy. Below 75% = too many bench employees = margin pressure. A key operational efficiency metric.
What Drives Growth
💻
Digital Transformation Spend
When global enterprises spend on cloud migration, AI implementation, ERP upgrades, and cybersecurity — Indian IT companies capture the largest share. Track quarterly IT budgets of Fortune 500 companies.
💱
INR/USD Exchange Rate
Revenue earned in USD, costs paid in INR. A weaker rupee means every dollar of revenue translates to more rupees — a 5% depreciation can add 150–200 bps to EBIT margin automatically.
🏢
US/Europe Macro & IT Budgets
70–80% of Indian IT revenue comes from North America and Europe. A US recession or budget freeze forces enterprises to cut discretionary IT spending — directly hurting Indian IT companies 1–2 quarters later.
🚨 Red Flags
Attrition above 20% for 2+ quarters
TCV deal wins declining QoQ
Revenue growth guidance cut
Margin below 20% (for tier-1 IT)
✅ Green Signals
Large TCV deal wins announced
Attrition falling, utilisation rising
New capability in AI/cloud products
Margin expansion alongside revenue growth

Sector 02 — Financial Services
Banking
HDFC Bank · ICICI · SBI · Kotak · Axis — Banks borrow money cheaply (deposits) and lend it expensively (loans). The spread between these two rates, managed carefully, is how banks create value.
NIM NPA / GNPA CASA Ratio Credit Growth
Key Performance Indicators
📈
NIM — Net Interest Margin
Difference between lending rate earned and deposit rate paid. HDFC Bank's NIM of ~4% means it earns ₹4 on every ₹100 deployed. The most important profitability metric for any bank.
⚠️
GNPA / NPA Ratio
Gross Non-Performing Assets — loans that borrowers aren't repaying. GNPA above 5% signals severe credit quality problems. PSU banks historically had 10–15% GNPA — a major investor red flag.
🏧
CASA Ratio
Current Account + Savings Account as % of total deposits. Savings accounts pay ~3.5% — far cheaper than fixed deposits at 7%. High CASA (50%+) means cheap deposits = higher NIM = more profit.
💸
Credit Growth
% growth in loan book year-on-year. 15–18% growth for private banks is healthy in India's expanding economy. Too fast (>25%) risks quality deterioration; too slow means losing market share.
🛡️
PCR — Provision Coverage
How much of bad loans are set aside as provisions. PCR above 70% means the bank has already provisioned most of its bad debt — future P&L impact from NPAs is reduced. A safety cushion indicator.
💰
ROA & ROE
Return on Assets (target >1.5%) and Return on Equity (target >15%) for private banks. HDFC Bank has consistently delivered ROA ~2%, ROE ~17% — the benchmark for Indian banking excellence.
What Drives Growth
📉
RBI Repo Rate Cycle
When the RBI raises rates, lending rates rise faster than deposit rates — expanding NIM. When rates fall, NIM compresses. Banks with more floating-rate loans benefit most from rising rate cycles.
🏗️
Economic Growth & Credit Demand
When the economy grows, businesses borrow for capex and consumers borrow for homes and cars. Credit growth directly drives bank revenue. India's GDP growth is the single biggest structural driver of banking profitability.
📱
Digital Banking & CASA Acquisition
Banks with superior mobile apps, easy account opening, and UPI integrations attract more CASA deposits from younger users — directly reducing their cost of funds and improving long-term NIM.
🚨 Red Flags
GNPA rising QoQ (especially in retail)
NIM compressing without explanation
CASA ratio declining over time
Credit growth far ahead of deposit growth
✅ Green Signals
NPA declining + PCR rising together
CASA above 45% and improving
ROA above 1.5% consistently
Credit and deposit growing together 15%+

Sector 03 — Consumer Staples
FMCG
HUL · Dabur · Nestle · Marico · Britannia — Products bought weekly, not annually. The true measure of FMCG health is whether people are buying more units — not just paying higher prices for the same quantity.
Volume Growth Rural vs Urban Split Market Share Gross Margin
Key Performance Indicators
📦
Volume Growth
Units sold, not rupee revenue. Revenue can grow from price hikes even when volumes decline — a dangerous sign. Volume growth means real demand is rising, not just inflation. Target: 4–6% annually.
🌾
Rural vs Urban Split
India's rural markets (60% of population) drive volume for most FMCG. A rural slowdown — from poor monsoon, farmer distress — immediately hits volume. Urban premiumisation drives margin but smaller population.
🏆
Market Share
% of category revenue captured vs all competitors. Gaining market share in a growing category = compounding growth. Losing market share even during industry growth signals fundamental brand weakness.
💰
Gross Margin
Revenue minus raw material costs. Palm oil, milk, wheat, crude derivatives — raw material cycles directly set gross margin. An FMCG company's gross margin trend is its most watched profitability metric.
📡
Distribution Reach
Number of retail outlets directly or indirectly serviced. HUL's 8M+ outlet reach = structural distribution moat. New entrants can't replicate this for a decade regardless of product quality.
🆕
New Product Launches
Innovation as % of revenue. A portfolio that doesn't refresh becomes stale. FMCG companies track "launches in last 3 years as % of revenue" — healthy innovation pipeline = future growth engine.
What Drives Growth
🌧️
Monsoon & Rural Income
Good monsoon → good harvest → higher rural income → more FMCG purchases. India's June–September monsoon season is the single biggest demand driver for rural FMCG volume. Watch IMD forecasts carefully.
🛢️
Raw Material Price Cycle
Palm oil, crude oil derivatives, packaging materials — when these fall, FMCG companies can either cut prices to gain share or hold prices and expand margins. The direction of raw material costs drives quarterly margins dramatically.
🏪
Modern Trade & E-Commerce Shift
Organised retail (Big Bazaar, DMart) and e-commerce (Amazon, Blinkit) are growing faster than traditional kirana stores. Companies with strong modern trade + D2C channels are gaining relevance with premium urban consumers.
🚨 Red Flags
Volume growth negative for 2+ quarters
Market share loss to D2C/private labels
A&P spend cut to protect margins
Rural slowdown persisting 3+ quarters
✅ Green Signals
Volume + value growth together (both rising)
Market share gains in core categories
Rural recovery visible in distributor data
Gross margin expansion on falling input costs

Sector 04 — Healthcare
Pharmaceuticals
Sun Pharma · Dr. Reddy's · Cipla · Aurobindo · Divi's — India is the pharmacy of the world. The business is driven by science, regulation, and geography — not by marketing budgets alone.
US ANDA Filings R&D Spend % API vs Formulation USFDA Status
Key Performance Indicators
📋
ANDA Approvals (US)
Abbreviated New Drug Applications approved by USFDA. Each approval = right to sell a generic drug in the US. A company with 50+ pending ANDAs has a pipeline of future revenue that isn't yet in financials — a hidden growth signal.
🔬
R&D Spend as % of Revenue
Investment in future products. Generics companies spend 5–8%. Novel drug developers spend 15–20%. R&D cuts to boost near-term earnings are a negative signal — the pipeline dries up in 3–5 years.
🏭
USFDA Inspection Status
USFDA regularly inspects Indian manufacturing plants. A Warning Letter or Import Alert can ban all exports from that plant — wiping 15–25% of revenue overnight. Plant inspection outcomes are critical risk events.
🌍
US Revenue Mix
US generics market is the highest-margin geography. Companies with 30–40%+ US revenue mix typically earn better margins. But US business also comes with USFDA regulatory risk — a double-edged sword.
💊
Domestic Prescription Share
Market share in key domestic therapy areas (cardiac, anti-diabetic, respiratory). Sun Pharma dominates branded generics with a large MR (Medical Representative) force reaching 6L+ doctors.
⚗️
API vs Formulation Revenue
API (Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient) is the raw material; Formulations are finished medicines. Formulations have much higher margins. Backward integration into API = cost advantage + supply chain control.
What Drives Growth
🏥
Patent Cliff in Innovator Drugs
When a branded drug's patent expires, the first generic manufacturer to get USFDA approval (Para IV filing) earns 180-day exclusivity — a window of premium pricing with no competition. These opportunities can be worth $100–500M in one year.
🌡️
Chronic Disease Prevalence in India
Diabetes, hypertension, and cardiac patients in India require lifetime medication. As India ages and lifestyle diseases rise, the domestic chronic therapy market compounds at 10–12% annually — a structural demand driver for domestic pharma.
🌐
Emerging Market Expansion
Africa, Southeast Asia, and Latin America are growing pharma markets. Indian companies with WHO-GMP certifications can supply these markets at competitive prices — diversifying from the US/EU regulatory risk.
🚨 Red Flags
USFDA Warning Letter or Import Alert
R&D spend declining YoY
US pricing pressure eroding margins
ANDA approvals drying up (thin pipeline)
✅ Green Signals
Para IV filing exclusivity win in US
Successful USFDA plant re-inspection
Large ANDA approval pipeline visible
Specialty product launches in US at premium

Sector 05 — Consumer Durables
Automobile
Maruti · Tata Motors · M&M · Bajaj · Hero MotoCorp — Autos are big-ticket, cyclical purchases deeply tied to interest rates, fuel prices, and consumer confidence. The distinction between wholesale and retail data is critical.
Wholesale vs Retail Dealer Inventory Days ASP Trend EV Mix %
Key Performance Indicators
🏭
Wholesale vs Retail Dispatches
Wholesale = factory to dealer. Retail = dealer to customer. A company can show strong wholesale (high revenue) while dealers are accumulating unsold stock — a leading indicator of coming slowdown. Always compare both.
📅
Dealer Inventory Days
How many days of stock sitting at dealerships. Normal = 3–4 weeks. Above 6 weeks = demand is weaker than production. Manufacturers will have to cut wholesale, hurting near-term revenue. Below 2 weeks = high demand signal.
💰
ASP — Average Selling Price
Average revenue per vehicle sold. Rising ASP = premiumisation (customers upgrading to SUVs, higher variants). Falling ASP = discounting to clear inventory, model mix shift to cheaper segments — both have different implications.
⚡
EV Mix %
EV as % of total sales. Tata Motors now sells ~15–20% EVs in the passenger vehicle segment. Companies with rising EV mix are capturing future-ready customers and gaining first-mover advantage in charging infrastructure. A key transition metric.
📊
EBITDA per Vehicle
Operating profit earned on each vehicle sold. Maruti earns ~₹70,000–80,000 EBITDA per car. Higher ASP vehicles (SUVs, EVs) typically earn more per unit. A rising trend = margin expansion from premiumisation.
🔧
Commodity Cost %
Steel, aluminium, copper, lithium costs as % of revenue. Autos are 60–70% raw material by cost. When steel prices spike, margins compress even if sales stay strong. Commodity cycle tracking is essential for auto investors.
What Drives Growth
💳
Auto Finance & EMI Rates
Over 80% of vehicles in India are purchased on EMI. When the RBI raises rates, auto loans become more expensive, monthly EMIs rise, and buyers defer purchases. The RBI rate cycle is arguably the single biggest short-term driver of auto volumes.
⛽
Fuel Prices & EV Adoption
High petrol prices accelerate EV adoption and CNG vehicle demand. Low petrol prices reduce urgency to switch. Fuel cost per km is the primary consideration for practical Indian buyers — not range anxiety or charging infrastructure alone.
🌆
Infrastructure & Government Policy
New highway projects → more commercial vehicle demand. FAME subsidies → EV adoption. Scrappage policy → replacement cycle boost. PLI schemes → domestic component manufacturing. Government policy directly shapes the demand and supply side of Indian auto.
🚨 Red Flags
Dealer inventory above 6 weeks
Discounts rising, ASP falling QoQ
Retail sales lagging wholesale 2+ qtrs
EV market share stagnant vs peers
✅ Green Signals
Retail outpacing wholesale (demand pulling)
ASP rising — SUV and premium mix shift
Low dealer inventory, booking period rising
EBITDA per vehicle expanding alongside volumes

Sector 06 — Real Estate
Real Estate
DLF · Godrej Properties · Prestige · Oberoi Realty · Brigade — India's housing supercycle is driven by urbanisation, rising incomes, and historically low home loan rates. Pre-sales velocity and launches are the lifeblood of realty stocks.
Pre-Sales VelocityCollectionsLaunchesNet Debt/Equity
Key Performance Indicators
🏘️
Pre-Sales / Bookings
Value of units booked but not yet delivered. Leading indicator — strong pre-sales today = revenue over next 2–3 years. DLF crossed ₹7,000 Cr quarterly pre-sales — a fundamental re-rating trigger.
💰
Collections
Cash actually received from buyers. Pre-sales are recognised as revenue only on delivery. High collections = strong cash flow = debt reduction capacity. Watch collections vs pre-sales ratio.
🏗️
Launches Pipeline
New projects launched. Developers need constant launches to sustain revenue. Track unsold inventory (months of supply) — below 15 months is healthy, above 24 is oversupply danger.
📉
Net Debt / Equity
Real estate is capital-intensive. High leverage amplifies returns in upcycles but destroys equity in downturns. Target D/E below 0.5× for quality developers. Godrej Properties operates with low debt advantage.
🗺️
Land Bank Value
Locked land at historic cost vs current market value creates hidden value. Developers with large land banks in high-demand micro-markets (NCR, Bengaluru, Mumbai) sit on assets worth multiples of book value.
🔄
Inventory Turnover
How quickly completed projects sell. Rising unsold inventory + slowing new launches = demand weakness. Mumbai Metropolitan Region tracks this quarterly via PropEquity data.
What Drives Growth
📈
Residential Upcycle
Post-2021 housing demand recovery driven by work-from-home (larger homes), low interest rates, and genuine end-user demand replacing speculative investment. India is in a multi-year upcycle.
🏙️
Urbanisation + Nuclear Families
India's urban population growing 2.5% annually. Nuclear family formation from joint families expanding the addressable market — 300M urban households by 2030 target.
💸
Home Loan Interest Rates
When RBI cuts rates, EMI affordability improves and volumes surge. The 2019–2023 low-rate cycle was the single biggest driver of the real estate recovery.
🚨 Red Flags
Unsold inventory above 24 months
Pre-sales declining for 2+ quarters
Developer missing delivery timelines
RBI rate hike cycle persisting
✅ Green Signals
Pre-sales exceeding ₹5,000+ Cr quarterly
Collections growing faster than pre-sales
New launches in premium segments
Net debt declining QoQ

Sector 07 — Materials
Cement
UltraTech · Ambuja · ACC · Shree Cement · Dalmia Bharat — India is the world's second-largest cement producer. Infrastructure push and housing construction drive 7–8% annual volume growth. Every major government project starts with cement.
Volume GrowthEBITDA/tonneCapacity UtilisationFuel Cost/tonne
Key Performance Indicators
📦
Volume Growth (MT)
Million tonnes despatched. Infrastructure projects (roads, railways) and residential construction are the two demand engines. 10%+ volume growth signals strong construction activity across both.
💰
EBITDA per Tonne
Operating profit on each tonne sold. Industry benchmark: ₹1,000–1,400/tonne is healthy. UltraTech consistently targets ₹1,200+. Margin = realisation minus raw material (limestone) and energy (pet coke/coal) costs.
🏭
Capacity Utilisation
Industry utilisation above 80% = pricing power. Below 70% = excess supply, price wars. Pan-India utilisation is the most important structural indicator for cement industry profitability.
⚡
Energy Cost per Tonne
Power and fuel = 25–30% of cement production cost. Cement kilns use coal or pet coke — price swings directly impact margins. Companies with captive power plants (like Shree Cement) have structural cost advantages.
🚚
Lead Distance
Distance from plant to customer. Shorter lead distance = lower freight cost = better margins. Regional players dominate in specific geographies because freight makes long-distance delivery uneconomical.
💹
Realisation per Tonne
Selling price per tonne. Tracks cement price trends in the market. Pricing is oligopolistic in India — top 5 players control ~55% of capacity and can coordinate on pricing during demand upswings.
What Drives Growth
🏗️
Infrastructure Capex Cycle
Government's ₹11 lakh crore annual infra spend is the structural demand engine. Roads require RCC (reinforced concrete), bridges need speciality cement, metro projects are multi-year cement consumers.
🏘️
Real Estate Construction
Residential housing starts (new project launches) drive retail cement demand. The 2021–2025 housing upcycle has been a primary driver of volume growth for all cement companies.
🌿
Green Cement / Blended Cement
Rising use of fly ash and slag (industrial waste) as cement additives reduces cost and CO₂. Companies investing in blended cement capacity gain cost and sustainability advantages simultaneously.
🚨 Red Flags
Cement price cuts in peak season
Coal price spike compressing margins
Oversupply from aggressive capacity additions
Industry utilisation falling below 70%
✅ Green Signals
Cement price hikes accepted in market
Coal prices falling improving margins
Capacity utilisation above 80% pan-India
Infrastructure project awarding accelerating

Sector 08 — Base Metals
Steel
Tata Steel · JSW Steel · SAIL · JSPL · AM/NS India — India's second-largest steel producer globally. The backbone of infrastructure, auto, and defence manufacturing. Steel cycle is 3–4 years and closely tracks infrastructure capex and global demand patterns.
EBITDA/tonneHRC PriceCapacity UtilisationIron Ore Cost
Key Performance Indicators
💰
EBITDA per Tonne
Operating profit per tonne of steel. JSW Steel targets ₹8,000–10,000/tonne; higher realisations from value-added products (CRCs, galvanised sheets) vs commodity HRC improve this metric significantly.
📊
HRC vs CRC Spread
Hot-Rolled Coil vs Cold-Rolled Coil price spread. Higher value products earn 15–20% premium. Tata Steel and JSW have been increasing share of downstream, high-margin products.
⛏️
Iron Ore Self-Sufficiency
Companies with captive iron ore mines (NMDC-linked, JSPL's mines) have structural cost advantage of ₹2,000–3,000/tonne vs market purchases. NMDC as a pure-play iron ore producer benefits directly from ore price cycles.
🌍
Export Realisation vs Domestic
When domestic prices are lower, some volume exported at global parity. Export mix affects blended realisation. Track quarterly export % and global HRC vs domestic HRC spread.
🏭
Capacity Utilisation
Steel plants are capital-intensive with high fixed costs. Utilisation above 85% = cost spreading efficiently. SAIL's legacy plants have been operating below optimal — a chronic structural issue.
⚡
Coking Coal Cost
Premium hard coking coal is the largest variable cost input. India imports 85%+ of coking coal requirements from Australia, Mozambique. Coal price cycles directly drive steel sector margin volatility.
What Drives Growth
🏗️
Infrastructure Capex
India's railway modernisation, highway construction, port expansion, and smart cities all require massive volumes of structural steel. Long-term demand is structurally supported by government capex.
🚗
Auto + Consumer Demand
Flat steel (HRC/CRC/galvanised) goes into vehicles and appliances. Auto recovery cycles create demand spikes for flat steel producers like Tata Steel and JSW.
🇨🇳
China Demand and Export Cycles
China's construction slowdown floods global markets with excess steel — Indian steel companies face import competition. Monitor China housing starts and steel PMI as lead indicators.
🚨 Red Flags
China export surge compressing domestic prices
Coking coal price spike squeezing margins
Government removing import duties on steel
Capacity additions ahead of demand recovery
✅ Green Signals
China cutting steel production for environmental compliance
Iron ore prices softening alongside stable HRC
Infrastructure order awards accelerating
Anti-dumping duties on Chinese steel imports

Sector 09 — Financial Services
NBFC
Bajaj Finance · Cholamandalam · Shriram Finance · Muthoot Finance · HDFC Ltd — NBFCs serve the credit gap between banks and unbanked India. Vehicle loans, gold loans, MSME credit, microfinance — specialised lending is NBFCs' superpower.
AUM GrowthNIMGNPACost of Funds
Key Performance Indicators
📈
AUM Growth (Assets Under Management)
Total loan book outstanding. 20–25% AUM growth = strong demand for specific loan category. Bajaj Finance's 25%+ AUM growth sustained for a decade is what drove its 50× stock return.
💰
Net Interest Margin (NIM)
Lending rate minus borrowing cost. NBFCs typically earn 6–10% NIM (much higher than banks at 3–4%) because they serve higher-risk, underserved borrowers. Higher NIM = better spread economics.
⚠️
GNPA / Credit Cost
Gross NPA ratio and annual credit provisioning. NBFCs serve riskier borrowers than banks — GNPA of 2–4% is typical; above 5% triggers investor concern. Credit cost (provisions/AUM) is the most watched risk metric.
💸
Cost of Funds
Borrowing cost. NBFCs borrow via bonds, NCD, bank credit lines. When RBI raises rates, NBFCs' cost of funds rises faster than banks (no cheap deposit base). Spread compression = margin squeeze.
🔄
Collection Efficiency
% of EMI due that is actually collected. Dropped to 70–80% during COVID (moratorium) — a severe stress indicator. Above 97% = high-quality loan book. Bajaj Finance's collection efficiency is an industry benchmark.
📊
OPEX Ratio
Operating expense as % of AUM. Digital NBFCs (Bajaj Finance) have OPEX ratios of 3–4% vs traditional NBFCs at 6–8%. Lower OPEX = higher ROE. Technology investment in underwriting and collections drives OPEX efficiency.
What Drives Growth
🏠
Real Estate + Vehicle Finance
Mortgage lending (HFCs), vehicle loans (Shriram Transport), and consumer durables EMI (Bajaj Finance) — each category tied to specific macro drivers: housing starts, auto sales, consumer spending.
💳
Digital Lending + Co-Lending
NBFCs partnering with banks via co-lending allow 50:50 loan book sharing — NBFC gets fee income without full credit risk. Digital lenders using AA framework (Account Aggregator) have structural cost advantages.
💰
Gold Price for Gold Loan NBFCs
Muthoot Finance and Manappuram Finance: gold price × LTV = loan amount. Rising gold prices allow larger loan against same collateral. Gold loan NBFCs perform best when gold is appreciating.
🚨 Red Flags
RBI regulatory action on specific loan categories
Sharp rise in GNPA from segment stress
Cost of funds rising faster than lending rates
Collection efficiency declining for 2+ quarters
✅ Green Signals
Credit cost falling with improving borrower quality
AUM growth accelerating with new loan categories
Co-lending partnerships expanding loan book
Gold prices rising (for gold loan NBFCs)

Sector 10 — Financial Services
Insurance
LIC · HDFC Life · SBI Life · ICICI Prudential · Star Health · New India Assurance — India's life and general insurance penetration is among the lowest in the world at 3–4% of GDP vs global average of 7%. The runway for growth is multi-decade.
VNB MarginPersistency RatioGWP GrowthLoss Ratio
Key Performance Indicators
💰
VNB — Value of New Business
Present value of future profits from new policies written. VNB Margin = VNB / Annualised Premium Equivalent (APE). HDFC Life targets 25–27% VNB margin — the key profitability metric in life insurance.
🔄
Persistency Ratio
% of policyholders renewing (paying premiums in year 2, 3, 5, 13). 61st month persistency above 55% = high-quality business. Low persistency = policies lapsing = earned premium disappears = VNB destroyed.
📈
GWP Growth — Gross Written Premium
Total premium income. 15–20% GWP growth for private life insurers in India = strong demand for term + ULIP products. General insurance: motor + health driving growth.
⚕️
Loss Ratio (General Insurance)
Claims paid / premiums earned. Star Health targets 65–70% loss ratio — above 80% means the company is paying out more than it earns. Claims inflation in health insurance is the key risk.
🏢
OPEX / Premium Ratio
Distribution and admin costs as % of premium. Companies with strong bancassurance (bank-insurance) partnerships (SBI Life via SBI branches) have lower customer acquisition costs = better margins.
💼
Embedded Value (EV)
Actuarial estimate of value already in force policies + adjusted net worth. P/EV ratio is the primary valuation metric for life insurers. Premium P/EV = higher quality of business / growth expectation.
What Drives Growth
🌱
Rising Financial Awareness
Pandemic accelerated term insurance uptake dramatically. Young India is buying financial protection products for the first time. Digital distribution via apps has reduced friction significantly.
🏥
Health Insurance Upcycle
Medical inflation running at 12–14% annually. Rising healthcare costs making health insurance a necessity, not a luxury. Star Health, Niva Bupa, ICICI Lombard Health seeing structural demand growth.
📱
Digital + Bancassurance Growth
Paytm, PolicyBazaar, and fintech aggregators driving online term insurance sales at lower distribution costs. Bancassurance channels (bank branch networks) remain the largest premium volume generators.
🚨 Red Flags
Persistency ratios falling (policy lapses rising)
Loss ratio above 80% for health insurers
Regulatory changes in product design cutting margins
Agent productivity declining
✅ Green Signals
VNB margin expansion from product mix shift
Persistency ratios improving year-on-year
Health insurance claim costs normalising
New product launches in protection segment

Sector 11 — Communications
Telecom
Reliance Jio · Bharti Airtel · BSNL · Vodafone Idea — India's telecom industry went through the most disruptive consolidation in global history (2016–2020) reducing from 10 players to effectively 3. Jio's pricing revolution forced massive re-investment in data infrastructure.
ARPUData Usage/Sub4G/5G Sub %EBITDA Margin
Key Performance Indicators
📱
ARPU — Average Revenue per User
Monthly revenue per subscriber. India's ARPU at ₹180–200 is among the lowest globally (China: ₹500, US: ₹4,000). Airtel's premium strategy targets ₹300+ ARPU — each ₹10 ARPU increase = ~₹600 Cr annual revenue for Airtel.
📊
Data Usage per Subscriber
GB consumed per user per month. India averages 25–30 GB/month — among highest globally due to cheap data. Rising usage demands constant network capacity expansion (investment trigger).
🌐
5G Subscriber Penetration
% of subscribers on 5G network. Airtel and Jio rolled out 5G in 2022–23. 5G enables premium enterprise (B2B) services at higher margins. Track 5G SIM upgrades and enterprise use-case announcements quarterly.

🧠 Memory Tricks

Remember Sector KPIs Instantly

Six mnemonics to lock in the critical KPIs for India’s five most-invested sectors — Banking, IT, FMCG, Pharma, and Auto — so you can analyse any quarterly result in under 10 minutes.

🏛
Banking KPIs · Acronym Trick
GNAC — GNPA, NIM, CASA, Advances growth — the 4 numbers every bank investor tracks
"Great NIMs Always Compound" — GNPA, NIM, Advances growth, CASA ratio — four banking KPIs that tell you everything.
G— GNPA % (Gross Non-Performing Assets): Bad loans as % of total loans. Below 2% = excellent asset quality (HDFC Bank historic: <1.5%). Above 5% = stress. Rising GNPA trend = danger signal.
N— NIM % (Net Interest Margin): Spread between lending rate and deposit rate. Above 4% = strong pricing power (HDFC Bank: 4.1%). Below 3% = squeezed (PSU banks: 3–3.5%). Rising NIM + rising CASA = best combination.
A— Advances Growth: Loan book growth rate. 15–20%+ CAGR = growth phase. Industry average 12–14% = maturity. Below GDP growth = losing market share. Watch: is growth in good-quality loans (retail/corporate) or risky segments (unsecured)?
C— CASA Ratio: Current Account + Savings Account deposits as % of total deposits. High CASA (45%+) = low funding cost = competitive advantage. HDFC Bank CASA: ~44%. Kotak Bank: ~50%. PSU banks: 40–45%. CASA is sticky, low-cost funding.
💡 5-minute bank analysis: Check GNAC every quarter. GNPA falling + NIM rising + Advances growing 15%+ + CASA stable/rising = buy signal. Any one deteriorating: investigate. All four deteriorating: exit. HDFC Bank has maintained all four positively for 30 consecutive years — the definition of a great bank.
💻
IT Services KPIs · Acronym Trick
TDAU — TCV, Deal wins, Attrition, Utilisation — leading vs lagging indicators
"Top Deals Always Utilise talent" — TCV (Total Contract Value), Deal wins, Attrition, Utilisation rate.
T— TCV (Total Contract Value): Total value of contracts won in the quarter. Leading indicator: strong TCV today = revenue 1–3 quarters ahead. TCS quarterly TCV: $7–10 billion range. Infosys: $3–5 billion. Rising TCV = pipeline building. TCV > LTM revenue/4 = backlog improving.
D— Deal Wins: Number and size of new client wins. Large deals (>$50M TCV) signal competitiveness. Mega deals (>$500M) signal vendor consolidation wins. Wipro winning a $1B deal = significant market share capture from a competitor.
A— Attrition Rate: Annual employee turnover %. Below 12% = healthy. Above 20% = talent crisis (cost and delivery risk). Indian IT attrition spiked to 25–30% in 2021–22 (tech boom) → margins compressed → stocks fell 30–40%. Leading margin pressure indicator.
U— Utilisation Rate: % of billable employees deployed on projects. Above 82–85% = efficient. Below 75% = too many bench employees (margin drag). Rising utilisation + falling attrition = best IT margin recovery signal.
💡 IT quarterly results cheat sheet: Strong quarter = TCV up QoQ + large deal wins + attrition falling below 15% + utilisation rising above 82%. Weak quarter = TCV declining + attrition above 20% + rising subcontracting costs (hiring external contractors to cover attrition). TCV is the most important leading indicator — revenue follows it with 1–3 quarter lag.
🍔
FMCG KPIs · Acronym Trick
VPGM — Volume growth, Price growth, Gross margin, Market share
"Very Profitable Growth Matters" — Volume, Price, Gross margin, Market share — the four FMCG numbers that reveal the business quality.
V— Volume Growth: Real unit growth excluding price increases. Positive volume = genuine demand growth. Negative volume with positive revenue = price-driven only (potential volume elasticity risk). HUL target: 4–6% volume growth + 3–4% price = 7–10% revenue growth.
P— Pricing Power: % of revenue growth from price increases. Consistent price hikes without volume loss = moat. If volumes fall >2% when price raised = weak brand. Nestle India 2022–23: raised Maggi price 15%, volumes fell only 1% = exceptional pricing power.
G— Gross Margin %: Revenue minus raw material cost. Above 50% = strong moat (HUL, Nestle). Falling gross margin = commodity inflation squeezing pricing power. Watch: palm oil, wheat, milk for FMCG cost pressure signals.
M— Market Share: Urban vs rural market share trend. Rural India = 40%+ of FMCG revenues. Rising rural share = distribution expanding. Losing urban share to private labels = premium moat erosion signal.
💡 FMCG quarterly cheat sheet: Strong = Volume growth 4%+ + Gross margin expanding (raw material cooling) + Rural share improving + market share stable/gaining. Weak = Volume declining + Gross margin contracting (commodity spike) + losing market share in core categories. Key: Volume growth of even 3–4% in a mature category like toothpaste or soap = excellent for a mature brand with 50%+ market share.
💊
Pharma KPIs · Acronym Trick
UFAR — US FDA approvals, Field force productivity, ANDA pipeline, R&D as % of revenue
"US Force Always Requires investment" — US FDA status, Field force (domestic), ANDA pipeline, R&D spend.
U— US FDA Plant Status: Export-facing Indian pharma plants must be FDA-approved. Warning letter or import alert = immediate revenue risk (35–50% of revenue for many companies). Sun Pharma Halol Warning Letter (2014): stock fell 30%. Most critical single risk factor for export pharma.
F— Field Force Productivity: Revenue per medical representative. India domestic pharma = 60–70% field-force driven. Improving MR productivity = better prescription market penetration. Key for domestic pure-plays (Torrent, Ajanta, Abbott India).
A— ANDA Pipeline: Abbreviated New Drug Applications filed with US FDA. Pipeline of 150+ ANDAs (Dr Reddy’s, Sun Pharma) = multi-year revenue visibility. First-to-file ANDAs (exclusive 180-day window) = highest-value opportunity for US-focused pharma.
R— R&D as % of Revenue: Investment in future products. Above 8–10% = innovating for complex generics/specialty. Below 5% = maintaining current portfolio only. Sun Pharma R&D: 8–10%. Dr Reddy’s: 10%+. Rising R&D spend = future earnings visibility.
💡 Pharma red flag checklist: US FDA Warning Letter (stop buying, investigate). US price erosion above 10% for core products (generic competition intensifying). Field force productivity declining for 3+ quarters (doctor switching to competitor drugs). R&D falling below 5% (no investment in future pipeline = eventual earnings decline). Any two red flags = exit.

Auto KPIs · Acronym Trick
WERM — Wholesale volumes, Export mix, Realisations per vehicle, Market share
"Wheels Export Revenue Matter" — Wholesale volumes, Export mix, Realisations (ASP), Market share.
W— Wholesale vs Retail Volumes: Wholesale = factory dispatch to dealers. Retail = actual sales to customers. Wholesale > Retail = channel stuffing (unsold dealer inventory building). Wholesale < Retail = demand exceeding supply = positive. SIAM monthly data shows both.
E— Export Mix %: Revenue from exports as % of total. Bajaj Auto exports 35%+ of two-wheelers → INR depreciation = natural hedge. TVS Motor exports growing in Africa and South Asia. Rising export mix = global competitiveness signal.
R— Realisations (ASP — Average Selling Price): Revenue per vehicle sold. Rising ASP = premiumisation trend. Maruti shifting from Alto to Baleno, Brezza, Grand Vitara = ASP rising from ₹4.5L to ₹7L+ = revenue growing faster than volumes.
M— Market Share by Category: SUV vs sedan vs hatchback market share. Premium 2W vs commuter 2W. CV: Tata Motors vs Ashok Leyland market share in MHCV. Winning share in premium categories = better margins + brand strength.
💡 Auto cycle signal: Channel inventory (wholesale vs retail gap) is the leading indicator for OEM margins. When dealer inventory exceeds 5–6 weeks, OEMs cut production → margins fall. When inventory below 3–4 weeks, OEMs increase production → operating leverage kicks in → margins expand. Society of Indian Automobile Manufacturers (SIAM) monthly data = free 2-minute channel check.

📚 Glossary

Key Terms Glossary

Every sector-specific KPI term — GNPA, NIM, CASA, TCV, ANDA, ASP, utilisation rate — defined with India sector examples for all 25 sectors covered.

🔍
GNPA (Gross Non-Performing Assets)
Banking
Gross Non-Performing Assets as a percentage of the total loan book. A loan becomes NPA when interest or principal is overdue for 90+ days (RBI guidelines). GNPA % is the single most important asset quality indicator for banks and NBFCs. Below 1.5% = excellent (HDFC Bank: consistently 1–1.4%). 2–4% = acceptable. Above 5% = stressed asset quality (PSU banks during 2016–2019 NPA crisis: 10–15% GNPA). Rising GNPA is more concerning than the absolute level — a stable 3% GNPA is better than rising from 1% to 3%.
💡 GNPA monitoring: Check quarterly results for: (1) Absolute GNPA in ₹crore trend. (2) GNPA % trend (rising = risk, falling = improving). (3) Slippage ratio (fresh NPA additions as % of loans) — leading indicator. (4) Coverage ratio (provisions as % of GNPA) — a high coverage ratio (75%+) means the bank has already "provided for" most bad loans, reducing future P&L impact. HDFC Bank’s 20-year average GNPA: 1.2% — the benchmark for Indian banking quality.
NIM (Net Interest Margin)
Banking
Net Interest Margin = Net Interest Income ÷ Average Interest-Earning Assets. Represents the spread between the rate a bank earns on loans and the rate it pays on deposits. NIM is the primary driver of banking profitability. Above 4% = strong competitive positioning (HDFC Bank: 4.1%, Kotak Bank: 4.8%). Below 3% = squeezed (PSU banks: 2.8–3.2%). NIM is primarily driven by CASA ratio (higher CASA = lower funding cost = higher NIM) and loan mix (retail/SME loans typically yield higher than corporate loans).
💡 NIM vs CASA connection: HDFC Bank's 44% CASA ratio means 44% of deposits are zero-cost (current account) or near-zero-cost (savings at 3%). This low-cost funding base vs lending at 8–12% (retail loans) creates 4.1% NIM. PSU banks with 38% CASA and higher fixed deposit dependence earn only 3% NIM. This NIM gap × loan book size = hundreds of crores of additional profit annually for HDFC Bank — the compounding power of a CASA moat.
TCV (Total Contract Value)
IT Services
The total value of all contracts signed in a reporting period (typically a quarter), expressed in USD. TCV is the most important leading indicator for IT services revenue because contracts typically run 3–5 years, with revenue recognised over the contract term. Strong TCV = healthy revenue pipeline for the next 2–6 quarters. TCV includes new client wins, renewals, and scope expansions. TCS quarterly TCV: $7–10B range (2022–2024). Infosys: $3–5B. Rising TCV QoQ across 2–3 quarters = revenue growth acceleration ahead. Declining TCV for 2+ quarters = revenue growth slowdown coming.
💡 TCV vs Revenue lag: Infosys wins a $2B, 5-year infrastructure management contract in Q2. Revenue recognition: $400M/year = $100M/quarter. This TCV win shows up in revenue over 20 quarters starting Q3. This is why TCV is a leading indicator — management and analysts watch it as the best forward-looking demand signal for the next 6–18 months of revenue growth.
Attrition Rate (IT Services)
IT Services
Annual employee turnover as a percentage of total workforce. The most important cost and quality risk indicator for IT services companies. High attrition (20%+) creates: (1) Increased hiring and training costs, (2) Project delivery risk (experienced staff replaced by fresher), (3) Client satisfaction risk (relationship managers leaving = client churn risk), (4) Subcontractor cost increase (hiring temp workers at premium rates). The 2021–2022 India IT attrition surge (25–30%) driven by the US tech hiring boom compressed EBITDA margins by 3–5% across the sector and contributed to IT stocks declining 30–40% despite record revenues.
💡 Attrition cycle (2021–2024): FY22 attrition: Infosys 28.4%, TCS 19.7%, Wipro 23.8% — peak of talent war. FY24: Infosys 12.9%, TCS 12.5%, Wipro 14.2% — normalisation. Margin recovery: TCS EBIT margin 23% (FY22) → 25%+ (FY24) as attrition normalised. Attrition falling below 15% = margin recovery signal. Always check attrition in IT concalls — it's the most forward-looking margin indicator the company provides.
Volume Growth vs Price Growth (FMCG)
FMCG
FMCG revenue growth is decomposed into: Volume growth (real unit sales increase) + Price/Mix growth (higher prices or premium product mix). Volume growth is the "real" growth — it reflects actual consumption increase. Price growth inflates revenue but may not be sustainable if consumers downsize or switch to cheaper alternatives. Ideal FMCG growth: 4–6% volume + 3–4% price/mix = 7–10% total revenue growth. Red flag: consistently negative volume with positive revenue (only price-driven) = brand losing real market share.
💡 HUL volume growth context: HUL FY24: Revenue growth 2% with volume growth slightly negative — raw material inflation forced price hikes that constrained volumes. Competitors gaining share in some categories. This is the challenge of mature FMCG in an inflationary environment. Compare: Nestlé India: consistent 5–8% volume growth even while raising prices = stronger brand pricing power. Volume growth is the ultimate test of brand health in FMCG.
ANDA (Abbreviated New Drug Application)
Pharma
An application filed with the US FDA to produce and market a generic version of an approved brand-name drug. The ANDA process: File → FDA reviews bioequivalence → Approval → Launch. First-to-file (Para IV ANDA): The first company to file a generic challenge on a patented drug gets 180 days of exclusive generic marketing — typically generating 70–80% of the brand price at full volume. This exclusivity period can generate $200M–$1B+ in revenue for a single drug. Large ANDA pipelines (Sun Pharma: 700+, Dr Reddy’s: 150+) = multi-year US revenue visibility and optionality.
💡 ANDA first-to-file value example: Lupin won first-to-file status for generic Glumetza (diabetes drug) — 180-day exclusive generic. Revenue generated in 6-month window: $300M+ (at ~70% of brand price). For context: Lupin's total annual US revenue at the time was ~$800M. A single first-to-file ANDA = 37% incremental annual revenue in 6 months. This is why analysts track ANDA first-to-file filings obsessively in pharma research.
Wholesale vs Retail Gap (Auto)
Auto
The difference between units dispatched by OEMs to dealers (wholesale) and units actually sold to end customers (retail). A key leading indicator of OEM production and margin cycles. Wholesale > Retail = channel inventory building (dealers over-stocked) = OEM must cut production = margin pressure (lower operating leverage). Wholesale < Retail = channel de-stocking (dealer inventory below 4 weeks) = OEM must increase production = positive operating leverage = margin expansion. SIAM publishes monthly wholesale and retail data for all vehicle categories. The gap between the two tells the next quarter’s OEM production story.
💡 Maruti Suzuki inventory example: FY24 mid-year: Dealer inventory fell to 3 weeks (tight supply). Maruti increased production 15% QoQ to replenish channel. Plant utilisation rose 85%→92% → operating leverage kicked in → EBITDA margins expanded 150 bps in the next quarter. The channel inventory data (free from SIAM monthly reports) gave 2-3 months advance warning of margin expansion — before it appeared in quarterly results.
PLF / Capacity Utilisation (Power & Energy)
Energy
Plant Load Factor (PLF) for thermal power: actual generation ÷ maximum possible generation. Higher PLF = better asset utilisation = lower per-unit cost. Capacity Utilisation for renewable energy: equivalent to PLF. Solar plants typically achieve 20–25% CUF (Capacity Utilisation Factor) due to intermittency. Wind: 25–35% CUF. For renewable energy companies (Adani Green, NTPC Renewables, Greenko), MW Commissioned is the primary growth metric — more MW = more revenue regardless of CUF, since power is sold under 25-year PPAs (Power Purchase Agreements) at fixed rates. LCOE (Levelised Cost of Energy) falling = renewable competitiveness improving vs thermal.
💡 Adani Green KPIs: MW commissioned (operational portfolio) = primary revenue driver. Energy sold (billion units) = revenue confirmation. EBITDA/MW = profitability efficiency. Debt/EBITDA <6x = sustainable leverage for PPP-backed projects. Adani Green commissioned 10,934 MW by FY24 vs 5,410 MW in FY22 = 2× growth = 2× energy generation = ~2× revenue. MW commissioned is the simplest metric for tracking renewable energy company growth.
