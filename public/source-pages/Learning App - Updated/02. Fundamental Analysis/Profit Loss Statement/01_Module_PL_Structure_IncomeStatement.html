01 — Key Learning Outcome

The P&L is a Company's Income Story

📊
Your Core Takeaway
A Profit & Loss (P&L) statement — also called the Income Statement — shows a company's Revenue, all its Expenses, and the resulting Net Profit (or loss) over a specific period, typically one financial year.
Think of it like your monthly salary slip. You earn a salary (Revenue), pay rent, EMIs, food and bills (Expenses), and whatever remains is your savings (Net Profit). For a company, this happens at a massive scale — from thousands of crores of sales down to the final rupee retained for shareholders. Unlike the Balance Sheet (which is a snapshot on a specific date), the P&L covers a time period — from April 1 to March 31 in India. It answers the most fundamental business question: "Is this company actually making money?" Revenue is called the Top Line. Net Profit is called the Bottom Line. Everything in between tells you how efficiently the company converts its sales into profit — and that efficiency is what separates great businesses from mediocre ones.

02B — Every Line Item Explained

Revenue, Costs & Profit — Each Row Decoded

Now that you've seen how the flow works, here's what each row actually represents in real business terms — with a plain-English explanation anyone can understand.

🏭 The P&L converts Revenue into Profit — every cost layer is stripped out until the final Net Profit remains for shareholders.
1

Revenue / Sales — The Top Line

Total income from selling products or services. Everything starts here. Growing revenue over 10 years is the first signal of a good business. On Screener.in it appears as "Sales +".

2

Operating Expenses — The Cost Engine

Raw materials, employee costs, manufacturing overheads. Subtracted from revenue to get EBITDA. Lower expenses relative to revenue = higher OPM%. Should grow slower than revenue.

3

Operating Profit & OPM% — Core Efficiency

Operating Profit ÷ Revenue × 100 = OPM%. The purest efficiency metric. A 50%+ OPM% for 10+ years signals extraordinary pricing power. CDSL's OPM never fell below 40% in 12 years.

4

Other Income, Depreciation & Interest

Other Income = non-core earnings (FD interest, dividends). Depreciation = non-cash asset cost. Interest = debt cost. Zero interest for 12 years = debt-free = strongest P&L signal possible.

5

Profit Before Tax (PBT) — Pre-Government

EBITDA + Other Income − Depreciation − Interest. Government taxes this. Effective tax rate should stay near 25–30%. Unusually low rates may not repeat. PBT trend matters more than any single year.

6

Net Profit (PAT) + EPS — The Bottom Line

PBT minus tax. This is what belongs to shareholders. Growing PAT at 15%+ CAGR over 10 years = compounding machine. EPS must grow with PAT — if it doesn't, shares are being diluted.

🚀

Revenue Grew 10× in 11 Years — From ₹105 Cr (FY15) to ₹1,145 Cr (FY26)

A 10-fold revenue increase over 11 years implies a CAGR of approximately 25%. This isn't a fluke — it's the compounding effect of India's demat account boom, with CDSL registering record account openings every year since 2020. More importantly, profits grew even faster than revenue (PAT: 43 Cr → 526 Cr = 12× growth), indicating improving operating leverage where costs grow slower than revenue. This is the ideal pattern: accelerating profitability relative to scale.

⚙️

OPM% Consistently Above 40% — Peak at 66% in FY22, Never Below 40% in 12 Years

Operating margins staying above 40% for 12 consecutive years is rare in any industry. CDSL's business model (collecting depository and transaction fees on a regulated monopoly-like infrastructure) means its cost structure is largely fixed. As revenue scales, margins expand — this is called operating leverage. The FY20 dip to 40% coincided with COVID-related lower market activity (fewer transactions). The recovery to 62% in FY21 and 66% in FY22 was driven by the massive retail investor surge. The FY26 dip to 51% reflects rising staff and technology costs as CDSL expands capacity — worth monitoring.

🏦

Interest = ₹0 Across All 12 Years — Debt-Free P&L Since Inception

Not a single rupee of interest expense in 12 years is extraordinary. This means CDSL has never borrowed to fund its operations — it is a self-financing, capital-light business. Every rupee of operating profit remains available to shareholders. There is no lender taking a cut. This dramatically de-risks the business in downturns: no debt covenants, no repayment pressure, no refinancing risk. A P&L with zero interest over a decade is one of the rarest and most valuable signals an investor can find.

📈

EPS Grew from ₹2.09 (FY15) to ₹25.20 (FY25) — 12× Growth Per Share

EPS (Earnings Per Share) is Net Profit divided by total shares. Growing EPS means each share is earning more every year — this is what drives long-term stock price appreciation. CDSL's EPS went from ₹2.09 to ₹25.20 in 10 years — a 12× jump. This happened without significant share dilution (share capital was stable at ₹104 Cr for years before a bonus issue). Growing EPS + stable shares = the investor's dream. The FY26 EPS fell to ₹21.82 due to lower net profit (₹455 Cr vs ₹526 Cr), reflecting a rare dip worth watching in FY27.

05 — What to Look for in Any P&L

3 Things Every Investor Should Check First

You don't need to read every line. These three checks take under 2 minutes and give you 80% of the insight — whether you're looking at a banking giant or a small consumer brand.

01
📈
Revenue · Top Line · 10-Year View
Is Revenue Growing Consistently?
Open Screener.in → search any company → click P&L → look at the Sales row across 10 years. Is it growing every year? Is the CAGR above 12–15%? Consistent revenue growth is the most reliable foundation of any investment thesis. Flat or declining revenue — even with improving margins — signals a business in trouble.

02

OPM% · Efficiency · Pricing Power
Is OPM% Stable or Improving?

10 — Advanced Analysis

Pro Tips for P&L Analysis

What experienced investors actually check when they open Screener.in — beyond the basics.

1
Look at 10-Year Trends, Not Just 1 Year
One good year means nothing. A company growing profits for 10 consecutive years has a durable competitive advantage. Screener.in shows 12 years — use all of it.
📅 10-Year Rule
2
Compare OPM% to Industry Peers
A 25% OPM in FMCG is great. In commodity steel, it's exceptional. Always benchmark against the industry average before judging a margin.
⚙️ Peer Benchmark
3
Sales Growth + Profit Growth = Power Combo
Revenue growing at 20% and profit at 25% means the company is getting more efficient as it scales. This "operating leverage" creates massive long-term value.
📈 Operating Leverage
4
Other Income Should Be Less Than 10% of PBT
If "Other Income" forms more than 10% of Profit Before Tax, the business is not self-sufficient. Core operations should generate the majority of profits.
💼 Quality Check
5
Match Depreciation with Capex
Rising depreciation means rising asset base. Check if the company's capital expenditure (in Cash Flow) justifies rising depreciation — it signals future growth capacity.
🏚️ Asset Quality
6
Tax Rate Should Be Close to 25–30%
An unusually low effective tax rate (below 15%) could mean aggressive deductions or one-time tax benefits that won't repeat. Normalize for tax before comparing companies.
🏛️ Tax Normalise
