01 — Key Learning Outcome

Compute and Interpret Earnings Per Share

📊
Your Core Takeaway
EPS is the single most important number connecting a company's profit to each individual share you own. If TCS earns ₹49,454 Cr PAT and has 366 Cr shares outstanding, each share "earned" ₹135. That ₹135 is the Basic EPS. But there are two complications: (1) the company may have given ESOPs (stock options) to employees that haven't been exercised yet — if they were all exercised, share count would rise and EPS would fall. This is Diluted EPS. (2) the PAT may include one-time exceptional items — strip those out and you get Adjusted EPS, the most reliable basis for the P/E ratio calculation used in valuation.
Think of it this way: you own a pizza restaurant that earned ₹1,00,000 this year. You split it 100 ways (100 shares). Each share earned ₹1,000 — that's Basic EPS. But you promised your chef 10 extra shares if he stays for 5 years. If he exercises them, the pizza is split 110 ways and each share earns only ₹909 — that's Diluted EPS. And if ₹20,000 of the ₹1,00,000 was a one-time insurance payout, the real recurring EPS is only ₹727 — that's Adjusted EPS. Which number you use changes the P/E ratio and therefore the valuation conclusion dramatically.
📊 Basic EPS = PAT ÷ Weighted Avg Shares ⚠️ Diluted EPS includes ESOP dilution ✅ Adjusted EPS strips one-time items 📋 Always use Diluted for P/E ratio 🚨 Rising EPS via buybacks ≠ business growth

02 — The Three EPS Variants

Basic · Diluted · Adjusted Decoded

Each of the three EPS variants answers a different question. Using the wrong one for valuation leads to systematically wrong conclusions about whether a stock is cheap or expensive.

📐
Basic EPS
The Starting Point
PAT (Net Profit)
÷
Weighted Avg Shares Outstanding
The simplest version. Take the year's net profit (PAT) and divide by the weighted average number of shares outstanding during the year. "Weighted average" means if shares changed mid-year, each count is weighted by the time it was outstanding. This is what Screener.in shows as "EPS" in the P&L section. Use this for trend analysis but not for P/E valuation.
🔍
Diluted EPS
The Conservative View
PAT + (ESOP interest saved, if any)
÷
Shares + All Potential Dilutive Shares
Assumes all employee stock options (ESOPs), convertible bonds, warrants, and other dilutive instruments are exercised right now. This gives the worst-case EPS if every potential share were issued. For IT companies with large ESOP grants (Infosys, Wipro), diluted EPS can be meaningfully lower than basic EPS. Always use Diluted EPS to calculate the P/E ratio — it's the standard used by professional analysts and on Screener.in's valuation section.
🎯
Adjusted EPS
The True Recurring Power
(PAT − After-tax exceptional items)
÷
Diluted Weighted Avg Shares
Strip all one-time exceptional gains or losses from PAT before dividing. This shows recurring, sustainable EPS — what the business will likely earn next year. Use Adjusted EPS for P/E based valuation. A company with ₹50 Basic EPS but ₹35 Adjusted EPS (₹15 from asset sales) should be valued on the ₹35, not ₹50 — otherwise you're overpaying for earnings that won't repeat.

🍕 The same ₹1,000 Cr PAT gives very different EPS depending on which share count you use and whether you strip one-time items. The P/E ratio changes dramatically based on which EPS you use as the denominator.

1

What "Weighted Average Shares" Means — and Why It Matters

If a company had 100 Cr shares for 9 months and issued 20 Cr new shares in October (3 months before year end), the weighted average = (100 × 9/12) + (120 × 3/12) = 75 + 30 = 105 Cr shares. Dividing PAT by 105 (not 120) gives a fairer picture of per-share earnings over the full year. On Screener.in, EPS is always calculated using weighted average shares — you don't need to compute this manually.

2

Why ESOP Dilution Matters for IT Companies Especially

Infosys, Wipro, and HCL Technologies grant millions of stock options to employees every year. When these ESOPs are exercised, existing shareholders' ownership percentage shrinks — and so does per-share earnings. The difference between Basic and Diluted EPS directly measures how much ESOP dilution is eating into your returns. If Basic EPS = ₹120 but Diluted EPS = ₹110, the 8.3% gap represents wealth being transferred from shareholders to employees.

3

How Buybacks Inflate EPS Without Growing the Business

When a company buys back its own shares, total shares outstanding falls. Even if PAT stays flat, EPS rises mechanically. Example: PAT stays ₹1,000 Cr. Shares fall from 100 Cr to 80 Cr. EPS rises from ₹10 to ₹12.50 — a 25% jump with zero business improvement. This is why tracking EPS alone can be misleading. Always track revenue and PAT growth alongside EPS growth to identify whether EPS growth comes from the business or share count manipulation.

4

Which EPS to Use for the P/E Ratio

The P/E (Price-to-Earnings) ratio = Share Price ÷ EPS. For this calculation, always use Adjusted Diluted EPS: (1) Diluted because it accounts for all potential share dilution, (2) Adjusted because it removes one-time items. If you use Basic EPS, you overstate the real earning power per share. If you use PAT with exceptional items included, you compare against inflated earnings that won't repeat — making the stock appear cheap when it isn't.

Satyam Computer — Fabricated EPS (Historical)
IT Services · Historical · Fake PAT inflated EPS for 10 years · 2009 fraud exposed
🚨 Fictitious PAT → fictitious EPS 🚨 CFO/PAT ratio was the warning sign 🚨 EPS collapsed to zero overnight
🚨 Satyam is the extreme case — when PAT is fraudulent, so is every EPS figure derived from it. For nearly a decade, Satyam reported EPS that showed steady ~20% annual growth — making it look like a high-quality compounder. The EPS was derived from inflated PAT built on fictitious bank balances and fabricated customer contracts. The cross-check that could have flagged it: despite reporting ~20% PAT margins and strong EPS, Cash from Operations (CFO) was consistently lower than PAT — the fictional profits weren't converting to real cash. The rule: always validate EPS with CFO/PAT ratio. If CFO is systematically below PAT for 3+ years, the EPS may not reflect real earnings.

2
Diluted EPS → Annual Report → Notes to Accounts → "EPS" note → Diluted EPS disclosed
Screener.in shows Basic EPS. For Diluted EPS, open the company's Annual Report → Notes to Accounts → find the "Earnings Per Share" note. It must disclose both Basic and Diluted EPS under Ind AS 33. Calculate the dilution gap = (Basic EPS − Diluted EPS) ÷ Basic EPS × 100. Above 3% gap = significant ESOP program eating shareholder returns.
Diluted EPS= PAT ÷ Weighted Avg Shares + Dilutive ESOP/Warrants
Basic ≈ Diluted (gap <0.5%) = very few ESOPs ✅ Gap 1–3% = moderate ESOP program ⚠️ Gap above 5% = heavy dilution, adjust P/E accordingly 🚨
3
Adjusted EPS → Strip exceptional items from PAT → Recalculate EPS on clean earnings
If the company has Exceptional Items in the P&L (visible on Screener.in between EBIT and PBT), calculate Adjusted EPS: (PAT − After-tax exceptional) ÷ Diluted shares. This is the earnings per share that will realistically repeat next year. Use this number — not the Screener.in Basic EPS — for P/E valuation whenever exceptional items are present.
Adjusted EPS= (PAT − Exceptional × 0.75) ÷ Diluted Shares
No exceptional items = Adjusted = Diluted EPS exactly ✅ Large exceptional gain → Adjusted EPS much lower → P/E actually higher 🚨
💡 Quick rule — 25% tax retained
Use effective tax rate ~25% for most Indian companies. Exceptional Item of ₹100 Cr → after-tax impact = ₹75 Cr. So if PAT = ₹1,000 Cr including ₹100 Cr exceptional gain, Adjusted PAT = ₹925 Cr. Divide by diluted shares to get Adjusted EPS.
4
Shares check → Has share count changed significantly? → Buyback ↓ = good · New issue ↑ = investigate
Find "Shares Outstanding" on Screener.in's quarterly data or the balance sheet. Compare it over 5 years. If share count is declining → company is buying back shares (good for EPS, check if using free cash or debt). If share count is rising → dilution from ESOPs, QIP, or rights issue. EPS growth from share count decline is financial engineering; EPS growth from PAT growth is genuine business compounding.
✅ Share count falling + PAT growing = double compounding. EPS grows from both sides. Best scenario — TCS, Nestlé are perfect examples.
🚨 Share count rising sharply + PAT flat = dilution destroying EPS per share despite flat PAT. Most dangerous when management uses Basic EPS (hiding dilution).
5
Final check → P/E using Adjusted Diluted EPS → Is P/E justified by EPS growth rate?
Calculate P/E = Share Price ÷ Adjusted Diluted EPS. Then compare to the 10-year EPS CAGR. A rough rule: fair P/E ≈ EPS CAGR × 1.5 (the "PEG ratio" benchmark). TCS with 14% EPS CAGR → fair P/E ≈ 21×. If trading at 28×, it's premium-priced but not extreme. If a company with 3% EPS CAGR trades at 35× P/E → dramatically overvalued relative to growth.
✅ P/E divided by EPS CAGR (PEG ratio) below 1.5× = reasonable valuation for growth. Below 1.0× = potentially undervalued if business quality is high.
🚨 P/E divided by EPS CAGR above 3× = paying very heavily for growth. Requires perfect execution for the next 5+ years to justify the price. High risk of disappointment.
⚡ 4 Bonus Quick Tricks — 10 Seconds Each
A
The "EPS vs Revenue" Consistency Test
Compare 10-year Revenue CAGR vs EPS CAGR on Screener.in. If EPS CAGR is significantly higher than Revenue CAGR, ask: how? Either margins expanded (great) or share count fell via buybacks (check). If EPS CAGR is far below Revenue CAGR, margins are being squeezed — revenues growing but profits per share not keeping up. Ideal: Revenue CAGR 12% + Margin expansion → EPS CAGR 17%+ is genuinely valuable compounding.
B
The Buyback EPS Inflation Detector
Check "Shares Outstanding" trend on Screener.in. If share count is falling 2-3% per year while EPS grows 10%+, subtract the buyback effect: true business EPS growth = EPS growth − share count reduction. Example: EPS grew 12%, share count fell 3% → actual business growth = ~9%. This is still good, but knowing the source of EPS growth is critical for predicting future trends.
C
The CFO/PAT → EPS Quality Validator
After reading EPS, go to Cash Flow tab → Cash from Operations (CFO). Calculate CFO ÷ PAT. Above 0.85× = EPS is backed by real cash — quality signal. Below 0.5× for 3+ years = PAT (and therefore EPS) may be inflated by accruals, channel stuffing, or aggressive revenue recognition. Real EPS and cash EPS should track closely.
D
The PEG Ratio Quick Screen
PEG (Price/Earnings to Growth) = P/E ÷ EPS CAGR (in %). A PEG below 1.0 = stock may be undervalued relative to growth. PEG 1.0–1.5 = fairly priced. PEG above 2.0 = expensive — priced for perfection. For TCS: P/E ~28 ÷ EPS CAGR ~14 = PEG 2.0 (premium). For a high-growth small-cap: P/E 25 ÷ EPS CAGR 30 = PEG 0.83 (potentially undervalued). Quick PEG screen is the fastest valuation sanity check available.

Basic EPSPAT divided by weighted average shares outstanding during the year. The simplest form of EPS. Shown directly on Screener.in's P&L tab. Useful for long-term trend analysis but does not account for potential dilution from ESOPs or convertible instruments. Never use for P/E ratio calculation.Trend only
Diluted EPSPAT divided by weighted average shares plus all dilutive potential shares (ESOPs, warrants, convertible bonds). Represents the worst-case EPS if all pending options are exercised. Required disclosure under Ind AS 33 in Annual Reports. Always use Diluted EPS for P/E ratio — it's the professional standard.Use for P/E
Adjusted EPSDiluted EPS after removing the after-tax impact of exceptional items, one-time gains, and prior period adjustments. Represents sustainable, recurring per-share earnings. The most reliable basis for forward P/E valuation. Not disclosed by companies — investors must calculate it manually using information from the P&L and exceptional items note.Best for PE
ESOPs / DilutionEmployee Stock Option Plans grant employees the right to buy company shares at a fixed (lower) price in the future. When exercised, new shares are created, increasing total share count and diluting EPS. Heavy ESOP grants in IT companies (Infosys, Wipro) create a systematic gap between Basic and Diluted EPS that grows over time if grants accelerate.Monitor gap
Weighted Avg SharesShare count weighted by the proportion of the year each share was outstanding. If 100 Cr shares existed for 9 months and 20 Cr new shares were issued in October, weighted avg = 105 Cr (not 120 Cr). Used in both Basic and Diluted EPS calculations to fairly reflect the per-share economics over the full year.EPS denominator
P/E RatioPrice-to-Earnings ratio = Share Price ÷ EPS (always use Adjusted Diluted EPS). The most widely used valuation multiple. A P/E of 25 means investors pay ₹25 for every ₹1 of annual earnings. Higher P/E = higher growth expectations or premium quality. Lower P/E = lower growth or perceived risk. Never use reported Basic EPS for P/E; always use Adjusted Diluted.Key metric
PEG RatioPrice/Earnings to Growth = P/E Ratio ÷ EPS CAGR (%). Adjusts P/E for growth rate. PEG below 1.0 = potentially undervalued. PEG 1.0–1.5 = fairly valued. PEG above 2.0 = expensive relative to growth. Example: P/E 30 with 20% EPS CAGR = PEG 1.5 (fair). P/E 30 with 5% EPS CAGR = PEG 6.0 (very expensive).Valuation
Share BuybackCompany purchases its own shares from the market, reducing total shares outstanding. This mechanically increases EPS even if PAT is unchanged. Buybacks are positive if funded from genuine free cash flow (TCS, Infosys). Negative if funded by borrowing. The key question: is EPS growth from buybacks or from actual business growth?Check source
EPS CAGRCompound Annual Growth Rate of EPS over 5 or 10 years. The most important long-term indicator of wealth creation potential. EPS CAGR should approximately equal long-term share price CAGR for a stable P/E business. 15%+ EPS CAGR over 10 years = exceptional compounding. Below 8% over 10 years = underperforms fixed income on a risk-adjusted basis.Key predictor
Dilution Gap %(Basic EPS − Diluted EPS) ÷ Basic EPS × 100. Measures how much ESOPs and other dilutive instruments reduce per-share earnings. Below 0.5% = negligible (TCS). 1–2% = moderate (Infosys). Above 5% = significant ESOP program; always use Diluted EPS and recalculate P/E accordingly. A widening gap year-over-year = ESOP grants accelerating.Watch trend
