01 — Key Learning Outcome

Why Filings? Because Companies Must Tell the Truth Here

📜
Your Core Takeaway
SEBI mandates that every listed company disclose all material information — board decisions, shareholding changes, quarterly results, annual reports — on BSE and NSE within 24–48 hours. These filings are legally binding. Management cannot lie here without criminal liability.
Imagine you're a detective. You can read what a suspect says in a newspaper interview, or you can read their sworn testimony filed with the court. The sworn testimony carries legal weight — they face consequences for lying. That's the difference between a company's press release and its SEBI filing.

When a promoter buys or sells shares, they must file a "Regulation 13" disclosure within 2 trading days. When a company signs a major contract, they must file a "Board Meeting outcome" disclosure on the same day. When quarterly results are declared, the auditor-certified numbers must be filed before the press release is issued. These filings — scattered across SEBI EDGAR, BSE, and NSE — are the raw material that every analyst, journalist, and research platform eventually uses. Going to the filing directly puts you ahead of everyone who waits for the interpretation.

Screener.in and Tijori Finance aggregate and organise these filings, adding analytical layers that make navigation faster — but the original filings remain the authoritative source. This chapter teaches you to navigate both: the raw regulatory databases and the organised analytical platforms.

02 — The Five Platforms — What Each Holds

From Regulator to Aggregator — The Complete Stack

Think of regulatory data as a pyramid: SEBI sets the rules and maintains the master database; BSE and NSE are the exchange-level depositories where companies actually file; Screener and Tijori aggregate and organise those filings into investor-friendly dashboards.

Companies file with BSE and NSE simultaneously. SEBI's EDGAR database maintains the master record. Screener.in and Tijori Finance aggregate and organise these filings for investor consumption. The FA analyst reads all three layers — raw filings for accuracy, aggregators for speed.

Key Filing Types — What to Search For and When
📊 Quarterly Financial Results
REG 33 · BSE / NSE XBRL Format
Auditor-certified P&L, Balance Sheet, and Cash Flow for each quarter. The most important regular filing. The actual filed numbers are available before analyst summaries are published — giving you a 30–60 minute information advantage to read the raw filing.
📅 Within 45 days of quarter end
👥 Shareholding Pattern
REG 31 · Filed Quarterly
Shows promoter holding %, FII %, DII %, and public holding each quarter. The most powerful signal: promoter holding rising = management conviction. Pledging increasing = financial stress. Both appear here before any newspaper picks it up.
📅 Within 21 days of quarter end
📋 Board Meeting Outcome
REG 30 · Material Event Disclosure
Any board decision — dividend announcement, new acquisition, MD resignation, fundraising — must be filed on the same day of the board meeting. This is the first public notification of major corporate events, often 12–24 hours before mainstream media coverage reaches you.
📅 Same day as board meeting
🚨 Insider Trading Disclosure
REG 13 · SEBI PIT Regulations
When any promoter, director, or connected person buys or sells shares in the company they work for, they must disclose it within 2 trading days. This is the most powerful single-stock signal: a promoter buying ₹10 Cr+ of own stock after a 30% fall is a strong conviction signal.
📅 Within 2 trading days of trade
📄 Annual Report & AGM Notice
Companies Act · Full Disclosure
The complete annual report — including the MD&A (Management Discussion & Analysis), auditor qualifications, related party transactions, and segment results — is filed on BSE/NSE. This is the most detailed document available for any company and is the foundation of all deep-dive fundamental analysis.
📅 Before AGM date (within 21 days)
🏗️ Offer / Takeover Documents
SEBI EDGAR · Takeover Code
Any public offer to acquire shares (open offer, IPO, rights issue, buyback, delisting) must file a detailed Letter of Offer on SEBI EDGAR — containing independent valuer reports, fair value assessments, and the acquirer's intentions. The most information-rich filing available for M&A situations.
📅 Depends on transaction timeline
🏛️
SEBI EDGAR
India's Securities Regulator · efts.sebi.gov.in · The Master Regulatory Database — Takeovers, Insider Trading, Offer Documents, SEBI Orders
Offer Documents Takeover Code SEBI Orders Insider Trading 100% Free
SEBI EDGAR (Electronic Data Gathering, Analysis and Retrieval) is the supreme regulatory repository for all formal securities market disclosures. Unlike BSE/NSE which receive company filings, SEBI EDGAR contains the most sensitive document types: open offer letters (when someone acquires 25%+ of a company), SEBI investigation orders, takeover public announcements, and independent valuer reports for delistings. These documents reveal information that company management would prefer to keep quiet — and they're completely free to search and download.

SEBI orders on EDGAR are particularly valuable for understanding a company's regulatory history. If a company has received a SEBI advisory or order — for disclosure violations, insider trading concerns, or related-party irregularities — it appears here. Many retail investors don't know to check this database, which means finding a SEBI action against a company management is information asymmetry in your favour.
📋
Open Offer / Public Announcement
When any entity acquires 25%+ shares in a listed company, they must make a public open offer for an additional 26% at a SEBI-determined price. The Letter of Offer contains independent valuer's opinion on fair value — often diverging from market price and revealing what the acquirer really thinks the company is worth.
🔍
SEBI Orders & Investigation Reports
SEBI publishes all its enforcement actions, prohibitory orders, and settlement agreements publicly. If a company's promoter was found manipulating share prices, or if insider trading was established — it's all here. Search any company + promoter name before investing to check their regulatory history.
🏢
Delisting Documents
When a company delist from exchanges, SEBI mandates an independent valuation by a SEBI-registered merchant banker, a price discovery through reverse book building, and a Letter of Offer with full financial disclosure. This document contains the fairest independent assessment of intrinsic value, uncontaminated by market sentiment.
🔄
Rights Issue & QIP Documents
When a company raises capital — rights issue to existing shareholders or Qualified Institutional Placement (QIP) to institutions — SEBI requires a detailed Offer Document disclosing all material risks, use of proceeds, and financial projections. The "Risk Factors" section of any offer document is the most honest description of what can go wrong with a business.
How to Use SEBI EDGAR — Step by Step
1
Go to efts.sebi.gov.in → Select "Takeovers" or "Public Issues" from left menu
SEBI EDGAR organises filings by type, not by company. Navigate to the relevant section based on what you're looking for — open offers, delistings, rights issues, or SEBI orders.
2
Search by company name or scrip code in the search box
Type the company name or NSE/BSE scrip code. All filings related to that company appear chronologically. Download the PDF — typically 50–200 pages but the key sections (Risk Factors, Valuation, Financial Information) are easy to identify.
efts.sebi.gov.in → Offer Documents → Search: "NESTLEIND"
3
Check SEBI Orders database for any company's regulatory history
Go to sebi.gov.in → Legal Framework → Orders → Search by entity name. A company with zero SEBI orders in 10 years has demonstrated regulatory compliance. Any orders should be read in full before investing — even settled orders reveal past management behaviour.
🔗 efts.sebi.gov.in (EDGAR) · sebi.gov.in/legal/orders.html (SEBI Orders) · Both free

BSE Filings
Bombay Stock Exchange · bseindia.com · India's Oldest Exchange Filing System — Real-time Company Announcements for 5,000+ Companies
Quarterly Results Annual Reports Board Decisions Shareholding 100% Free
BSE's company announcement portal is the most comprehensive real-time database of corporate disclosures in India — covering all 5,000+ listed companies across BSE. Every board decision, every quarterly result, every change in promoter holding, every material information disclosure appears here within hours of the event. BSE's system is company-wise, meaning you can see everything one company has ever disclosed — from its IPO prospectus to yesterday's board meeting outcome — in chronological order.

The key advantage of BSE filings over news sources: filings appear 30–120 minutes before mainstream financial media picks them up and writes summaries. An investor who reads the raw filing directly understands exactly what was disclosed — without the interpretive layer that may emphasise different points. BSE also has a mobile-friendly format and a dedicated app (BSE India) that shows real-time filing alerts.
📊
Financial Results (XBRL)
All quarterly and annual financials are filed in both PDF (human readable) and XBRL (machine readable) format. The XBRL format allows direct comparison across periods without manual data entry — every number is tagged with a standard identifier that Screener and other tools use to pull data automatically.
📅
Board Meeting Outcomes (Reg 30)
The most time-sensitive filing type. Within 30 minutes of a board meeting concluding, the company must file the outcome — dividend declared, buyback approved, key management change, new plant sanctioned. This is the raw, unedited disclosure before any PR team adds spin to the press release.
👥
Shareholding Pattern (Reg 31)
Quarterly snapshot of the entire ownership structure — promoter %, FII %, DII %, individual public %. Also shows pledge details: how much of promoter holding is pledged to lenders. Rising pledge % is one of the earliest distress signals available in public data.
📄
Annual Report PDF
The full annual report — including MD&A, Corporate Governance Report, audited financial statements, notes to accounts, and segment reporting — is filed here months before it appears in company websites or third-party platforms. The Notes to Accounts section reveals related-party transactions, contingent liabilities, and accounting policy changes that financial summaries never show.
How to Navigate BSE Filings — Step by Step
1
Go to bseindia.com → Click "Corporates" top menu → "Announcements"
The default view shows all company announcements across BSE in real-time chronological order. This feed is where every material disclosure from every listed company appears as it is filed — the most current picture of what's happening across the market.
2
Enter Scrip Code or Company Name in the search box to filter
Search for a specific company (e.g., "NESTLEIND" or scrip code 500790) to see all its filings. Use the "Category" filter to show only Quarterly Results, or only Shareholding filings, or only Board Meetings — narrowing down to what you need.
bseindia.com/corporates/ann/BSEAnnouncementDetails.aspx?scripcode=500790
3
Download the PDF → Focus on 5 key pages in any annual report
Don't read all 200 pages. Navigate to: (a) MD&A section — management's own commentary on business performance, (b) Related Party Transactions table — who the company is transacting with, (c) Notes to Accounts Note 32+ — contingent liabilities (pending legal cases), (d) Auditor's Report — any qualifications or emphasis of matter, (e) Segment Reporting — revenue breakdown by business line.
🔗 bseindia.com/corporates/ann → Filter by company + category + date range

NSE Data Portal
National Stock Exchange · nseindia.com · Circulars, Block Deals, Insider Trading, Bulk Deals — India's Largest Exchange by Volume
Circulars Block Deals Insider Trading Corporate Actions 100% Free
NSE's data portal duplicates most of BSE's company filing data, but adds several unique datasets not found on BSE: comprehensive insider trading history (all trades by insiders in any NSE-listed stock), bulk deal data (trades above 0.5% of shares outstanding in one session), and block deal data (large pre-negotiated trades between institutions). These datasets are the most direct evidence of what insiders and large institutions are doing with a stock — information that retail investors typically receive only through secondhand news.
🕵️
Insider Trading (SAST / PIT)
NSE publishes a daily file showing all insider trades disclosed under SEBI's Prevention of Insider Trading (PIT) regulations. When a promoter buys ₹20 Cr of their own stock after a 30% correction — this is the signal professional traders look for. Accessible for free, updated daily, available for all NSE-listed stocks going back years.
🏦
Bulk & Block Deals
Any trade exceeding 0.5% of total shares outstanding in a single session must be reported as a bulk deal. Block deals (pre-arranged trades above 0.5%) are disclosed separately. When a well-known institutional investor — Norges Bank, Government Pension Fund, major domestic MF — appears in bulk deals, it signals smart money positioning that often precedes analyst upgrades.
🔔
Corporate Actions Calendar
NSE maintains a complete calendar of upcoming corporate actions — dividend ex-dates, bonus issue record dates, rights issue timelines, stock split record dates — for all listed companies. Planning your investment around ex-dates (when dividend becomes due to current holders) requires this calendar to know exactly when the cut-off falls.
📡
Exchange Circulars
NSE publishes its own regulatory circulars — changes in margin requirements, new disclosure norms, trading halt announcements, surveillance additions. When a stock enters NSE's "additional surveillance measure" (ASM) list, a circular is published — indicating abnormal price movement concern. Stocks under ASM face trading restrictions that can trap retail investors.
NSE Data — Three Most Useful Searches
1
Insider Trading: nseindia.com → Market Data → Insider Trading → Filter by Company
Select date range (last 6 months), enter company name. See every insider trade — name of buyer/seller, number of shares, average price, type (buy/sell), and their designation. Sort by value — focus on trades above ₹1 Cr by promoters or senior management.
nseindia.com/market-data/insider-trading
2
Bulk Deals: nseindia.com → Market Data → Bulk Deals → Daily/Archival search
Download daily bulk deal files (CSV format — easily opened in Excel or Google Sheets). Search for any stock symbol. When reputed institutions appear repeatedly on the buy side over 2–3 weeks, it's a significant accumulation signal worth noting alongside fundamental research.
3
ASM / ESM List: nseindia.com → Regulations → Surveillance → ASM List
Check if any stock you're considering is on the Additional Surveillance Measure (ASM) or Enhanced Surveillance Measure (ESM) list. Stocks on these lists have special trading restrictions — mandatory upfront margin, intraday restrictions. Being on this list often indicates abnormal price or volume activity requiring extra caution.
🔗 nseindia.com/market-data/insider-trading · /bulk-deals · /corporate-actions

The Master Filing Rule
Every Piece of Information in an Analyst Report Originally Came From a Filing — Go to the Source
When you read an analyst report saying "promoter pledging at 62% — caution warranted", that analyst got the 62% from the BSE Regulation 31 shareholding filing. When a news article says "Maruti board approves ₹11,000 Cr Kharkhoda plant", the journalist read the BSE Regulation 30 Board Outcome filing. When Screener shows a company's revenue for the last 10 years, that data came from BSE XBRL quarterly result filings.

Every piece of investment information traces back to a regulatory filing. Learning to access these filings directly means you get information in its most accurate, uninterpreted form — before anyone adds their own framing. And you get it hours or days before it reaches mainstream news or analyst notes.

The five-platform stack for serious fundamental investors: SEBI EDGAR for regulatory history and offer documents → BSE for company-specific filings (results, board outcomes, annual reports) → NSE for insider trades and bulk deals → Screener for organised financial history → Tijori for aggregated insider intelligence and segment data. Each has a distinct role. Together they give you a complete regulatory and qualitative picture of any listed Indian company.
