01 — Key Learning Outcome

Calculate Net Asset Value & Identify Discount to NAV

🏗
Your Core Takeaway
When Godrej Properties or DLF releases a new project, it doesn't earn revenue immediately — construction takes 2–4 years. A P/E ratio would say the company is "expensive" or "loss-making" because cash hasn't come in yet. But the company already owns the land and has sold apartments at contracted prices. The true value is in the asset base, not in current earnings. NAV captures this: it values every piece of land at today's market price, every under-construction project at its expected realisable value, and subtracts all debt to find what shareholders actually own per share.
Think of a real estate developer like a baker who buys expensive flour today to bake and sell bread next year. P/E says "spending money, not earning" — but NAV says "they own Rs.10 worth of flour that becomes Rs.18 of bread." NAV is the baker's pantry valuation. For NBFCs, NAV values the loan book at fair value minus NPAs. For holding companies, NAV is the sum of stakes in subsidiaries. The approach: value each asset at market price, subtract all liabilities, divide by shares outstanding.

02 — How NAV Works

When P/E Fails — Why Real Estate Needs NAV Valuation

P/E ratio requires profits. But a real estate company buying land and starting construction has minimal current earnings. NAV bypasses the income statement entirely and looks at what the company OWNS — asset by asset — and what it OWES. The difference per share is the true value investors should track.

🏗 Three assets: Completed Rs.800 Cr + Construction Rs.500 Cr + Land Rs.1,200 Cr = Gross NAV Rs.2,500 Cr. Subtract net debt Rs.700 Cr = Net NAV Rs.1,800 Cr. With 30 Cr shares = Rs.600/share NAV. Stock at Rs.420 = 30% discount to NAV.

1

Why P/E Fails for Real Estate, NBFCs, and Holding Companies

Real estate: Revenues are recognised on completion — a company can buy land and start construction for 3 years with zero revenue recognised. P/E in Year 1 = infinity (appears loss-making). But NAV rises each year as construction progresses and apartment prices appreciate. NBFCs: A loan book's value depends on credit quality, not just reported income. Holding companies: Their "earnings" may be tiny dividends from subsidiaries, but the value of those subsidiaries can be enormous. P/E would say the holding company is extremely expensive — NAV shows the true intrinsic value.

2

NAV for Real Estate: Valuing Land, WIP, and Completed Properties

Land bank: valued at current market price per sq ft in that micro-market (from property consultants: JLL, CBRE, Anarock). Under-construction (WIP): value as contracted sales price of sold units + current ASP for unsold units, minus remaining construction cost. Completed unsold inventory: value at current market selling price. Rental assets: value using cap rate = Net Operating Income divided by Market Cap Rate (typically 6-8% for Grade A Indian commercial real estate). Sum all of these to get Gross NAV.

3

NAV for NBFCs: Adjusted Book Value Method

For Housing Finance Companies like LIC Housing Finance, NAV = Adjusted Net Worth = Book Value of Equity minus provisions that may be understated. Compare Adjusted NAV to market cap — if trading below 0.8x Adjusted NAV, either there is deep distress OR a buying opportunity. The key: look at GNPA and NNPA ratios on Screener.in to judge credit quality. NNPA above 3% with rising trend means significant uncertainty in the NAV. Apply the NNPA x 60% rule: subtract this from book value to get a conservative Adjusted NAV.

4

NAV for Holding Companies: Sum of Stakes in Subsidiaries

For listed subsidiaries: Holding Company NAV = Sum of (Parent's stake percentage x Listed subsidiary market cap). For unlisted subsidiaries: estimate using P/E or EV/EBITDA of comparable listed peers. Example: If Bajaj Finserv holds 52.49% of Bajaj Finance (listed), that stake's value = 52.49% x Bajaj Finance market cap. Add all subsidiary stakes to get Gross NAV. Subtract holding company-level debt. Divide by holding company's own shares outstanding. Then apply a holdco structural discount of 20-40% — the holding company always trades below Gross NAV.

LIC Housing Finance — Adjusted Book Value NAV for Housing Finance Companies

✅ For Housing Finance Companies like LIC Housing Finance or Can Fin Homes, NAV is not about real estate assets — it is the Adjusted Net Worth (Adjusted Book Value) of the loan book itself. On Screener.in: find Book Value per Share in the Ratios tab, then adjust for NNPA ratio. HFCs with low GNPA/NNPA deserve to trade near or above Adjusted NAV. Those with rising NPAs deserve a discount. LIC Housing Finance's P/B of 0.8-1.2x reflects this framework — investors track its NAV discount as closely as any real estate stock in the market.

05 — NAV Valuation Pitfalls

5 Ways NAV Analysis Goes Dangerously Wrong

NAV is only as good as the asset values and debt figures used. Stale land valuations, understated debt, inflated project assumptions, and opaque disclosures can make a company look cheap on NAV when it is actually overvalued or in distress.

📅
Stale Land Valuations — Using 2007 Cost Price as Current Market Value
Using land's book value (purchase cost) instead of current market value systematically misstates NAV dramatically in both directions
🚨 Book cost can be 10x away from market value 🚨 Old cheap land = understated NAV 🚨 Old expensive land = overstated NAV
🚨 The most common NAV error: using the land's book value (purchase cost) instead of current market value. A developer who bought land in Noida in 2005 for Rs.50 Cr would show Rs.50 Cr on the balance sheet — but that land is worth Rs.800 Cr today. Using book value would massively understate NAV. But the reverse trap also exists: a developer who bought land at 2007-08 peak prices (say Rs.500 Cr) for land now worth Rs.200 Cr would overstate NAV by 2.5x using book value. The rule: always use current market price from independent property consultants (JLL, CBRE, Cushman and Wakefield, Knight Frank) — NOT balance sheet book value. This requires access to property consultant reports, which are available through developer investor presentations, RERA filings, and annual reports. Never trust any NAV model that uses gross development value from 2015 for 2024 analysis.
🏗
Inflated WIP Assumptions — The "Rs.15,000/sq ft will sell" Optimism Problem
Using management's hoped-for ASP targets instead of currently achievable contracted prices for under-construction projects
🚨 Aspirational vs achievable ASP gap 🚨 NAV easily inflated 30-50% this way 🚨 Cross-check with RERA portal prices always
🚨 The second most common NAV mistake: using management's aspirational ASP targets for unsold under-construction inventory instead of currently achievable market prices. If a developer has 10M sq ft of unsold WIP inventory and uses Rs.15,000 per sq ft (aspirational target) instead of the currently achievable Rs.10,000 per sq ft, the NAV gets inflated by Rs.5,000 Cr at just that one project. Several mid-tier developers communicated NAV estimates based on peak-cycle ASP assumptions during 2019-21 that proved grossly optimistic during the COVID slowdown. The rule: for sold inventory with signed agreements, use the actual contracted price (available from RERA). For unsold inventory, use the most recent transacted prices from the RERA portal — not management guidance. RERA portals now publish actual transaction prices project-by-project, making this verification straightforward and free.
💸
Missing Off-Balance-Sheet Debt — The Structured Finance Trap
Project SPV borrowings and structured NCDs that do not fully consolidate into the parent company's reported net debt
🚨 Effective debt 20-40% higher than stated 🚨 NAV appears positive, equity near zero 🚨 Check Notes to Accounts thoroughly
🚨 Real estate companies have historically used complex financing structures — Non-Convertible Debentures, structured finance, and project SPV borrowings — that may not fully consolidate into the parent company's reported net debt on Screener.in. This creates a dangerous gap: the NAV model uses reported consolidated debt but the actual economic debt is 20-40% higher once all project-level financing is included. The NBCC crisis, IL and FS connected real estate exposures, and several mid-tier developer collapses during 2018-2022 all involved debt structures not fully visible in consolidated balance sheets. The fix: read the Notes to Accounts carefully — look for Related Party Transactions, Contingent Liabilities, and Project-Specific Borrowings disclosures. Also check credit rating agency reports (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE) — they typically capture the complete debt picture including structured instruments that may not appear on Screener.
🏦
NBFC NAV Without NPA Adjustment — The Evergreening Trap
Using raw book value of loan book without adjusting for embedded but not fully provisioned NPAs
🚨 GNPA often understated through evergreening 🚨 Yes Bank overvaluation visible in hindsight 🚨 Apply NNPA x 60% haircut rule always
🚨 For NBFCs and HFCs, using the reported book value directly as NAV without adjusting for credit quality produces a dangerously optimistic estimate. Loan book NAV is only valid if provisions are adequate. During 2018-2020 (post IL and FS crisis), multiple smaller NBFCs reported book values implying NAV of Rs.150-200 per share while actual recoverable value after recognising all embedded NPAs was Rs.40-80 per share. The mechanism is "evergreening" — giving fresh loans to stressed borrowers so they can repay original loans, artificially keeping the asset status as Standard (not NPA). The fix on Screener.in: find GNPA% and NNPA% in the Ratios section for all listed NBFCs. NNPA above 3% with rising trend means significant uncertainty in the stated NAV. Apply Adjusted NAV = Book Value minus (NNPA x 60%) as a conservative floor. This rule helped identify Yes Bank's overvaluation in 2018 and also highlighted LIC Housing Finance's genuine discount in 2020 as a buying opportunity.
🔍
Holdco NAV Without Structural Discount — The "Just Add Up Stakes" Error
Adding subsidiary market caps without applying the structural holdco discount overvalues the parent by 20-45% systematically
🚨 Gross NAV does not equal holdco market cap 🚨 Structural discount is 20-45% always 🚨 Unlisted stakes need 30-50% haircut
🚨 Adding up the current market value of all listed subsidiary stakes and claiming that sum is the fair value of the holding company systematically overvalues the holdco by 20-45% through four structural mechanisms. Reason 1: Dividend distribution tax creates layers of tax on cash extracted from subsidiaries. Reason 2: Liquidity discount — the holdco cannot sell its large stake at current market price without crashing the subsidiary's stock price. Reason 3: Governance and complexity premium — investors can simply buy the subsidiary directly, avoiding holdco overhead. Reason 4: Capital allocation risk — holding company management may redeploy capital from good businesses into bad ones. Historical data: Indian conglomerates trade at 15-35% below gross NAV on average. For unlisted subsidiaries, the discount should be 30-50% since there is no liquid market exit. Example error: "Holding company owns 70% of XYZ Ltd worth Rs.10,000 Cr, so holdco should be valued at Rs.7,000 Cr." Wrong. Apply 30% holdco discount = Rs.4,900 Cr. Missing this step inflates per-share holdco value by the full discount amount.

4
Calculate → Gross NAV = Land + WIP + Completed + Investments
In a spreadsheet, add: (a) Land bank: Area in sq ft x current market price per sq ft by location from JLL/CBRE, (b) WIP: Use total contracted sales pipeline from investor presentation as proxy, minus remaining construction cost (typically 30-40% of total project cost for projects 60-70% complete), (c) Completed unsold: units x current ASP (RERA verified), (d) JV stakes and investments at estimated values. Sum all to get Gross Asset NAV. Then: Net NAV = Gross Asset NAV minus Net Debt. NAV per Share = Net NAV divided by Shares Outstanding from Screener.
✅ NAV higher than market cap by 20%+: investigate as potential opportunity. Always verify debt quality, balance sheet health, and execution track record before concluding undervaluation.
🚨 NAV lower than market cap by 20%+: market pricing in future NAV growth (new launches, land bank appreciation). Justified only if management has strong execution history and pre-sales are accelerating.
5
Interpret → Discount = (Stock Price / NAV per share - 1) x 100
Calculate: Discount % = (Stock Price minus NAV per share) divided by NAV per share x 100. Negative result = stock trading at a discount to NAV. Positive result = stock trading at a premium to NAV. Compare to the company's historical average discount or premium. If the company normally trades at 20% premium and is now at 40% discount — investigate why without assuming opportunity. Track the discount quarterly — a widening discount without fundamental reason is a potential signal; a narrowing discount or growing premium often signals reducing upside. The three signals together: wider discount + improving balance sheet + accelerating pre-sales = higher probability of undervaluation.
✅ Discount wider than historical + improving balance sheet + pre-sales accelerating = three signals aligned for potential undervaluation. All three together are more reliable than discount alone.
🚨 Premium above 50% NAV + pre-sales slowing + debt rising = caution. Market may be pricing in growth that is not materialising. Re-examine NAV assumptions and track record.
⚡ 4 Advanced NAV Tricks
A
The Pre-Sales vs Collections Ratio
For RE developers, pre-sales (bookings) drive future NAV while collections drive current cash flow. The ratio Pre-Sales divided by Collections tells you: if close to 1x, the developer is efficiently converting bookings to cash. If much above 1x (high bookings, low collections), the risk exists that buyers are defaulting or projects are delayed. On Screener.in, track Operating Cash Flow versus reported pre-sales from investor presentations. A developer with 2x pre-sales but only 0.5x collections efficiency has fragile NAV — the pipeline exists on paper but cash is not following. Healthy NAV requires both strong bookings AND strong collections.
B
Cap Rate Valuation for Rental Assets
For commercial real estate (offices, malls), use cap rate valuation: Value = NOI divided by Market Cap Rate. Current Indian market cap rates: Grade A offices in BKC/Gurgaon/Bengaluru = 7-8%. Grade B offices = 8-10%. Retail malls = 8-9%. Hotels = 9-11%. Example: An office with NOI of Rs.500 Cr at 8% cap rate gives Value = Rs.500 divided by 8% = Rs.6,250 Cr. This method applies to DLF's Cyber City portfolio, Prestige's tech parks, and Embassy Office Parks REIT. Always compare your cap rate assumption against recent actual transactions — JLL publishes annual India Cap Rate surveys that are partially available in the public domain.
C
Holdco Discount Trigger Watch
Holding company discounts can close rapidly when a catalyst is announced: (1) Subsidiary listing on exchanges, (2) Dividend policy change increasing payout, (3) Share buyback by the holding company, (4) Strategic review or demerger announcement. Monitor news for any of these four triggers for your holdco investment. The playbook: when the discount is wide AND a catalyst appears approaching, the trade is to buy the holdco. The discount closing means the holdco stock rises while the subsidiary stock stays stable or falls. This spread has worked multiple times in Indian markets including Bajaj Finserv versus Bajaj Finance, Reliance versus listed subsidiaries, and various Tata Group holdco situations.
D
The NNPA x 0.6 Rule for NBFC NAV
Quick NBFC Adjusted NAV formula: Adjusted NAV = Book Value minus (NNPA% x Total Advances x 0.6). The 0.6 factor represents the additional provision you should conservatively apply to net NPAs that may not be fully recovered. Example: NBFC with Book Value Rs.150/share, NNPA 4%, Total Advances Rs.10,000 Cr means Adjusted NAV = Rs.150 minus (4% x Rs.10,000 Cr x 0.6 divided by shares). This gives a conservative floor NAV. If the stock trades below this conservative Adjusted NAV, it may be genuinely cheap or there is undisclosed stress — investigate further. This rule helped identify Yes Bank overvaluation in 2018 and LIC Housing Finance's genuine discount in 2020 as a buying opportunity.

07 — Reference Glossary

NAV and Real Estate Valuation Glossary

Term What It Means in Simple Words Key Point
NAV (Net Asset Value)The per-share value of a company's assets at current market prices, minus all liabilities. Formula: (Gross Asset Value minus Net Debt) divided by Shares Outstanding. Most relevant for real estate developers, NBFCs, and holding companies where assets are the primary value driver rather than current earnings. NAV answers: what does the company own today and what would it be worth if everything were sold and debt repaid?Core metric
Gross Development Value (GDV)The total expected revenue from all units in a real estate project if sold at current market prices. GDV = Total saleable area x Average Selling Price. This is the top-line of project economics before subtracting construction cost, land cost, and financing costs. GDV is the basis for WIP valuation in developer NAV models, but it is not NAV — it must be reduced by all project costs to arrive at the project's contribution to Net Asset Value.RE developers
Cap RateCapitalisation Rate = Net Operating Income divided by Property Market Value. Used to value income-producing real estate: offices, malls, warehouses, hotels. If an office earns NOI of Rs.100 Cr and the market cap rate is 8%, its value = Rs.100 divided by 8% = Rs.1,250 Cr. Lower cap rates equal higher valuations for the same income stream. Grade A offices in BKC/Gurgaon currently trade at 7-8% cap rates. Cap rates move inversely to property values — rising rates mean falling property values, similar to bond duration mathematics.Commercial RE
Average Selling Price (ASP)The per-square-foot price at which a real estate developer is selling units in a project. ASP is the most critical input in developer NAV modelling — small changes in ASP assumption cause large swings in NAV. For sold inventory: use the contracted ASP from the registered sale agreement. For unsold inventory: use current market ASP from RERA data or management guidance cross-checked against RERA transaction prices. Always distinguish between carpet area ASP reported on RERA and built-up or super built-up ASP quoted by developers in their marketing.Key NAV input
Holdco DiscountThe percentage below gross NAV at which a holding company typically trades in the market. Structural discount of 20-45% exists because: dividend distribution creates tax layers, management cannot sell subsidiary stakes without market impact, investors can directly own the subsidiary more efficiently, and capital allocation risk adds uncertainty. Discount narrows when subsidiaries list separately, dividends increase, or demergers are announced. Measuring the current discount vs historical average is the primary investment signal for holding company analysis.20-45% typical
Adjusted Book Value (ABV)For NBFCs and banks: ABV = Reported Book Value minus Estimated credit losses not yet provisioned. Quick calculation: ABV = Book Value minus (NNPA% x Total Advances x 60%). This adjustment accounts for the possibility that net NPAs will not be fully recovered even with existing provisions. Companies with high NNPA and poor provision coverage deserve to trade below ABV. Those with low NNPA and adequate provisions deserve to trade at or above ABV. ABV is the NAV equivalent for financial sector companies including HFCs and NBFCs.NBFCs and HFCs
Pre-Sales (Bookings)The value of apartments sold — agreements signed and advances paid — but not yet delivered or revenue-recognised under accounting standards. Pre-sales generate deposits that reduce effective working capital needs and provide contracted future revenue visibility. Pre-sales are the leading indicator for a developer's NAV trajectory: strong pre-sales mean the WIP component of NAV is growing. Tracked quarterly in investor presentations. Strong pre-sales with strong collections equals strong NAV momentum; high pre-sales with weak collections signals execution risk.RE leading indicator
NNPA (Net Non-Performing Assets)Net NPA = Gross NPA minus Provisions made against those bad loans. Represents the credit risk in an NBFC's loan book not yet covered by provisions. Available on Screener.in Ratios section for all listed NBFCs and banks. NNPA below 1% = excellent credit quality. NNPA 1-3% = acceptable. NNPA above 3% = elevated credit risk. For NAV adjustment: approximately 60% of NNPA may represent unrecoverable loss and should be deducted from book value to arrive at a conservative Adjusted NAV for NBFC valuation.NBFC credit risk
RERAReal Estate Regulatory Authority — India's regulatory framework requiring all residential projects above a threshold to be registered before marketing. State RERA portals (MahaRERA for Maharashtra, RERA Karnataka, UP RERA) disclose project completion timelines, actual carpet area measurements, registered transaction prices for completed sales, and escrow account balances. For NAV analysis, RERA is an invaluable free data source to verify ASP assumptions and project completion status — two of the most critical inputs to any real estate developer NAV model.Free RE data
Embedded Value (Insurance)An actuarial measure of a life insurance company's current value, representing the present value of future profits from in-force policies plus adjusted net worth. Published annually by listed life insurers. P/EV multiple = Market Cap divided by Embedded Value. Used to value insurance subsidiaries within holding company NAV calculations. A growing EV with high new business margins supports a premium EV multiple. Stagnant EV with low margins supports a discount. India's large life insurers (LIC, HDFC Life, SBI Life) publish EV in their annual disclosures, making holdco NAV calculation for insurance-heavy companies precise and verifiable.Insurance companies
