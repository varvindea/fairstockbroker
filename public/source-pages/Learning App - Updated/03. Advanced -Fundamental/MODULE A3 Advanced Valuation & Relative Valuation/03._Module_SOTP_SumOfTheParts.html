01 — Key Learning Outcome

Value Conglomerates by Segment: Reliance · ITC · L&T

🏢
Your Core Takeaway
Reliance Industries is not one company. It is six businesses living under one stock symbol: an O2C (oil-to-chemicals) refinery, Jio Platforms (telecom + digital), Reliance Retail (biggest Indian retailer), Reliance New Energy (green energy), financial services, and media/entertainment. Valuing Reliance with a single P/E ratio — as if it were Nestlé or TCS — produces a wildly inaccurate number. SOTP solves this: value each segment using the multiple or method most appropriate for that type of business, then add the segment values together.
Think of a conglomerate as a thali plate: biryani, dal makhani, gulab jamun, and raita — all in one plate. You wouldn't price the whole thali using only the price-per-gram of biryani. The biryani is high-margin, the dal is lower-margin, the gulab jamun is sweet (premium), and the raita is commoditised. Each dish has its own pricing logic. Similarly, each segment of a conglomerate deserves its own valuation method. Adding up the per-dish prices gives you the true value of the thali.

02 — How SOTP Works

Why One Multiple Can't Value a Conglomerate

Reliance Industries' O2C business deserves a 6–8× EV/EBITDA (commodity business). Jio Platforms deserves 18–22× EV/EBITDA (telecom + digital). Reliance Retail deserves 35–50× P/E (high-growth retail). Using any single multiple for the whole group either overvalues the commodities or undervalues the high-growth segments.

🏢 Reliance Industries split into 5 segments. Each segment gets its own valuation method — O2C gets EV/EBITDA 6–8×, Jio gets 20×, Retail gets 40× P/E. Sum them up, subtract 15% conglomerate discount = ₹19.3 Lakh Crore SOTP fair value.

1

What is the Conglomerate Discount — and Why Does It Exist?

The conglomerate discount (typically 10–25%) is the gap between SOTP value and the actual market cap. It exists because: (1) Investors can't cleanly track each segment — one annual report covers all businesses, creating opacity. (2) Capital allocation risk — management may invest profits from the good business into the bad one. (3) Complexity premium — analysts require a return for the extra work. (4) Holding company structure — the parent often holds subsidiaries through intermediate holdcos, creating tax drag. Reliance consistently trades at 10–20% below its SOTP value.

2

Which Valuation Method for Which Segment?

Rule of thumb: Asset-heavy commodities (oil, steel, cement) → EV/EBITDA 5–10×. Consumer/FMCG → P/E 25–50×. Telecom → EV/EBITDA 12–20×. Banking/NBFC → P/B 1–4×. Technology/SaaS → P/E 30–60× or EV/Revenue 5–15×. Real estate → NAV (Net Asset Value). Pre-revenue businesses → DCF. Using the wrong method for a segment — like applying a commodity multiple to a high-growth digital segment — is the single biggest source of SOTP error.

3

How to Find Segment EBITDA/PAT on Screener.in

Most conglomerates file consolidated + standalone results. For SOTP: go to the company's Annual Report → "Segment Information" note — this is a mandatory Ind AS 108 disclosure. It shows revenue, EBIT, and assets per segment. On Screener.in, the P&L shows consolidated numbers — use the annual report segment note for the breakdown. Some companies (Reliance, ITC, L&T) also publish detailed segment-level disclosures in investor presentations, which are easier to use.

4

Calculating the Right Conglomerate Discount

The discount varies by transparency and holding structure: Clean holdco (Bajaj Finserv — financial services only, transparent) → 5–10% discount. Mixed conglomerate (ITC — tobacco + FMCG + hotels + agri + paperboards) → 15–25% discount. Opaque conglomerate (complex holdco chains, related-party transactions) → 25–40% discount. The discount narrows when management announces demergers or separate listings — any news of "unlocking value" via demerger causes the stock to re-rate toward SOTP value.

04 — SOTP Done Right

5 Conglomerates Where SOTP Works Perfectly

These conglomerates have clear segment boundaries, transparent disclosures, and business units that are genuinely comparable to standalone listed peers — making SOTP the ideal valuation framework.

🛢️
Reliance Industries — SOTP Clarity Champion
O2C · Jio · Retail · New Energy · Media · Financial Services — each comparable to listed peers
✅ Segment-level investor disclosures ✅ Jio Financial already listed 📌 Discount narrows on demerger news
✅ Reliance's SOTP is the most analysed and most debated in Indian equity research — and for good reason. Every major brokerage (Motilal Oswal, Kotak, UBS, Jefferies) publishes an SOTP model for Reliance, and they all produce slightly different answers based on which multiple they use for Retail and New Energy. The discipline of the SOTP framework forces analysts to justify their assumptions for each segment explicitly. When Jio Financial Services was listed separately in August 2023, it gave investors direct price discovery for one segment — reducing the SOTP uncertainty and contributing to Reliance's stock re-rating. The SOTP approach correctly captures the multi-faceted value of this business in a way no single P/E ratio ever could.
🚬
ITC Limited — Multi-Segment SOTP Classic
Cigarettes · FMCG · Hotels · Agribusiness · Paperboards · Each a different multiple
✅ Transparent segment P&L in AR ✅ Hotels demerger unlocking value ✅ FMCG segment = fastest growing
✅ ITC is the textbook SOTP stock in India — every analyst covers it using SOTP because its segments couldn't be more different from each other. Cigarettes: near-monopoly, 80%+ EBIT margin, valued at 20–25× EBIT. FMCG (Sunfeast, Bingo, Classmate): high-growth, 8–12% EBIT margin, valued at 50–70× EBIT. Hotels (ITC Hotels): asset-heavy hospitality, valued at EV/EBITDA 18–22×. Agribusiness: commodity, EV/EBITDA 8–10×. Paperboards: packaging, EV/EBITDA 8–12×. Each segment's EBIT and appropriate multiple can be directly pulled from ITC's Annual Report. The SOTP for ITC typically shows the cigarette business alone is worth more than the company's total market cap — which explains why ITC has historically traded at a discount (cigarette regulatory risk creates the conglomerate discount).
⚙️
L&T (Larsen & Toubro) — Engineering + Financial Services SOTP
Infrastructure · Defence · Technology · Financial Services · Hydrocarbon · Realty
✅ Order book visibility → DCF accuracy ✅ L&T Finance listed separately 📌 Defence segment getting premium
✅ L&T's SOTP perfectly captures the diversity of a world-class engineering conglomerate that cannot be valued with a single multiple. Infrastructure (roads, metros, buildings): valued on EV/EBITDA 12–16×. Defence (missiles, submarines): valued at P/E 35–50× (government visibility, high barriers). Technology (LTIMindtree: listed at its own market cap — use that directly as the listed price). Financial Services (L&T Finance: listed separately). Hydrocarbon: EV/EBITDA 10–14×. The genius of L&T's SOTP is that two major segments (LTIMindtree and L&T Finance) are already listed, giving precise price discovery without any estimation uncertainty. L&T's SOTP is almost the ideal case study for why conglomerate SOTP works — transparent segments, listed subsidiaries as anchors, and a clear parent holding discount.
🏦
Bajaj Finserv — Financial Services SOTP Model
Bajaj Finance · Bajaj Allianz Life · Bajaj Allianz General — each with its own valuation
✅ Bajaj Finance listed — direct price ✅ Insurance valued on EV (embedded value) ✅ Minimal conglomerate discount (5–10%)
✅ Bajaj Finserv's SOTP is one of the cleaner ones in India — because the primary business (Bajaj Finance) is listed, giving exact market price discovery for the largest segment. Bajaj Finance stake (52.49%): just multiply the listed market cap of Bajaj Finance by Bajaj Finserv's stake — no estimation needed. Bajaj Allianz Life Insurance: valued using Embedded Value (EV) methodology — a standard actuarial method for life insurance that estimates the present value of future policyholder profits. Bajaj Allianz General Insurance: valued at P/B 3–5× or EV/GWP (Gross Written Premium) 2–3×. The holding company discount for Bajaj Finserv is typically only 5–10% because of the clean structure and listed anchor. When Bajaj Finance's stock price moves, Bajaj Finserv's SOTP mechanically adjusts — making it easy to track whether the discount is widening or narrowing.
🏗️
Tata Sons / Tata Group (Unlisted Holdco Context)
TCS · Titan · Tata Motors · Tata Steel · Indian Hotels — all separately listed
✅ All subsidiaries listed → direct prices ✅ Holdco discount historically 30–50% ⚠️ Not directly investable (unlisted)
✅ Tata Sons is the purest SOTP case study in India — all major subsidiaries are separately listed, making the SOTP essentially a portfolio of known market caps. TCS (68.19% stake × TCS market cap), Titan (25% stake × Titan market cap), Tata Motors (46% stake × Tata Motors market cap), Tata Steel (33% stake × Tata Steel market cap), Indian Hotels (38% stake × IHCL market cap). The Tata Sons SOTP at any given time = Σ(Tata Sons stake % × listed subsidiary market cap). The holding company discount (30–50% below SOTP) reflects the fact that Tata Sons is unlisted, making investors' exit difficult. If Tata Sons ever lists (rumoured periodically), the stock would re-rate dramatically toward SOTP value — one of India's biggest potential unlocking events.
05 — SOTP Valuation Errors

5 Ways SOTP Analysis Goes Wrong

SOTP fails when segment data is unavailable, when wrong multiples are used for each segment, or when the conglomerate discount is ignored. Each error produces a systematically wrong valuation.

❓
No Segment Disclosure — Opaque Conglomerates
Some conglomerates don't disclose segment EBIT clearly — makes SOTP impossible without estimates
🚨 Estimated segments = estimated SOTP 🚨 Wide error range 🚨 SOTP is directional only
🚨 When a conglomerate doesn't clearly disclose segment-level EBIT or EBITDA, the SOTP becomes a guessing exercise — and guessing produces wrong valuations with high confidence. Some Indian holding companies disclose only consolidated P&L without clean segment breakdowns. In this case, analysts attempt to "reverse-engineer" segment profitability from management commentary, investor day slides, and channel checks. The result is SOTP estimates that can differ by 30–50% between analysts. The lesson: if you can't reliably identify segment-level EBITDA or PAT from the Annual Report's Segment Note (Ind AS 108), be very conservative about trusting any SOTP model for that company. Wide confidence intervals in SOTP inputs produce extremely wide output ranges — often useless for investment decisions.
📊
Applying Same Multiple to All Segments (Conglomerate P/E Trap)
Using one P/E for a company with both commodity and high-growth segments
🚨 Undervalues growth segments 🚨 Overvalues commodity segments 🚨 P/E of 20× for oil is too high
🚨 The most common beginner SOTP error: assigning the same P/E or EV/EBITDA multiple to every segment just because it's convenient. Example: Valuing Reliance's O2C business at 25× P/E (same as Reliance Retail) massively overvalues a commodity refining business that should be at 6–8× EV/EBITDA. Conversely, valuing Jio Platforms at 8× EV/EBITDA (same as the refinery) massively undervalues one of India's most valuable digital infrastructure businesses. The whole point of SOTP is to use the RIGHT multiple for each segment. Rule: look at what standalone listed comparables trade at for that specific business type. Refining → look at HPCL/BPCL/IOC. Digital telecom → look at Bharti Airtel. Retail → look at DMart/Trent. Use those trading multiples for each segment, not one blended multiple.
🔻
Ignoring the Conglomerate Discount Entirely
Adding up segments and claiming that is the market cap — ignores structural holding discount
🚨 Gross SOTP ≠ Market Cap 🚨 Discount is 10–40% structurally 🚨 Overestimates fair value
🚨 Taking the SOTP gross total and declaring it the fair market cap of the conglomerate is a systematic overvaluation that ignores how conglomerates actually trade. Conglomerates always trade at a discount to their SOTP sum for structural reasons: opacity, capital allocation risk, higher taxes at the holdco level (dividend distribution tax on dividends received from subsidiaries), management overhead, and investor preference for pure-play exposure. Historical data: Indian conglomerates have traded at 15–35% below their SOTP on average. For ITC, the discount has historically been 20–30% due to cigarette regulatory risk contaminating the perception of the FMCG and Hotels businesses. Always apply a segment-appropriate discount: clean holdcos (Bajaj Finserv) → 5–10%; complex industrial conglomerates (L&T, Reliance) → 15–25%; opaque conglomerates → 25–40%.
💸
Ignoring Holdco Net Debt and Cross-Holdings
SOTP gives enterprise value of segments — must deduct holdco debt to get equity value
🚨 Gross EV ≠ Equity value 🚨 Missing net debt deduction 🚨 Per-share value overstated
🚨 SOTP gives you the Enterprise Value of each segment — but investors own the equity, not the EV. To arrive at equity value per share: SOTP Gross EV − Holdco Net Debt (debt at the parent company level, not at subsidiaries) + Cash at Holdco − Cross-holdings eliminated. Example: If Reliance SOTP = ₹22.7L Cr and Reliance holds ₹1.4L Cr net debt at the holdco level, the equity value = ₹21.3L Cr. Divide by 674 Cr shares = ₹3,160/share BEFORE conglomerate discount. Missing the net debt step inflates per-share SOTP by 5–10%. Additionally: cross-holdings (where Company A holds Company B which also holds Company A) must be eliminated to avoid double-counting — this is more common in complex family business structures.
🌱
Overvaluing Pre-Revenue Segments (New Energy, Startups)
Assigning large DCF values to businesses with no revenue creates SOTP inflation
🚨 DCF assumptions are everything 🚨 Terminal value drives 80% of DCF 🚨 Scenario analysis is essential
🚨 Assigning aggressive DCF values to pre-revenue segments (Reliance New Energy, startup subsidiaries, early-stage ventures) systematically inflates SOTP without any market cross-check. When Reliance New Energy is valued at ₹3L Cr using optimistic green hydrogen price assumptions and aggressive market share projections, it adds 13% to the total SOTP — based entirely on assumptions that may not materialise. The discipline: for pre-revenue segments, use scenario analysis (bear/base/bull cases) and weight them by probability. Present the SOTP range (₹16L Cr to ₹28L Cr) rather than a single point estimate. Single-point SOTP with an aggressive pre-revenue segment is not a valuation — it's optimism dressed as analysis. Professional equity research always presents SOTP sensitivity tables showing how the output changes with ±10% change in each segment's multiple.

Annual Report → Find "Segment Information" note (Ind AS 108)
Download the company's Annual Report from BSE/NSE filings page. Open the PDF and search for "Segment Information" or "Segment Reporting" — this is a mandatory Ind AS 108 note that discloses revenue, EBIT, and total assets for each reportable segment. Write down: Segment Name, Segment Revenue, Segment EBIT (or EBITDA if provided). Also note any inter-segment eliminations — these must not be double-counted.
📋 Goal: A table of all segments with their EBIT or EBITDA. If only revenue is given, estimate EBIT by applying each segment's approximate margin from management commentary. Segments with less than 10% of total revenue can be grouped as "Others."
2
Screener.in → Find pure-play peers for each segment
For each segment, find the closest listed pure-play comparable. Go to Screener.in → search for a company that does ONLY that business. For ITC's Hotels segment: search "Indian Hotels" (Taj) on Screener → note its EV/EBITDA. For ITC's FMCG segment: search "Britannia" and "HUL" → note their P/E. These listed pure-plays trade at the "undiscounted" standalone value — their multiples are your segment benchmarks. If a subsidiary is itself listed (e.g., Tata Motors' subsidiary Jaguar Land Rover contributes — but JLR isn't listed; use BMW/Audi as proxies with India discount).
Segment Value= Segment EBITDA × Pure-Play Peer EV/EBITDA
OR: Segment Value= Segment PAT × Pure-Play Peer P/E
3
Spreadsheet → Calculate each segment EV → sum to SOTP Gross
Create a simple table: Segment | Metric (EBITDA/PAT) | Value | Multiple | Segment EV. Multiply each metric by its multiple to get segment EV. Sum all segment EVs = SOTP Gross Value. If any segment is already listed (e.g., Bajaj Finance within Bajaj Finserv) — just use that listed company's market cap × parent's stake — no multiple calculation needed.
💡 Listed subsidiary shortcut
If a subsidiary is independently listed on NSE/BSE, go to Screener.in, find the subsidiary's current market cap, multiply by the parent's ownership percentage = that segment's contribution to SOTP. No guesswork about multiples — the market has already priced it. This applies to: L&T → LTIMindtree (73%); Bajaj Finserv → Bajaj Finance (52%); Reliance → Jio Financial Services (6.1% directly).
4
Adjust → Deduct holdco net debt + apply conglomerate discount
From the SOTP Gross Value, deduct: (1) Holdco-level net debt (debt at the parent company, separate from subsidiary-level debt already in EV calculations). (2) Conglomerate discount — 5–10% for clean holdcos, 15–25% for complex conglomerates, 25–40% for opaque structures. The result is SOTP Net Equity Value. Divide by total shares outstanding = SOTP per-share value. Compare this to current market price — if trading at a significant discount (15%+), investigate why.
SOTP Equity= SOTP Gross − Holdco Net Debt − Conglomerate Discount %
5
Sanity check → Per-share SOTP vs current price → discount/premium %
Calculate: SOTP Discount % = (SOTP per share − Current Price) ÷ SOTP per share × 100. If stock trades at 20% below SOTP → conglomerate discount is within historical range (acceptable). If stock trades at 40% below SOTP → either your SOTP is too optimistic OR there is genuine hidden value. Cross-check by looking at whether the discount has been widening or narrowing over time — a narrowing discount suggests market is beginning to value segments properly (often driven by demerger news or investor day presentations).
✅ Discount 10–25% = typical conglomerate discount. No obvious mispricing — stock may close this discount gradually over time. Monitor for demerger news as catalyst.
🚨 Discount below 5% = trading near SOTP, already fully priced in. Or discount above 35% = either your SOTP is too aggressive or deep structural issues. Re-examine each segment's multiple.
⚡ 4 Advanced SOTP Tricks
A
The "Demerger Catalyst" Trade
When a conglomerate announces a demerger or separate listing of a subsidiary, the conglomerate discount on that segment immediately narrows toward zero for the demerging unit. Watch for demerger announcements: ITC Hotels demerger, Reliance's listing plans, Tata Sons IPO discussions. Buy the parent before the demerger is announced; sell after the listing as the discount closes. The per-share SOTP value doesn't change — but the market price moves toward it as the discount narrows.
B
The "Sensitivity Table" Rule
Always present SOTP with a 2×2 sensitivity table: rows = two key segment multiples at ±10%, columns = conglomerate discount at ±5%. This shows the per-share SOTP range. Example: Reliance SOTP ranges from ₹2,200 to ₹3,400 across the sensitivity table — that's the honest range of uncertainty. Presenting a single-point SOTP of ₹2,800 as "the" value ignores the inherent uncertainty in every multiple assumption.
C
The "Cigarette Test" for ITC
A quick ITC SOTP sanity check: ITC's cigarette business alone generates ~₹21,000 Cr EBIT. At 22× EBIT (reasonable for a near-monopoly consumer staple): cigarettes = ₹4.62L Cr. ITC's total market cap is ~₹5.5L Cr. This means the entire FMCG portfolio (₹12,000 Cr revenue, high-growth), Hotels (₹2,000+ Cr EBIT), Agri, and Paperboards are collectively valued at just ₹0.9L Cr — probably a massive undervaluation of these segments. This "cigarette test" immediately flags whether ITC is cheap or not.
D
Tracking SOTP Discount Over Time
Build a simple tracker: every quarter, recalculate SOTP using latest segment EBITDA from quarterly results and current peer multiples. Plot SOTP vs market price over time. When the gap widens beyond historical average → contrarian buy signal (subject to checking why discount widened). When gap narrows to historical minimum → take profits. For Reliance, this discount has ranged from 5% (Jan 2020) to 30% (Mar 2023). This dynamic tracking approach makes SOTP a living investment tool, not just a one-time calculation.

07 — Reference Glossary

SOTP & Conglomerate Glossary

Term What It Means Key Point
SOTP ValuationSum-of-the-Parts: valuing each business segment of a diversified company separately, using the most appropriate valuation method for each, then adding the individual values to arrive at total company value. The opposite of using a single P/E or EV/EBITDA for the whole group. Most appropriate for conglomerates, holding companies, and diversified industrial groups.Core method
Conglomerate DiscountThe percentage by which a conglomerate's market cap typically trades below its SOTP value. Ranges from 5–10% for clean holding companies to 25–40% for complex, opaque groups. Caused by investor preference for pure-play exposure, capital allocation risk, opacity, and structural tax drags. Narrows when demergers or subsidiary listings are announced.15–25% typical
Ind AS 108 Segment NoteMandatory accounting disclosure under Indian Accounting Standard 108 (Operating Segments). Every listed Indian company must disclose revenue, EBIT, and total assets for each "reportable segment" (any segment exceeding 10% of total revenue or assets). This note in the Annual Report is the primary data source for SOTP — without it, segment-level SOTP is estimation, not analysis.Primary data
Embedded Value (EV)An actuarial valuation method specific to life insurance companies. EV = Adjusted Net Worth + Present Value of Future Profits from in-force policies. The standard method for valuing insurance subsidiaries within SOTP. Indian life insurers (LIC, HDFC Life, SBI Life) publish their EV annually. EV/EV multiples (market cap ÷ EV) are the standard comparison for life insurers — typically 1.5–4× for growth insurers.Insurance
NAV (Net Asset Value)For real estate development companies within a conglomerate: NAV = market value of all land parcels + inventory − debt. The primary valuation method for real estate. Applying P/E or EV/EBITDA to real estate companies severely misstates value because real estate income can be lumpy and land bank value doesn't show up in EBITDA. NAV gives the per-share "break-up value" of the real estate business.Real estate
Holdco DiscountThe additional discount applied to a holding company (parent) that owns stakes in listed subsidiaries. Even after applying the conglomerate discount to SOTP, pure holding companies (that own stakes but don't operate businesses themselves) trade at an extra 20–40% below the market value of their stakes — because investors can't extract the value easily (taxes on dividends, lack of liquidity, governance concerns).Unlisted holdco
Demerger CatalystA corporate event where a conglomerate separates one or more subsidiaries into independently listed entities. The demerger causes the conglomerate discount on the demerging subsidiary to close toward zero — because investors can now buy that specific business directly. Historical examples: ITC Hotels demerger announcement (2024), Reliance Jio Financial Services listing (2023). Buying conglomerates ahead of demerger announcements is a well-known investment strategy.Unlocking event
Pure-Play ComparableA listed company that operates in exactly one business segment — used as the benchmark multiple for that segment in SOTP. ITC FMCG segment → Britannia, HUL as pure-play comparables. ITC Hotels → Indian Hotels (Taj). The pure-play's P/E or EV/EBITDA is the "undiscounted" price for that business, which is then applied to the conglomerate's segment earnings to derive segment value.Segment anchor
Inter-Segment EliminationTransactions between different segments of the same company that are included in both segments' revenues but must be cancelled out in the consolidated accounts. Example: if Reliance's O2C sells petroleum products to Reliance Retail at an arm's length price, this revenue appears in O2C segment and as cost in Retail — in SOTP, it must not be double-counted. Always check the Annual Report's "Inter-Segment Revenue" line and ensure it's excluded from both segment valuations.Avoid duplication
SOTP Sensitivity TableA 2×2 or 3×3 table showing how SOTP per-share value changes as key inputs vary. Rows: different multiple assumptions for the most uncertain segment (e.g., New Energy EBITDA multiple from 10× to 20×). Columns: conglomerate discount from 10% to 25%. Each cell shows the resulting per-share value. Standard in professional equity research — displays the honest range of uncertainty rather than a false single-point precision.Range analysis
