01 — Key Learning Outcome

What is Forensic Accounting and Why Does it Matter?

🔬
Your Core Takeaway
Forensic accounting is the art of reading between the lines of financial statements — using quantitative models and qualitative red flags to detect when management is manipulating reported earnings through accounting tricks rather than genuine business improvement.
Imagine two delivery men arriving at your door. Both carry packages labelled "₹1,000 worth of goods." But one box actually contains ₹1,000 of goods, and the other contains ₹200 of goods padded with newspaper to look full. From the outside — the label — they look identical. Forensic accounting teaches you how to open the box and weigh its actual contents instead of trusting the label.

In Indian capital markets, forensic analysis has identified problems at companies like Satyam Computer (₹7,800 Cr fraud), Manpasand Beverages (inflated revenue), IL&FS (hidden leverage), and dozens of smaller companies that reported growing profits while cash flows deteriorated. The Beneish M-Score — developed by Professor Messod Beneish at Indiana University in 1999 — is a statistical model using 8 financial ratios that predicts whether a company is manipulating its earnings. It correctly identified Enron, WorldCom, and multiple Indian fraud cases as high-risk years before their collapses became public.

This chapter teaches you to calculate the M-Score from data freely available on Screener.in, identify the six most dangerous red flags in Indian company financials, and apply this toolkit to 10 real companies — 5 that pass the forensic test and 5 that failed or show serious warning signs.

The 8 Ratios — What Each Detects
DSRI
Days Sales in Receivables Index
Danger if > 1.31
Compares receivables growth to revenue growth. If receivables grow much faster than revenue, the company is booking sales before collecting cash — classic revenue inflation signal. Screener: Receivables ÷ Revenue, current year vs prior year.
GMI
Gross Margin Index
Danger if > 1.0
Measures whether gross margin is deteriorating. A company with falling gross margins is under pressure — and is more likely to engage in manipulation to maintain earnings. Prior year GM ÷ Current year GM.
AQI
Asset Quality Index
Danger if > 1.0
Detects off-balance-sheet capitalisation. If non-current assets (excluding PP&E) grow as percentage of total assets, the company may be capitalising operating expenses — improving current earnings at the cost of future write-offs.
SGI
Sales Growth Index
Context needed
Measures revenue growth rate. High growth is not manipulation — but high-growth companies face pressure to maintain momentum and are statistically more likely to engage in accounting tricks. Current revenue ÷ Prior revenue.
DEPI
Depreciation Index
Danger if > 1.0
If depreciation rate slows (assets are being depreciated more slowly), net income looks higher but asset quality is declining. Prior year dep rate ÷ Current year dep rate. A rising DEPI suggests management is reducing depreciation to boost profits.
SGAI
SG&A Expense Index
Danger if > 1.0
Selling, General & Administrative costs rising faster than revenue. Disproportionate SGA growth signals operational inefficiency that management may be motivated to hide through revenue manipulation. Current SGA/Revenue ÷ Prior SGA/Revenue.
LVGI
Leverage Index
Danger if > 1.0
Rising total debt relative to total assets. Increasing leverage creates incentive to maintain earnings — highly leveraged companies that miss covenants risk immediate debt recall, creating intense pressure to manipulate reported numbers.
TATA
Total Accruals to Total Assets
Danger if > 0.031
The most powerful single ratio. (Net Income − Operating Cash Flow) ÷ Total Assets. High accruals mean profit is driven by accounting entries, not cash. This is the CFO/PAT problem: if PAT rises but CFO doesn't, TATA flags it immediately. The "Earnings Quality" ratio.
6 Creative Accounting Red Flags to Check on Screener.in
🧾
Receivables Growing Faster than Revenue
🚨 Revenue Inflation
When trade receivables consistently grow 30–50% faster than revenue, the company is booking sales before cash is collected. Eventually, these become bad debts — crashing both PAT and cash position simultaneously. On Screener.in: check 5-year trend of "Debtors Days" rising.
⚠️ Manpasand Beverages: Receivables grew 4× faster than revenue for 3 years before the fraud was exposed (2019).
💸
PAT Rising While CFO Stagnates or Falls
🚨 Accrual Manipulation
The single most reliable red flag: Net Profit growing 20% per year while Operating Cash Flow grows 0% or turns negative. On Screener.in Cash Flow tab: Calculate CFO/PAT for 5 years. Below 0.6× for 2+ consecutive years is a major warning. Satyam's CFO was systematically negative while PAT was positive.
⚠️ HFCL: CFO negative for 4 of 5 years while PAT positive every year — exact pattern of accrual manipulation regardless of cause.
🏗️
Unusual CapEx Spikes with No Revenue Follow-Through
⚠️ Expense Capitalisation
If a company suddenly increases CapEx 200–300% but revenue doesn't grow proportionally 2–3 years later, expenses may have been capitalised (moved from P&L to Balance Sheet). This inflates current PAT by reducing the expense charge this year, while the asset sits on books never generating returns.
⚠️ WorldCom (global): Capitalised $3.8 Bn in operating costs as CapEx over 18 months, inflating PAT before collapse.
📦
Inventory Growing Faster than Revenue (COGS)
🚨 Inventory Overstatement
If inventory grows significantly faster than revenue over multiple years, the company may be overstating inventory values to inflate assets and hide COGS write-offs. On Screener.in: track "Inventory Turnover" — a falling ratio while margins stay stable is a contradiction requiring explanation.
⚠️ Satyam: Bank balances overstated by ₹7,800 Cr. Cash on books that didn't exist — extreme version of asset overstatement principle.
🔄
Related Party Transactions Increasing Rapidly
⚠️ Siphoning Risk
Related party purchases/sales growing faster than business growth can indicate siphoning — selling at inflated prices to subsidiaries (revenue inflation) or buying from subsidiaries at above-market rates (expense siphoning). Check Annual Report Note 32: Related Party Transactions. Any RPT above 10% of revenue needs explanation.
⚠️ IL&FS: Complex web of 347 subsidiaries used for inter-company transactions that obscured total debt and actual financial position.
📊
Frequent Auditor Changes or Qualified Opinions
🔍 Governance Red Flag
Changing auditors every 2–3 years, or receiving a qualified/emphasis-of-matter in the auditor's report, is a serious governance warning. On Screener.in: check "Peer Comparison → Auditor" and read the Annual Report's auditor section. An "Emphasis of Matter" note often precedes a restatement or fraud disclosure by 1–2 years.
⚠️ Satyam's auditor PricewaterhouseCoopers India later faced regulatory action for signing off on falsified statements for years.

The Master Forensic Rule
The M-Score Is a Smoke Detector, Not a Fire Station
The Beneish M-Score does not prove fraud — it flags elevated risk of earnings manipulation that requires deeper investigation. An M-Score above −2.22 means: "Stop and ask hard questions." It does not mean: "This company is definitely committing fraud."

False positives exist: a company with a high M-Score in one year due to a major acquisition (which inflates AQI and DSRI temporarily) is not necessarily manipulating earnings. The power of the M-Score comes from trends — watch the score across 3–5 years. A score that migrates from −3.0 to −2.5 to −1.8 over three years (even if it never crosses −2.22) tells a deteriorating earnings quality story that the single-year number misses.

The complete forensic workflow: (1) Calculate M-Score from Screener.in data for 5 years. (2) Identify which of the 8 ratios is deteriorating. (3) Go to the Annual Report to explain the specific ratio. (4) Check the Related Party Transactions Note for siphoning risk. (5) Check auditor opinion — any qualification or change. (6) Check BSE/NSE filings for insider selling during the high-M-Score period. If you cannot explain why a high M-Score ratio is NOT manipulation — reduce position or exit. Unexplained forensic signals are the most dangerous signals of all.
