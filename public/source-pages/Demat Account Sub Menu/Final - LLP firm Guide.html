📖 Definition

Understanding a Limited Liability Partnership

An LLP is a modern hybrid business structure in India, governed by the LLP Act, 2008. It combines the flexibility of a partnership with the limited-liability protection of a company — making it ideal for professional and investment firms.

Partners → LLP → Demat Account
Two or more partners pool their capital into the LLP. The LLP becomes a separate legal entity with its own PAN — it can own property, sign contracts and hold a demat account independently of its partners.
⚖️
Legal Basis
  • Governed by the LLP Act, 2008.
  • A body corporate with a separate legal identity from its partners.
  • Combines partnership flexibility with corporate limited liability.
LLP Act 2008
👥
Partners
  • Minimum 2 Designated Partners required (at least one resident Indian).
  • No upper limit on the number of partners.
  • Partners can be individuals or body corporates.
Min 2 · No Limit

LLP Agreement
  • A written LLP Agreement defines partner roles and duties.
  • Sets out profit-sharing ratios and capital contributions.
  • Filed with MCA on Form 3.
Filed with MCA

Limited Liability
  • All partners' personal assets are protected.
  • Liability is limited to the agreed capital contribution.
  • No partner is liable for another partner's wrongful acts.
Protected Assets
🏦
Separate Legal Entity
  • An LLP can own property and enter contracts in its own name.
  • It can sue and be sued independently.
  • It has its own PAN card and files its own Income Tax Return.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about forming an LLP, opening a demat account, taxation, F&O treatment, compliance and Zerodha — answered with FY 2026–27 rules.

1What is the difference between an LLP and a general Partnership Firm?▾
📖 Definition

An LLP and a general Partnership Firm are both taxed at a flat 30%, but they differ in liability. In an LLP, all Designated Partners have limited liability — personal assets are protected. In a general partnership, partners face unlimited personal liability.

2008LLP Act Year
OfflineAccount Opening
7–10 DaysActivation Time
Mandatory Registration: An LLP must be incorporated with the MCA — it cannot exist informally. A general partnership can operate on a deed alone.
Separate Legal Entity: An LLP is a body corporate with its own PAN, can own property and sign contracts in its own name.
Zerodha Requirement: Zerodha needs the Certificate of Incorporation, LLP Agreement, all partners' PAN/Aadhaar and an LLP current account to open the account.
📌 Tip: Keep the Certificate of Incorporation and LLP Agreement ready before applying — they are mandatory and the most common cause of delays.
2How is F&O income taxed in an LLP?▾
🧾 Taxation

F&O (Futures & Options) income in an LLP is classified as non-speculative business income and taxed at the firm's flat rate of 30% — with full expense deduction and an 8-year loss carry-forward.

30%Flat Tax Rate
8 YrsLoss Carry Forward

Non-Speculative Business: F&O can be set off against other business income of the firm in the same year — a key advantage over individual traders.
Expense Deductions: Internet bills, data feeds, advisory subscriptions and brokerage charges are all deductible before the 30% rate applies.
Audit Threshold: If F&O turnover (absolute profit + absolute loss) exceeds the prescribed limit, a tax audit under Section 44AB is mandatory.

3Can partners also keep their own individual demat accounts alongside the LLP's account?▾
👥 Accounts

Yes — partners can maintain personal demat accounts for individual investments while the LLP also has its own separate demat account. There is no restriction or conflict.

Completely Independent: The firm's demat operates under the LLP's PAN; each partner's personal demat operates under their individual PAN. Profits, losses and tax obligations stay separate.
No Limit on Accounts: SEBI permits any person or firm to hold multiple demat accounts with different depositories or brokers.
Don't Mix Transactions: Firm trades must go through the firm's account and personal trades through individual accounts — keep audit trails clean.
💚 Best practice: Partners in higher individual tax slabs often route F&O and long-term equity through the firm while keeping personal accounts for personal goals.

💰 Capital

There is no minimum capital required to open the demat account. Even ₹1,000 is sufficient to start equity investing — though F&O activation requires margin deposits.

5Is a tax audit mandatory for an LLP trading in F&O?▾
🔍 Audit

A tax audit under Section 44AB becomes mandatory for an LLP if F&O trading turnover — calculated as absolute profit + absolute loss — exceeds the prescribed threshold in a financial year.

Turnover Calculation: F&O turnover = the sum of absolute values of all trade profits and losses. It is NOT the notional contract value. Zerodha Console's Tax P&L report provides this figure directly.
Audit Report: A Chartered Accountant prepares and files the audit report in Form 3CB/3CD before the ITR-5 due date.
Due Date Shifts: If an audit applies, the ITR-5 deadline moves from July 31 to September 30.
📋 Always confirm the current-year audit threshold with your CA, as the limits are revised periodically.
6Is the profit share received by partners taxed again in their hands?▾
🎉 Exemption

No. A partner's share of profit from the LLP is fully exempt in their individual hands under Section 10(2A) of the Income Tax Act — there is no double taxation on distributed profit.

Profit Share — Exempt: Since the firm has already paid 30% tax on its net profit, the partner's share is not taxed again.
Salary & Interest — Taxable: A working partner's salary and interest on capital are taxable in the partner's hands at slab rates (these are deductible for the firm).
💚 This pass-through treatment is a core reason high-income investors prefer the LLP structure over a private company, where distributed dividends are taxable.
7How long does it take to open an LLP demat account with Zerodha?▾

8Is GST applicable on an LLP's stock market trading?▾
📊 GST

GST is not applicable on the trading of securities themselves — buying and selling shares, F&O and bonds does not attract GST. However, the broker's charges (brokerage, transaction fees) do attract 18% GST.

Securities Are Exempt: Shares, derivatives and bonds are outside the scope of GST as goods or services.
Registration Trigger: If the LLP also provides other services and that turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh, GST registration becomes mandatory for those services.
