Health Insurance Buying Guide 2026–27

Everything you need to choose the right plan with confidence

Cover Amount
📏
How Much Cover Do You Need in 2026–27?
Metro minimum is now ₹25L following rapid medical inflation (14–16% annually). For Tier-2 cities, ₹15L is adequate. Senior citizens (60+) should target ₹30–50L, ideally a ₹20L base policy combined with a ₹25L super top-up to cover ICU, cardiac and oncology costs without paying for an expensive standalone large policy.

₹50LSenior Target
IRDAI 2025 Update
📋
New IRDAI Health Insurance Regulations 2025
Landmark regulations effective April 2025 mandate: PED waiting period capped at 3 years (down from 4), moratorium period reduced to 5 years, mental illness coverage made mandatory in all comprehensive plans, day-care procedures expanded to 1,200+, and no room rent sub-limits in new standard policies. Review your existing policy against these new norms.

Tax Benefit FY 2026-27
🧾
Section 80D Deductions — FY 2026–27
Under Old Tax Regime: ₹25,000 deduction for self/spouse/children's premium. Additional ₹25,000 for parents' premium — or ₹50,000 if parents are senior citizens aged 60+. Maximum combined deduction is ₹75,000/year. This benefit is NOT available in the New Tax Regime, so factor it into your regime comparison at ITR filing time.

NCB — 2025 Update
🎁
No-Claim Bonus — Now Protected Under IRDAI 2025
IRDAI 2025 regulations now protect NCB — insurers cannot reduce accumulated bonus to zero after a single small claim. NCB can also be applied as a premium discount (not just SI increase), giving policyholders more flexibility. After 5 claim-free years, a ₹20L policy grows to ₹30L cover for free. Never claim amounts under ₹15,000 to preserve this.

Individual vs Family Floater — 2026 Decision Guide
For young, healthy families (eldest below 50, no PEDs), floater remains best value — 25–35% cheaper than combined individual covers. However, with premiums rising 12–18% annually, consider a ₹20L floater + ₹25L super top-up rather than a single large base policy. IRDAI 2025 allows adding family members mid-term without fresh underwriting up to 60% of base SI.

Base + Super Top-Up: The Best-Value Formula 2026
With standalone ₹50L+ premiums now costing ₹40,000–70,000/year, the cost-optimal 2026 strategy is: ₹15L base policy (₹14,000–18,000/yr for age 35) plus ₹50L super top-up with ₹15L deductible (₹4,000–6,000/yr). Effective cover is ₹65L at just ₹20,000–24,000/year — a 65% saving vs a standalone ₹65L policy.

Pro Tips — Maximise Your Health Cover in 2026

Expert strategies to save money and increase protection

01
Buy Before Age 30 — Lock in Your Premium for Life
Buying at 25 vs 45 saves 60–70% on annual premium — and this gap widens every year as medical inflation (14–16% in 2025–26) pushes rates higher. A ₹20L policy at age 25 costs ~₹9,500/yr. At age 45, the same cover costs ₹26,000–32,000/yr. Buying early also means no PED declarations for conditions that typically develop in your 30s and 40s.
💡 Buy Early Strategy
02
Base + Super Top-Up: The 2026 Value Formula
Standalone ₹50L+ policies now cost ₹40,000–70,000/year post GST hikes. Instead: get a ₹15L base + ₹50L super top-up (deductible ₹15L) for just ₹20,000–24,000/year. Same emergency protection, 60–65% cheaper. Best super top-up plans in 2026: HDFC ERGO Optima Super, Niva Bupa ReAssure, Star Health Super Surplus.
💰 Premium Saver
03
Never Claim Below ₹15,000 — Protect Your NCB
Under IRDAI 2025 rules, NCB is now partially protected — but small claims still significantly reduce accumulated bonus. For bills under ₹15,000, pay out of pocket. A 5-year accumulated NCB gives ₹10L free extra cover on a ₹20L policy. That's worth far more than any small claim you could make. Set this threshold and stick to it every single year.
🎁 NCB Protection
04
Restore Benefit — Now Mandatory Under IRDAI 2025
IRDAI 2025 regulations mandate Unlimited Restore as a standard feature in comprehensive health plans from April 2025. Verify your existing policy includes it — if not, switch at renewal. Restore is critical for families: once one member exhausts the SI for a major surgery, the full sum insured is reinstated for any other member's hospitalisation in the same year.
🔄 IRDAI Mandate
05
Port Before April 2026 Renewal — 60-Day Window
Premium hikes of 15–25% are expected across insurers in 2026 renewals due to elevated claims and medical inflation. This is the best time to compare and switch. Under IRDAI 2025, the porting window is now 60 days before renewal (increased from 45). Port to retain all NCB, waiting period credits, and continuity benefits. Top 2026 performers: Niva Bupa (97.4% CSR), HDFC ERGO (92.1% CSR).
🔀 Port Now
06
Add OPD Cover + Mental Health Rider in 2026
IRDAI 2025 mandates mental illness coverage in all standard health policies from April 2025 — at no extra cost. Additionally, OPD riders now cover consultations, diagnostics and pharmacy costs (₹15,000–50,000/year limit). With preventive care and mental health costs rising 20%/year in India, these riders provide significant real-world value at just ₹2,000–5,000 extra annual premium.
🧠 New in 2025
